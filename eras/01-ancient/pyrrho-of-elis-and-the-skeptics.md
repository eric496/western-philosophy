# Pyrrho of Elis & the Skeptics

*Era: Ancient Philosophy (c. 600 BCE – c. 270 CE) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

Distinct from skeptical phase of Plato's Academy (Arcesilaus, Carneades).
- Major: Suspend judgement (epochē) about everything non-evident. Equally strong arguments on both sides (isostheneia) → neither affirm nor deny → ataraxia follows "like a shadow". Skeptic does NOT claim "nothing can be known" (that would be dogma); lives by appearances (nature, hunger, custom, arts) without assenting to theories. Tools: Aenesidemus' Ten Modes, Agrippa's Five Modes (regress, circularity, hypothesis, relativity, dispute).
- Key works: Pyrrho wrote nothing. Essential surviving text: Sextus Empiricus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism (late 2nd c. CE).
- Lineage: Travelled with Alexander to India (Indian influence debated); reacts against dogmatic systems; via Sextus' Renaissance rediscovery ignites Montaigne, Descartes' doubt, Hume.
- Why: Turns doubt into philosophy and therapy; poses criterion problem — by what standard do you justify your standard?
- Read first: Sextus, Outlines Book I. Caution: cliff-walking anecdotes are hostile legend; ancient skeptic ≠ "believes nothing".
