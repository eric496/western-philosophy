# Zeno of Citium & the Stoics

*Era: Ancient Philosophy (c. 600 BCE – c. 270 CE) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

Includes successors Cleanthes, Chrysippus (c. 279–206 BCE, systematizer; no complete early Stoic work survives); Roman Stoics Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius.
- Major: System in three parts — logic, physics, ethics. Universe is rational, providential, material whole run by Logos (God/Nature/Fate). Only true good is virtue (living rationally, in agreement with nature); health, wealth, reputation, life itself are "indifferents" (some preferred, none make you happy/miserable). Disturbing emotions (pathē) rest on false judgements, correctable by reason. Cosmopolitanism: citizen of cosmos, duties to all humanity. Fate + responsibility via character (early compatibilism).
- Key works: Early Stoa fragments only; Zeno's Republic lost. Seneca (c. 4 BCE–65 CE): Letters to Lucilius (124), On the Shortness of Life, On Anger. Epictetus (c. 50/55–135 CE, former slave): Discourses and Enchiridion (Handbook), recorded by Arrian — "Some things are up to us, some are not." Marcus Aurelius (121–180 CE, emperor 161–180): Meditations, private notebook never meant for publication.
- Lineage: Zeno studied with Cynic Crates and in the Academy — Cynic ethics + Heraclitean physics + logic. Rival: Epicureanism. Influence: Cicero, Christian ethics (uneasily), Spinoza, Kant (duty), modern CBT (borrows judgement-theory of emotion).
- Why: Most practically durable ancient philosophy.
- Read first: Enchiridion → Meditations II–V → Seneca, On the Shortness of Life and selected Letters. Zeno/Chrysippus via secondary (Sellars, Stoicism). Cautions: Stoics aim to replace false-judgement emotions with good ones, not suppress emotion; historical Stoicism stands on its physics/determinism, not just resilience tips.
