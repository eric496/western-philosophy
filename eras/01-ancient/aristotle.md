# Aristotle

*Era: Ancient Philosophy (c. 600 BCE – c. 270 CE) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Form exists in things (hylomorphism: substance = matter + form). Four causes: material, formal, efficient, final (telos). Change = potentiality becoming actuality, chain ending in Unmoved Mover. Invents formal logic (syllogism, Organon). Ethics: human good is eudaimonia — flourishing over a whole life via activity in accordance with virtue; moral virtue is trained mean between excess and deficiency, guided by practical wisdom (phronēsis).
- Key works (treatises/lecture texts, mainly 335–322 BCE): Organon, Physics, Metaphysics, On the Soul, Nicomachean Ethics, Politics, Poetics (tragedy, catharsis), Rhetoric.
- Lineage: Relentless respectful disagreement with Plato; tutor to Alexander; dominates late-antique commentary, transmitted/transformed by Arabic philosophers and Aquinas; foundational for virtue ethics, logic, philosophy of science.
- Why: The great systematizer — defines most sub-fields and basic vocabulary.
- Read first: Nicomachean Ethics I–II (and X). Metaphysics/Physics as guided reference. Caution: his defences of natural slavery and inferiority of women are rightly rejected today; his physics/biology often wrong as fact despite brilliant method. Mean is relative to person/situation — some acts have no mean; eudaimonia is activity over a lifetime, not feeling happy.
