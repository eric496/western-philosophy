# Epicurus

*Era: Ancient Philosophy (c. 600 BCE – c. 270 CE) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Therapy for anxiety. Physics: Democritus' atoms and void plus random atomic swerve (clinamen) breaking strict determinism. Ethics: goal is pleasure correctly understood — aponia (no bodily pain) + ataraxia (no mental disturbance); prefer simple natural necessary desires; friendship supreme; avoid politics/luxury. Gods exist but do not intervene; death is "nothing to us". Later summary: tetrapharmakos (four-fold remedy).
- Key works: ~300 works mostly lost. Surviving via Diogenes Laertius Book X: Letter to Menoeceus (ethics — read this), Letter to Herodotus (physics), Letter to Pythocles, Principal Doctrines (40 maxims). Lucretius, On the Nature of Things (1st c. BCE) is great full exposition.
- Lineage: Builds on Democritus against Plato's Academy and popular religion; rival to Stoa; revived by Gassendi; influence on utilitarian and secular thought.
- Why: Ancient world's most fully secular, naturalistic answer to living without fear.
- Read first: Letter to Menoeceus (~10 pages), Principal Doctrines. Caution: "Epicurean" gourmet indulgence is almost opposite of his teaching (bread, water, friends, philosophy).
