# Diogenes of Sinope & the Cynics

*Era: Ancient Philosophy (c. 600 BCE – c. 270 CE) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

Founded in spirit by Antisthenes (pupil of Socrates); Diogenes is archetype. Name from kynikos, "dog-like".
- Major: Live according to nature, against convention. Virtue only good; requires autarkeia (self-sufficiency): need almost nothing, fortune can't hurt you. Taught by performance: lived in large storage jar (pithos, not barrel), told Alexander to stop blocking his sunlight. First recorded "citizen of the world" (kosmopolitēs). Shamelessness (anaideia) and blunt free speech (parrhēsia) expose hypocrisy.
- Key works: None survive. Known via anecdotes, chiefly Diogenes Laertius, Lives Book VI (3rd c. CE, ~600 years later) — teaching tales, not transcripts.
- Lineage: Socrates' indifference to wealth taken to extreme, against Plato ("a Socrates gone mad"); pupil-line Crates → Zeno, so Cynicism seeds Stoicism.
- Why: Philosophy as embodied public critique of civilization.
- Read first: Diogenes Laertius, Lives VI (Diogenes section). Caution: modern "cynical" (distrusting motives) is almost unrelated — ancient Cynic is idealist about virtue/nature.
