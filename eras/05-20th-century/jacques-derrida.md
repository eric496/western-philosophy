# Jacques Derrida

*Era: 20th Century to Present (1900 – ) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Deconstruction: close-reading text to show it depends on oppositions it can't keep stable (speech/writing, presence/absence, nature/culture), "lesser" term turns out needed by greater. Not destroying text, not "means anything". Différance: deliberate misspelling combining differ + defer — word means by differing from other words, meaning always deferred along chain; never fully present in one moment/sign/intention. Logocentrism / metaphysics of presence: Western habit privileging speech over writing, presence over absence as if speech gave direct access to present thought; writing's features (working in author's absence, difference, deferral) are conditions of ALL language.
- Works: Three books 1967: Of Grammatology, Writing and Difference, Speech and Phenomena; Margins of Philosophy (1972, incl. "Différance" essay 1968); Dissemination (1972); Specters of Marx (1993); "Force of Law" (1989–90).
- Lineage: Saussure structural linguistics, Husserl's self-present consciousness, Heidegger (adapts Destruktion, criticises), Plato, Rousseau, Hegel, Levinas; famous 1963 break with Foucault over Descartes/madness. Influenced literary theory (de Man, Yale School), Spivak, Butler, architecture, legal/postcolonial theory.
- Why: Made philosophy attend to language itself — binaries/certainties texts rest on are unstable, with consequences for law/politics/ethics not just literature.
- Read first: NOT Of Grammatology alone. "Différance" essay + "Letter to a Japanese Friend" (1983, what deconstruction is/isn't) + SEP Derrida; then opening Of Grammatology.
- Caution: "There is nothing outside the text" (il n'y a pas de hors-texte, 1967) better rendered "there is no outside-text" — nothing available outside context of interpretation; NOT denial of world. Deconstruction is painstaking close reading, opposite of free association.
