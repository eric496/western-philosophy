# Simone de Beauvoir

*Era: 20th Century to Present (1900 – ) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: "One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman": femininity made (upbringing, custom, work, marriage, myth), not biological destiny. Man cast as Subject (default human), woman as Other, confined to immanence (repetition, maintenance) while men claim transcendence (projects, creation). Ethics of ambiguity (1947) supplies moral theory Sartre promised, never delivered: life genuinely ambiguous (free AND situated), no absolute rules to hide behind; willing my freedom requires willing others' freedom; oppression is refusal of that.
- Works: She Came to Stay (1943 novel); Ethics of Ambiguity (1947); Second Sex (1949, 2 vols); Mandarins (1954, Prix Goncourt); Coming of Age (1970).
- Lineage: Hegel master–slave recast Subject/Other; Sartre partnership of MUTUAL influence, not discipleship; Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty, Marxism, biology/psychoanalysis used and criticised. Influenced second-wave feminism (Friedan's Feminine Mystique 1963 drew on her), Millett, Judith Butler (developed into sex/gender distinction).
- Why: Existential phenomenology applied to women's lived experience exposes "woman" as social construction; founded modern feminism.
- Read first: Ethics of Ambiguity (short, clear, arguably most accessible entry into French existentialism). Then Second Sex Introduction + opening Vol. II (famous sentence), in 2010 Borde & Malovany-Chevallier translation — 1953 English translation severely cut/distorted. Reception: scandal 1949 (Vatican Index of Forbidden Books); decades under-read as "Sartre's partner" — modern scholarship substantially reversed.
- Caution: Not Sartre's disciple applying his ideas.
