# Michel Foucault

*Era: 20th Century to Present (1900 – ) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Power/knowledge: what society counts as true (medicine, psychiatry, criminology, sexuality) is produced by and sustains institutions classifying, treating, punishing, normalising. Discipline and biopower: modern power works less by public spectacle than quiet surveillance/training (prison, school, barracks, hospital): Bentham's Panopticon — inmates who might always be watched monitor themselves. Extended to populations (health, birth rates, hygiene) = biopolitics. Methods: archaeology digs unspoken rules letting period's statements count as knowledge; genealogy (from Nietzsche) traces how rules emerged through contingent struggle — what looks natural ("mental illness", "the homosexual" as type of person) has history, could be otherwise.
- Works: History of Madness / Madness and Civilization (1961); Birth of the Clinic (1963); Order of Things (1966); Archaeology of Knowledge (1969); Discipline and Punish (1975); History of Sexuality Vol. 1 (1976); Vols. 2–3 (1984).
- Lineage: Against Sartre's free subject/"universal intellectual"; against orthodox Marxism, Freud's "repressive hypothesis". Shaped by Nietzsche, Canguilhem, Bachelard, Heidegger. Influenced Butler/queer theory, Agamben (biopolitics), postcolonial, feminist, prison-abolition, surveillance, medical, education studies.
- Why: Changed picture of power: not just rulers hold/repress, but productive, spread through everyday knowledge/institutions, shaping who we take ourselves to be.
- Read first: NOT Order of Things. Opening of Discipline and Punish (public execution vs prison timetable) + History of Sexuality Vol. 1 (short, readable); pair SEP Foucault.
- Caution: Not "everything is power, truth is fake, resistance pointless" — power productive and generates resistance; histories are detailed empirical claims open to checking, not licence to dismiss claims by chanting "power".
