# Jean-Paul Sartre

*Era: 20th Century to Present (1900 – ) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: "Existence precedes essence": no God-given/fixed human nature decides you in advance; you exist, act, thereby make yourself. Radical freedom — "condemned to be free": refusing to choose is a choice, responsibility total (and anguishing). Bad faith: self-deception denying freedom (waiter who plays at being waiter as if role were thing's fixed nature). In-itself (things, just what they are) vs for-itself (consciousness, negates/transcends what it is). "Hell is other people" (No Exit) is about being fixed under Look of the Other, not simple misanthropy.
- Works: Nausea (1938 novel); Being and Nothingness (1943); No Exit (1944 play); Existentialism Is a Humanism (1946, 1945 lecture); Critique of Dialectical Reason (1960).
- Lineage: Studied Husserl in Berlin (1933–34); Heidegger, Hegel, Descartes, Marx. He and Beauvoir shaped each other lifelong; co-founded Les Temps modernes (1945). Influenced Merleau-Ponty, early Camus, Fanon/postcolonial theory, existential psychotherapy.
- Why: Made existentialism public philosophy of post-war Europe, in novels/plays as much as treatises.
- Read first: Existentialism Is a Humanism (one sitting — note Sartre later treated popular lecture as oversimplified) + No Exit, then Nausea; Being and Nothingness selections (bad faith, freedom) only then. Politics: fellow-travelling support for French Communist Party (1952–56), defences of revolutionary violence, alongside condemnations of Soviet invasions Hungary 1956, Czechoslovakia 1968; refused Nobel Literature 1964.
- Caution: Freedom always situated (facticity: body, past, others), burden/anguish not licence; bad faith = pretending fully determined OR situation doesn't constrain.
