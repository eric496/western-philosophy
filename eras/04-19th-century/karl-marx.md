# Karl Marx

*Era: 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Historical materialism: organisation of production (forces of production + relations of production/who owns what) fundamentally shapes law, politics, dominant ideas (1859 Preface "base/superstructure"); societies rise developing productive power, fall fettering it; history driven centrally via class struggle. Alienation (Entfremdung, early manuscripts): worker alienated from product, activity of labour, species-being (free creative human capacities), and other people. Young vs mature Marx continuity/"epistemological break" (Althusser) is a debate, not settled. Surplus value: worker sells labour-power, produces more value than it costs → difference is profit; commodity fetishism (social relations appearing as relations between things); exploitation as structural, not individual greed. Praxis: Theses on Feuerbach (1845) Thesis XI — not only interpret world, change it.
- Works: Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844 (written 1844, published 1932); Theses on Feuerbach (1845); German Ideology (with Engels, 1845–46, unpublished then); Communist Manifesto (with Engels, 1848 — SEP: "not best guide to his thought" alone); Contribution to Critique of Political Economy (1859 Preface); Capital Vol. 1 (1867); Vols. 2–3 edited by Engels posthumously (1885, 1894).
- Lineage: Hegel (inverting idealist dialectic into materialist history/labour); Feuerbach (projection theory/alienation, then criticised as passive); classical political economy (Smith, Ricardo); French socialism. Influenced socialist/communist movements, Western Marxism (Lukács, Gramsci, Frankfurt School), sociology, labour/economic history, postcolonial and feminist theory, and opponents defined against him.
- Why: Theory of history + most influential systematic critique of capitalism; much modern social science downstream or in argument.
- Read first: Manifesto WITH 1859 Preface and 1844 alienation chapter. Capital later, ch. 1 with guide.
- Cautions: Historical materialism ≠ crude economic determinism (Engels qualified mechanical readings); Marx ≠ Manifesto only, ≠ simple endorsement of every 20th-c. regime in his name (reception history ≠ text); surplus value ≠ slogan "profit is theft"; alienation ≠ feeling unhappy at work.
