# William James

*Era: 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Pragmatic truth: idea's "cash-value" in experience; true ideas prove themselves in long run — lead satisfactorily, connect with other beliefs, verifiable in practice, constrained by reality/prior truths (not "comforting = true"; truth happens to an idea through verification). Will to believe: live, forced, momentous options evidence can't decide → may believe ahead of proof (paradigm religion); narrow class only. Psychology: stream of consciousness; James–Lange emotion theory (emotion ≈ perception of bodily changes). Varieties of Religious Experience (1902): religion via individual experience as data. Radical empiricism/pluralism: relations themselves experienced; universe plural and unfinished vs Hegelian Absolute.
- Works: Principles of Psychology (1890); Will to Believe and Other Essays (1897); Varieties of Religious Experience (1902, Gifford Lectures 1901–02); Pragmatism (1907); Meaning of Truth (1909); A Pluralistic Universe (1909).
- Lineage: Peirce (source, credited in 1898 Berkeley address); British empiricism; German laboratory psychology; against Hegelian absolute idealism (Royce). Influenced Dewey/progressive thought, philosophy of religion, psychology, modernist literature; Wittgenstein read him closely.
- Why: Made pragmatism public philosophy and psychology a discipline in America; most readable great philosopher in era.
- Read first: Pragmatism Lectures 2 and 6; then Varieties lectures on conversion/saintliness in any order.
