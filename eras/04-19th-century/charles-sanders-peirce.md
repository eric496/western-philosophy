# Charles Sanders Peirce

*Era: 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Pragmatic maxim: clarify concept by conceivable practical effects; conception of effects IS conception of object ("How to Make Our Ideas Clear", 1878) — dissolves empty verbal disputes. Inquiry/doubt/belief; fallibilism: belief = habit of action, genuine doubt spurs inquiry; "Fixation of Belief" (1877): scientific method superior to tenacity/authority/a priori because external reality settles belief; no belief immune to revision. Truth/reality = what indefinite community of inquirers would converge on in long run (regulative ideal, not majority opinion). Semeiotic: all thought in signs; sign stands for object to interpretant (triadic). Categories: Firstness (quality/possibility), Secondness (brute reaction/fact), Thirdness (mediation/law/habit). Also abduction alongside deduction/induction.
- Works: Mostly papers, no finished system in lifetime. "Fixation of Belief" (1877), "How to Make Our Ideas Clear" (1878) in Illustrations of the Logic of Science; Collected Papers (8 vols, posthumous, 1931–58).
- Lineage: Against Descartes (paper doubt, intuition); Kant's categories reworked; British empiricism; scholastic realism (Scotus). Pragmatism formed in Harvard Metaphysical Club (c. 1870s) with James. Influenced James (credited him 1898), Dewey, philosophy of science, logic, semiotics.
- Why: Founded America's first original philosophical movement in its most rigorous scientific form; advanced modern logic/sign theory.
- Read first: The two 1877–78 papers, before James, to see what James changed.
