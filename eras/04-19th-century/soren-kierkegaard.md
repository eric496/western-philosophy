# Søren Kierkegaard

*Era: 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

"Father of existentialism" is retrospective label SEP warns "obscures at least as much as it reveals"; he saw himself as Christian religious writer.
- Major: Three stages/spheres: aesthetic (pleasure, novelty; Seducer in Either/Or), ethical (commitment, duty, universal; Judge William), religious (absolute relation to God, may go beyond universal ethical). Not automatic age-stages — moving requires choice. "Leap"/faith: Fear and Trembling (pseudonym Johannes de Silentio): Abraham as knight of faith, "teleological suspension of the ethical"; popular "leap of faith" simplifies his leap/qualitative transition, not licence to believe blindly. Subjectivity/anxiety/despair: "truth is subjectivity" (Postscript) — for existential/religious questions, how belief is appropriated matters, not only objective certainty; Concept of Anxiety (1844): anxiety as dizziness of freedom; Sickness Unto Death (1849): despair as self's failure/refusal to be itself. Pseudonymity/indirect communication: invented authors with opposed views — pseudonym's view ≠ automatically Kierkegaard's.
- Works: Either/Or (1843); Fear and Trembling (1843); Philosophical Fragments (1844); Concept of Anxiety (1844); Concluding Unscientific Postscript (1846); Works of Love (1847, signed); The Sickness Unto Death (1849).
- Lineage: Against Hegel/Danish Hegelianism and complacent state "Christendom". Little read outside Denmark in life; 20th-c. influence on Heidegger, Jaspers, Sartre, Camus, Barth, existential theology/psychology.
- Why: Re-centred philosophy on the single existing individual — choice, anxiety, faith, selfhood.
- Read first: Fear and Trembling; selections Either/Or Part II; Postscript later.
