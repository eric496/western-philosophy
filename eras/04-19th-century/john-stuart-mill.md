# John Stuart Mill

*Era: 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

SEP: "most influential English-speaking philosopher of the 19th century". Bentham predecessor above.
- Major: Utilitarianism refined: right = promotes happiness of all affected, impartially counted; pleasures differ in quality as well as quantity ("better Socrates dissatisfied than fool satisfied"); act- vs rule-utilitarian reading is live dispute. Harm principle (On Liberty): power over individual against their will only to prevent harm to others; individual sovereign over own body/mind. Grounds free thought/discussion, individuality/experiments in living, limits on state and "tyranny of majority". Logic/empiricism: System of Logic (1843), inductivist philosophy of science, "Mill's Methods". The Subjection of Women (1869): subordination of women is relic of force, equality just and useful. Credited Harriet Taylor Mill as major intellectual partner/joint author of On Liberty (his account).
- Works: System of Logic (1843); Principles of Political Economy (1848); On Liberty (1859); Utilitarianism (serialised 1861; book 1863); Considerations on Representative Government (1861); Subjection of Women (1869); Autobiography (1873, posthumous).
- Lineage: Responded to Bentham/James Mill, and after mental crisis 1826–27 to narrowness of upbringing (Coleridge, Carlyle, Romantics). Influenced liberal tradition, Sidgwick, modern consequentialism, Popper/Berlin, free speech and feminist arguments.
- Why: Gave utilitarian ethics and political liberalism their most durable formulations.
- Read first: On Liberty ch. 1–3, then Utilitarianism ch. 2; then Subjection of Women ch. 1.
- Cautions: Utilitarianism ≠ egoism; harm ≠ offence; On Liberty excludes children and (contested passage) societies Mill deemed "barbarian"; don't present as unambiguously act- or rule-.
