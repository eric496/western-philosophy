# Friedrich Nietzsche

*Era: 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major, carefully: "God is dead" and nihilism (Gay Science §125, 1882, madman parable): cultural diagnosis — belief in Christian God became unbelievable, values built on it remain and collapse threatens nihilism; not triumphant slogan. Genealogy; master and slave morality (Genealogy of Morals, 1887): master morality (noble self-valuing "good", "bad" as afterthought) vs slave morality born of ressentiment (masters' traits revalued "evil", humility/patience/pity "good"). NOT Hegel's lordship/bondage. Also bad conscience, ascetic ideal. Will to power: life/world best interpreted as drives seeking growth/overcoming/self-overcoming; contested whether psychology, metaphysics or heuristic; NOT simply political domination/brute force. The Will to Power book is posthumous notebook selection assembled by others (incl. sister) — not a book he published. Eternal recurrence (Gay Science §341 "greatest weight", Zarathustra): life repeating exactly forever — primarily existential test of affirmation, literal cosmology debated. Übermensch (Zarathustra): figure who creates values after death of God vs comfortable "last man"; aspiration/regulative ideal, not biological master race. Perspectivism: knowing always from perspective shaped by drives — makes us more honest/multi-perspectival, not "anything goes". Early: Apollo/Dionysus in Birth of Tragedy (1872).
- Works: Birth of Tragedy (1872); Human, All Too Human (1878); Gay Science (1882; expanded 1887); Thus Spoke Zarathustra (1883–85); Beyond Good and Evil (1886); On the Genealogy of Morals (1887); Twilight of the Idols, Antichrist (pub. 1895), Ecce Homo (pub. 1908) — all written 1888.
- Lineage: Schopenhauer (early educator, transformed pessimism → affirmation), Wagner (ally → target), Socrates/Plato/Christianity as he understood them, Darwinian culture, German nationalism and anti-Semitism (both despised; broke with Wagner/sister partly over). Nazi appropriation via sister's editing/promotion treated by most scholars as falsification (his contempt for German nationalism, herd politics, anti-Semites). Legitimate influence: Heidegger, Sartre, Camus, Foucault, Deleuze, Derrida, modernist literature. Anti-egalitarianism and harsh rhetoric are really there — confront, don't edit out or whitewash.
- Why: Century's most radical critic of morality itself — where did morals come from, whom do they serve?
- Read first: Genealogy, First Essay; then Gay Science §§125, 276, 341. Zarathustra later (literary, parodic, easy to misread alone).
- Cautions: See above; perspectivism ≠ relativism; master/slave morality ≠ Hegel's dialectic.
