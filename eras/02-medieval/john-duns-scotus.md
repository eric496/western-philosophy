# John Duns Scotus

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Univocity of being: against Aquinas' analogy, terms like "good", "wise", "being" must apply to God and creatures in one same sense (univocally) — otherwise no certain concept of God, every theological argument equivocates. Arguably hinge between medieval and modern metaphysics. Haecceity and common nature: realist, fine-grained — "humanity" in Socrates/Plato is real common nature with unity "less than numerical"; what makes Socrates THIS one is individuating non-qualitative property, haecceity ("thisness", haec) vs quiddity ("whatness"). SEP: haecceity = non-qualitative property responsible for individuation and identity. Formal distinction: between common nature and haecceity, distinction neither real (separable things) nor merely mental — formal, obtaining in thing itself; signature tool, also applied to God's attributes/Trinity. Voluntarism: primacy of free will over intellect in God and us; morality grounded significantly in God's free willing. Modal proof of God as infinite being (infinity, not simplicity, is master divine attribute).
- Works: Ordinatio (revised Oxford lectures on Lombard's Sentences, c. 1300–07, unfinished); Lectura (lectures as delivered); Treatise on First Principle (De Primo Principio) (late c. 1307–08, most self-contained, proof of infinite first principle); Reportatio (student reports of Paris lectures).
- Lineage: Engaged Aquinas point-by-point, Henry of Ghent, Avicenna, Maimonides (attributes/prophecy). Founded Scotism, rival school to Thomism; univocity/modal metaphysics live in contemporary metaphysics; Deleuze and Heidegger claimed him.
- Why: Made medieval metaphysics maximally precise — univocity, modal thinking, dignity of individual "this" quietly dismantled Thomist synthesis from inside.
- Read first: NOT Ordinatio raw. Modern guide first (Richard Cross, Duns Scotus; Thomas Williams SEP + Cambridge Companion intro), then Treatise on First Principle (Wolter translation), short and self-contained.
- Caution: Not "Aquinas but more complicated" — disagreed on univocity vs analogy, will vs intellect, individuation; hence rival schools.
