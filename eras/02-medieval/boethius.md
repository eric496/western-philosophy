# Boethius

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: The transmitter: project to translate ALL Plato and Aristotle into Latin; completed Aristotle's logic (Organon) + commentaries → for ~600 years, until 12th-c. translations, almost only Aristotle Latin West had (logica vetus). Framed problem of universals: second commentary on Porphyry's Isagoge posed question, deliberately left open. Consolation: written in prison awaiting execution, dialogue with Lady Philosophy: worldly goods ruled by Fortune (Wheel); true happiness in unchanging Good (God). Foreknowledge solution: God doesn't fore-know in time; in eternity God sees all times at once, as watching a road doesn't force travellers — divine knowledge doesn't cause/necessitate free acts.
- Works: Consolation of Philosophy (c. 524, prison); translations/commentaries on Categories, On Interpretation, Porphyry's Isagoge (c. 510–522); theological tractates (Opuscula Sacra); arithmetic/music textbooks defining quadrivium.
- Lineage: Drew Plato, Aristotle, Cicero, Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus, Augustine. Influenced entire early medieval curriculum; Consolation among most copied medieval books (translated by Alfred the Great, Chaucer).
- Why: Without Boethius no Latin Aristotle, no universals debate, no standard vocabulary (substance, person, nature) for medieval theology.
- Read first: Consolation itself — short, literary (prose+verse), whole book (Penguin/Oxford).
- Caution: Consolation strikingly never mentions Christ/Scripture — Lady Philosophy consoles with (Neo)Platonic/Stoic reason; part of its puzzle.
