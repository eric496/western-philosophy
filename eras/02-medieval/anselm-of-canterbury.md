# Anselm of Canterbury

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Ontological argument (Proslogion chs. 2–3): God is "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"; even fool denying God has this in understanding; being existing in reality as well as understanding is greater — so existing only in mind yields contradiction, it must exist in reality. Ch. 3 adds modal twist: such being cannot even be conceived not to exist (necessary existence). Faith seeking understanding: argument offered inside prayer, by believer, reason unfolding faith — not missionary proof forced on unbelievers. Satisfaction theory of atonement (Cur Deus Homo): humanity's sin dishonours God infinitely; only God-man can make proportionate satisfaction — Incarnation rationally fitting/necessary, not arbitrary.
- Works: Monologion (1076); Proslogion (1077–78); Gaunilo's In Behalf of the Fool critique + Anselm's reply followed immediately; Cur Deus Homo (c. 1094–98).
- Lineage: Augustinian tradition, teacher Lanfranc; debated by Gaunilo in lifetime. Rejected/refined by Aquinas, revived Descartes/Leibniz, attacked by Kant ("existence is not a predicate" — SEP), revived modally Hartshorne/Malcolm/Plantinga.
- Why: Most discussed single argument in philosophy, in two pages; rigorous proof inside theology.
- Read first: Proslogion chs. 1–4 WITH Gaunilo's "perfect island" parody + Anselm's reply — exchange is real lesson, few pages (Davies & Evans Oxford, Major Works).
- Caution: Not cheap word-trick/defining God into existence — written as prayer; logic turns on necessary existence; serious opponents for 900 years.
