# Augustine of Hippo

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Original sin, grace, divided will: humanity inherits damaged will from Adam's Fall; reliably chooses lesser goods, cannot self-heal — only God's grace can; virtue = rightly ordered love (caritas). Two cities (City of God): history as intermingled story of earthly city (love of self to contempt of God) vs City of God (love of God to contempt of self); written after pagans blamed Christians for sack of Rome (410). Time/memory/evil: Confessions XI — past/future don't exist, present has no extent, time is "distension of mind" (distentio animi) held by memory/expectation; God eternal, outside time. Evil is privation (lack/corruption of good), not a thing God made — break with former Manichaeism.
- Works: Confessions (c. 397–400; Books X–XI memory/time are philosophical core); City of God (413–426, 22 books); On the Trinity (c. 399–419); On Free Choice of the Will (begun c. 388).
- Lineage: Against Manichaeism (former sect), Pelagius (grace); absorbed Christianised Plotinus/Neoplatonism, Cicero. Influenced virtually everyone after — Boethius, Anselm, Abelard, Aquinas (cites constantly), Scotus, later Luther/Calvin.
- Why: Set questions (sin, grace, will, time, history) argued about for 1,100 years.
- Read first: Confessions Books 1, 7, 8 (conversion), 10 (memory), 11 (time), modern translation (Chadwick). City of God selected (XI–XIV, XIX) abridged — not all 22 first.
- Caution: He systematised a distinctively Western Augustinian version of original sin (from Paul); Eastern church never adopted same form; Pelagius contested in his lifetime — not "invented unopposed".
