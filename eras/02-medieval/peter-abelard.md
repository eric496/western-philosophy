# Peter Abelard

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Conceptualism on universals: against teacher William of Champeaux's realism (same essence wholly in each individual → contradictions per Abelard) and Roscelin's universals as mere puff of voice (flatus vocis): universals are sermones — words as meaningful signs; general word signifies via common cause of imposition (real resemblances) and generates concept, but no general thing exists. "Conceptualism" label later and debated. Sic et Non method: 158 theological questions (Britannica), each with apparently contradictory Scripture/Fathers quotes pro and contra, prologue rules for resolving (context, wording, textual corruption, meaning change) — not scepticism, training manual for dialectic, founding document of scholastic quaestio. Intention ethics (Ethics/Scito te ipsum "Know Thyself"): sin lies not in outward act or desire but in consent — will's contempt for what it knows God requires; morality moves inward to intention/conscience.
- Works: Sic et Non (c. 1120–21); Logica Ingredientibus / Glosses on Porphyry (c. 1119–20, universals theory); Historia Calamitatum (c. 1132, autobiography incl. affair with Héloïse and castration by her uncle's agents); Ethica/Scito te ipsum (c. 1138–39); Theologia versions.
- Lineage: Refuted teachers Roscelin and William of Champeaux; opposed by Bernard of Clairvaux (engineered Sens condemnation). Influenced Peter Lombard (Sentences systematised Sic et Non approach), Paris schools.
- Why: Turned disagreement among authorities from embarrassment into method — scholasticism's engine; most sophisticated early universals position.
- Read first: Historia Calamitatum (short, gripping), then Sic et Non Prologue + 2–3 questions, then universals excerpts in anthology (Spade, Five Texts on Mediaeval Problem of Universals).
