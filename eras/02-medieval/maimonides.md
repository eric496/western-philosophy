# Maimonides (Moses ben Maimon / Rambam)

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Negative theology: can't say what God IS, only what God is NOT (not weak, not multiple, not body, not ignorant); positive attributes in same sense for God/creatures would compromise unity/simplicity, so scriptural descriptions ("God's hand", "God was angry") read figuratively. Guide is largely dictionary dissolving literal readings. Creation vs eternity — reason hits limit: against Aristotle/Averroes (eternal) and crude literalism, NEITHER eternity NOR temporal creation demonstrable by reason; accepts creation on scriptural grounds, partly because Aristotelian eternity would undermine miracles/law. Aquinas took over this "undemonstrable either way" verdict. Providence/evil/purpose of Law: providence tracks intellectual perfection, not membership; most evil from matter, others' actions, own choices; every commandment has rational purpose aimed at welfare of soul and body — law as moral/political education. Guide framed as letter to student "perplexed" between philosophy and religion; deliberately written with contradictions/esotericism scholars (Strauss famously) still debate.
- Works: Guide of the Perplexed (completed 1190; Hebrew translations immediately, Latin 13th c.); Mishneh Torah (c. 1170–80, 14-volume law code; first book Book of Knowledge has philosophical foundations; Thirteen Principles in earlier Commentary on Mishnah, c. 1168).
- Lineage: Built on al-Fārābī, Avicenna, Averroes, Aristotle; critiqued kalām. Latin scholastics as "Rabbi Moyses": Aquinas cites on creation, divine names, providence; Scotus engages attributes doctrine. Later: Spinoza his great critic-heir.
- Why: Most rigorous statement across three monotheisms of how little language can say about God; modelled being scientist-philosopher and lawgiver without splitting.
- Read first: SEP "Maimonides" first, then Guide Part I chs. 51–60 (attributes/negative theology), Pines translation with introductions. Sample with guide, not cover-to-cover cold.
