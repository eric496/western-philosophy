# Averroes (Ibn Rushd)

*Era: Medieval Philosophy (c. 400 – c. 1400) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Recovering "real" Aristotle: against Avicenna's Neoplatonised Aristotle, wrote short/middle/long (line-by-line) commentaries on nearly all Aristotle, stripping later accretions; Latin Europe learned Aristotle through his lens. Harmony of religion/philosophy (Decisive Treatise): as jurist argues Islamic law obliges qualified people to study philosophy; Scripture and demonstration can't truly conflict — where literal Scripture clashes with proven truth, Scripture interpreted allegorically (by philosophers, not preached to everyone). "Double truth" is NOT his position — hostile label pinned on Latin followers (Latin Averroists, Siger of Brabant). Unity of intellect: reading De Anima III, material (possible) intellect is one single, separate, eternal intellect for all humans; individuals contribute sense-images but don't each own immortal intellect → threatened personal immortality, provoked Aquinas' On the Unity of the Intellect (1270), figured in Paris condemnations 1270/1277.
- Works: Long Commentaries on Physics, Metaphysics, De Caelo, esp. De Anima (mostly 1170s–80s); Decisive Treatise (c. 1179–80); Incoherence of the Incoherence (c. 1180, defence of philosophy vs al-Ghazālī, defending real natural causation).
- Lineage: Corrected Avicenna, answered al-Ghazālī, explicated Aristotle. West: cited constantly by Aquinas (learned from and fought), Scotus, Latin Averroists; central to Jewish philosophy; many works survive only in Hebrew/Latin, Arabic lost.
- Why: Conduit AND provocation — Aristotle arriving in 1200s Paris wore Averroes' glasses; fights over him created Latin scholasticism's agenda.
- Read first: Decisive Treatise (short, lucid; Butterworth translation) — judge ruling philosophy legally required; then chunk of Long Commentary alongside Aristotle passage it glosses.
