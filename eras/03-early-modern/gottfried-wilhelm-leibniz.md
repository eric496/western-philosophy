# Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz

*Era: Early Modern Philosophy (c. 1600 – 1800) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Monads and pre-established harmony: reality ultimately infinitely many monads — simple, indivisible, mind-like, "windowless" (no causal interaction), each mirrors entire universe from its point of view; mind/body and all monads appear coordinated because God set pre-established harmony (perfectly synchronised clocks). Two principles: principle of contradiction (necessary truths) and principle of sufficient reason (nothing true/exists without sufficient reason why so and not otherwise) → God as sufficient reason for contingent world. Theodicy/"best of all possible worlds": God chose best compossible world among infinite possible worlds; evil (metaphysical finitude, physical suffering, moral sin) permitted as inseparable from best whole. Identity of indiscernibles; innate ideas as dispositions (mind = veined marble, not blank slate).
- Works: Discourse on Metaphysics (1686, unpublished in lifetime); Theodicy (1710, only full-length book published in lifetime); Monadology (written 1714, ~90 paragraphs, posthumous); New Essays on Human Understanding (written 1704, reply to Locke, published 1765 — Locke never saw it); Principles of Nature and Grace (1714).
- Lineage: Dialogue with Descartes, Spinoza (visited 1676; pluralised his monism), Locke, Newton (Leibniz–Clarke correspondence 1715–16 on space/time), Arnauld. Recovered later: modern logic, possible-worlds semantics, analytic metaphysics deeply Leibnizian.
- Why: Rationalism at most inventive — saves individuality, freedom, God at once, invents half of modern logic's vocabulary.
- Read first: Monadology (one sitting with notes); note there's NO single Leibniz book containing his system. Then Discourse §§1–18.
- Cautions: "Best possible world" is technical (best compossible package), not "every event is good"; Candide (1759) satirises popularised version post-Lisbon earthquake, not a refutation. Possible worlds are ideas in God's mind, not parallel universes.
