# Blaise Pascal

*Era: Early Modern Philosophy (c. 1600 – 1800) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Core: Reason has principled limits — "the heart has its reasons, which reason does not know"; humans paradoxical greatness/wretchedness, distracting themselves (divertissement). Pascal's Wager: where reason can't decide God's existence, prudence can — finite loss risked for possible infinite gain. SEP nuance: Pascal wrote as believer arguing belief is not unreasonable; did NOT think argument could manufacture saving faith (faith is gift, not conclusion) — presenting Wager as conversion machine / proof reverses his theology.
- Works: Pensées (unfinished notebooks, posthumous 1670); Provincial Letters (1656–57).
- Lineage: Contemporary critic of Cartesian reach; anticipates existential themes (Kierkegaard).
- Read first: Selected Pensées — diversion, two infinities, man's disproportion, Wager, in that order.
