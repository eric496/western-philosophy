# Jean-Jacques Rousseau

*Era: Early Modern Philosophy (c. 1600 – 1800) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Core: Civilisation corrupts: Discourse on Inequality — natural humans solitary, peaceful, moved by self-preservation and pity; private property and comparison generate inequality, vanity (amour-propre), dependence. Social Contract (1762): "Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains" — legitimacy through general will: citizens giving themselves laws collectively can be "forced to be free" (obeying general will = self-legislation = freedom). General will is defined by object (common good, general laws), NOT majority whim/vote count or leader embodying people; can be outvoted in error.
- Works: Discourse on Origin of Inequality (1755); Émile (1762); Social Contract (1762).
- Lineage: Turns Hobbes/Locke contract democratic/participatory; Kant revered him ("Rousseau set me right" on dignity); major (debated) influence on French Revolution, democratic/communitarian theory.
- Why: Asks whether progress itself is problem; gives democracy its most demanding idea of self-government.
- Read first: Social Contract I and II.1–7, then Discourse Part II opening (first man enclosing ground, "This is mine").
- Cautions: Did not propose abolishing society / literal "return to nature" (diagnosis device); totalitarian reading is 20th-c. interpretation to present as such, not settled.
