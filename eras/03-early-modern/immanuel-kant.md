# Immanuel Kant

*Era: Early Modern Philosophy (c. 1600 – 1800) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Synthetic a priori knowledge and "Copernican revolution": some knowledge a priori (independent of particular experience) yet synthetic (extends knowledge — "every event has a cause", arithmetic/geometry on his account). How? Objects of experience conform to our cognition: mind supplies a priori forms of intuition (space, time) and categories of understanding (substance, causality, unity...) structuring all experience; experience = sensibility (given) + understanding (organised). Transcendental idealism: phenomena (appearances under our forms/categories) knowable; noumena (things in themselves) exist/ground appearances but can't be known by theoretical reason. Secures science (phenomenal world law-governed because we structure it), limits metaphysics (God, soul-as-substance, world-as-totality generate antinomies — equally compelling contradictory proofs). Ethics: autonomy — rational agent bound by moral law it gives itself, not inclination/authority/consequences. Categorical imperative: (1) act only on maxim you can at same time will to become universal law; (2) treat humanity always at same time as end, never merely as means. Commands unconditionally (vs hypothetical "if you want X, do Y"). Freedom, God, immortality return as postulates of practical reason, not knowledge.
- Works: Critique of Pure Reason (1781 "A"; substantially revised 1787 "B"); Prolegomena (1783, shorter clearer restatement); Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785); Critique of Practical Reason (1788); Critique of Judgment (1790); Perpetual Peace (1795).
- Lineage: Responds to Leibnizian-Wolffian rationalism (training), Hume (trigger on causality; SEP: central figure of modern philosophy for synthesising rationalism/empiricism), Rousseau (dignity/autonomy). Next era reacts: German Idealism tries removing thing-in-itself; Schopenhauer, neo-Kantianism, analytic philosophy, Rawls, modern deontology rebuild on Kant.
- Why: Redraws map — shows which questions finite rational mind can answer, which must be answered practically, why it keeps asking unanswerable ones.
- Read first: NOT Critique of Pure Reason. Groundwork I–II (ethics), Prolegomena Preamble + Part I (epistemology). First Critique (Aesthetic + Analytic, guided edition) as capstone.
- Cautions: Not "we can know nothing" / not Berkeley-style idealist — Kant rejected that; empirical realism (appearances real, law-governed; space/time transcendentally ideal but empirically real). Noumenon is limit-concept, not hidden world. Categorical imperative ≠ Golden Rule (Kant distinguishes); it's a test of your action's maxim, not a rule list; his strict applications (e.g. lying) are his applications, debated.
