# George Berkeley

*Era: Early Modern Philosophy (c. 1600 – 1800) · [Era overview](README.md) · [Master index](../../index.md)*

- Major: Esse est percipi — "to be is to be perceived": reality consists exclusively of minds and their ideas (SEP summary). Tree/table/stone are collections of ideas; idea exists only in perceiving mind, so unperceived material world behind ideas is incoherent. Denies material substance, NOT existence of things seen/touched. Attack on Locke: primary/secondary collapse (extension/figure as perception-dependent as colour); abstract general ideas impossible (only particular triangles, never "triangle in general"); material substance explains nothing. God as permanent perceiver: things persist unperceived by humans because God perceives continuously; orderliness of unbidden ideas is God's governance — this saves world from scepticism/atheism, which he charged materialism with promoting.
- Works: Essay Towards a New Theory of Vision (1709); Treatise Concerning Principles of Human Knowledge (1710); Three Dialogues between Hylas and Philonous (1713, clearer rewrite for misunderstood Principles; Philonous = Berkeley, Hylas = Lockean). Also De Motu (1721) vs Newtonian absolute space.
- Lineage: Accepts Locke's inventory of ideas, shows it can't stop at material substance. Hume took critique of abstract ideas/sceptical pressure; later claimed by phenomenalism/positivism; Dr Johnson's stone-kick misses point.
- Why: Cleanest demonstration that starting assumptions honestly followed lead somewhere unexpected — perfect training text for argument.
- Read first: Three Dialogues 1–2 — arguably most beginner-friendly primary text in era.
- Caution: Did NOT say things stop existing when you stop looking, nor deny physical world as experienced.
