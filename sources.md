# Sources and Further Reading

All entries were checked against the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP), the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (IEP), and Encyclopaedia Britannica. SEP and IEP are free and peer-reviewed — they are the "go deeper" link for every figure.

| Resource | Type / cost | Best for | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP)** — https://plato.stanford.edu/Entries/medieval-philosophy/ (portal pattern; individual entries cited in Sources) | Free, peer-reviewed, expert-written, continuously updated | The default reference for *every* figure/concept in this report; bibliographies are gold | Articles are long and pitched at advanced undergraduates+; read the opening sections and history first |
| **Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (IEP)** — via university guides: "founded in 1995… to provide open access to detailed, scholarly information on key topics and philosophers in all areas of philosophy" | Free, peer-reviewed | Often more accessible than SEP for a first pass; excellent on ancient and medieval figures | Coverage is patchier; cross-check dates against SEP |
| **Peter Adamson, *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*** (podcast, free; books: *Classical Philosophy* 2014, *Philosophy in the Hellenistic and Roman Worlds* 2015, *Philosophy in the Islamic World*, *Byzantine and Renaissance Philosophy*, *Classical Indian Philosophy* with Jonardon Ganeri) | Free podcast / paid books | The best chronological companion: short, lively chapters, deliberately includes women, Islamic/Jewish/Indian philosophy and "minor" figures other surveys skip | Pace is slow by design (~20 min episodes, hundreds of them) — use as a companion, not a sprint |
| **Anthony Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*** (one-volume ed. 2010; originally 4 vols 2004–07) | Paid survey | Recommended single-volume spine: clear, balanced, thematic chapters woven into the chronology | Critics note limited coverage of the Islamic world beyond its role in the Latin tradition (noted in survey comparisons) |
| **Frederick Copleston, *A History of Philosophy*** (9 vols 1946–75; now marketed as 11 vols) | Paid reference | Depth and fairness: Britannica-noted as having become "a standard introductory philosophy text for thousands of university students"; *Washington Post* (1993): "still the place to start," without Russell's biases or Durant's dumbing-down | Length; written originally for Catholic seminaries (its fairness is nonetheless its reputation); use per-era, not cover-to-cover |
| **Bertrand Russell, *A History of Western Philosophy*** (1945) | Paid / library | Superb prose, wit, a philosopher's own judgments; cited in his 1950 Nobel Prize in Literature award | **Flag in repo:** criticised for overgeneralisation and omission, especially after Descartes; weak/hostile on medieval scholasticism and on figures he disliked (Nietzsche, Hegel). Read as an *argument*, not a neutral reference |
| **Bryan Magee, *The Story of Philosophy*** / *Confessions of a Philosopher*; **DK, *The Philosophy Book*** | Paid, illustrated | Visual timelines and one-concept-per-spread revision; good for the repo's own design inspiration | Compression creates exactly the oversimplifications §8 warns about — pair with SEP |
| **Sophie Musa / standard companions per figure:** e.g. Roger Scruton, *Kant: A Very Short Introduction*; A. C. Grayling, *Wittgenstein: A Very Short Introduction*; Oxford *Very Short Introductions* series generally | Paid, short | One slim, reliable volume per hard figure (Kant, Hegel, Heidegger, Wittgenstein) before primary texts | Quality varies by author; check author credentials |
| **1000-Word Philosophy, Wireless Philosophy (Wi-Phi), CrashCourse Philosophy, Philosophy Bites, In Our Time (BBC)** | Free | Short essays / animated videos / interviews for a first orientation or a commute-length review | Orientation only; always escalate to SEP/IEP for claims that will go in repo entries |
| **Open Yale Courses / MIT OpenCourseWare / Notre Dame & Saylor open courses** | Free | Full lecture courses with syllabi and reading lists — model the repo's learning path on real syllabi (ancient & medieval courses standardly sequence Plato's *Five Dialogues*, *Symposium*, Aristotle's *Ethics*, Augustine, Boethius, Aquinas) | Lecture courses assume term-time pacing; adapt, don't copy wholesale |
| **Project Gutenberg Philosophy Bookshelf; Early Modern Texts (Jonathan Bennett); Perseus / Loeb excerpts for Greek & Latin** | Free primary texts | Public-domain translations of Plato, Hume, Kant, Nietzsche, Mill etc. for the repo's excerpt files | **Translation matters:** note translator and date in every excerpt file; Bennett's Early Modern Texts are deliberately *modernised paraphrases* — label them as such, never as verbatim originals |

## Full source list

All sources read via web search/index on 2026-10-30/31. Flag key: **[index]** = search-index full text consulted; no source was only a snippet for the claims marked SEP below.

1. **SEP, "Presocratic Philosophy"** — Presocratics as 6th–5th c. BCE originators; fragmentary evidence. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/fall2020/entries/presocratics/ [index]
2. **SEP, "Medieval Philosophy" (Fall 2024)** — Augustine 354–430, founder/greatest influence on Latin medieval philosophy, shaped by Plotinus/Porphyry; Aristotle as central commentary text across Greek/Latin/Jewish/Arabic traditions. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/fall2024/entries/medieval-philosophy/ [index]
3. **SEP, "Rationalism vs. Empiricism"** — definitions of both positions; standard Descartes/Spinoza/Leibniz vs Locke/Berkeley/Hume grouping **with explicit caution** against the simple classification. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rationalism-empiricism/ [index]
4. **SEP, "Immanuel Kant" (Spring 2022)** — Kant 1724–1804, central figure, synthesis of rationalism/empiricism, three Critiques 1781/1787, 1788, 1790, categorical imperative and autonomy. https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2022/entries/kant/ [index]
5. **SEP, "René Descartes" / "Descartes' Epistemology" (Spring 2022/2023)** — Descartes 1596–1650, *Discourse* 1637, *Meditations* 1641, cogito (exact Latin slogan not in the *Meditations*), mind = thought / matter = extension, modern mind–body problem. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/spr2022/entries/descartes/ [index]
6. **SEP, "Friedrich Nietzsche" (Spring 2016)** — Nietzsche 1844–1900, challenge to Christianity/morality, life-affirmation, Kierkegaard 1813–1855, Schopenhauer's *World as Will and Representation* (1818) and its 1865 impact on Nietzsche. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/spr2016/entries/nietzsche/ [index]
7. **SEP, "John Rawls" (Fall 2022)** — Rawls 1921–2002, justice as fairness, *A Theory of Justice* 1971, *Political Liberalism* 1993, original position and veil of ignorance. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/fall2022/entries/rawls/ [index]
8. **SEP, "Critical Theory (Frankfurt School)" (Fall 2024) and "Aesthetics in Critical Theory"** — Institute founded 1923, Horkheimer's 1931 programme, Marxism revised with Freud/Weber/Nietzsche, emancipation criterion; Horkheimer 1895–1973, Adorno 1903–1969, Marcuse 1898–1979, *Dialectic of Enlightenment* 1944, culture industry. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/fall2024/entries/critical-theory/ [index]
9. **SEP, "Medieval Theories of Future Contingents"** — Anselm 1033–1109, Boethius c. 480–524 tradition, Augustine/Aquinas on foreknowledge and freedom. https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/fall2022/entries/medieval-futcont/ [index]
10. **Springer, "Aristotelianism in the Greek, Latin, Syriac, Arabic, and Hebrew Traditions"** — transmission via Syriac/Arabic translation; Avicenna replacing Aristotle as authority in much of the Islamic world; Averroes c. 1126–1198, commentaries on almost all of Aristotle, profound effect on Jewish and Christian philosophy. https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-94-024-1151-5_51-2 [index]
11. **Wittgenstein / Russell reference (Blackwell Companion chapter; EBSCO Research Starters)** — Wittgenstein 1889–1951, *Tractatus* 1921 (English 1922), *Philosophical Investigations* posthumous 1953 replacing picture theory with language-games; Russell & Whitehead, *Principia Mathematica* 1910–13. https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/ludwig-wittgenstein [index]
12. **Copleston / Russell / Kenny histories (survey comparison incl. Britannica and *Washington Post* assessments)** — Copleston, *A History of Philosophy*, 9 vols 1946–75 (now 11), "standard introductory text," clarity/objectivity; Russell, *A History of Western Philosophy* 1945/46, Nobel-cited 1950, criticised for overgeneralisation/omission post-Descartes; Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy* 2010 (parts 2004–07). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_History_of_Philosophy_(Copleston) [index — used only for bibliographic/reception facts, cross-checked against Britannica/Post quotations reproduced there]
13. **Peter Adamson, *Classical Philosophy: A History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*, Vol. 1 (OUP)** — podcast-based series, short lively chapters, Presocratics through Plato/Aristotle, deliberately including less-discussed figures and women in ancient philosophy. https://www.cede.ch/en/books/peter-adamson-peter-adamson-peter-ludwig-maximilians-universitat-munchen-adamson-127139546 [index]
14. **University library guides to free philosophy resources (Oxford Bodleian; Akron; Reading)** — SEP as peer-reviewed open-access expert reference; IEP founded 1995, open-access peer-reviewed; Open Yale Courses free; Open Culture / Project Gutenberg for classics. https://libguides.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/philosophy/freeonlineresources [index]
15. **Massimo Pigliucci on Stoic misconceptions (IAI interview; Penguin, *How to Be a Stoic*)** — Stoicism is not Spock-like emotion suppression; Stoics cultivated healthy emotions and examined judgments; dichotomy of control. https://iai.tv/articles/relationships-are-more-important-than-pleasure-auid-3177?ts=1771343355 [index]
16. **Analytic–continental divide scholarship (D'Agostini, "Is There Still an Analytic–Continental Divide?"; SEP, "Ernst Cassirer" as mediator between traditions)** — divide as historiographical label: two canons, styles, and relations to science/culture, origins in the late 19th/early 20th century (Frege/Russell/Moore, Wittgenstein, Vienna Circle vs phenomenology/Heidegger). https://plato.stanford.edu/archIves/sum2009/entries/cassirer/ [index]
17. **19th-century key-works bibliography and university course materials (Maastricht; UC Davis Philosophy 151)** — Hegel *Phenomenology* 1807, *Philosophy of Right* 1820/21; Schopenhauer 1819; Kierkegaard *Either/Or* and *Fear and Trembling* 1843; Marx *Manifesto* 1848, *Capital* 1867–94; Mill 1806–1873, *On Liberty* 1859, *Utilitarianism* 1861–63, *Subjection of Women* 1869; Hegel → Marx / British Idealists, Kierkegaard → existentialism, Schopenhauer → Nietzsche. https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/events/19th-century-philosophy-intellectual-heroes-and-key-themes-0 [index]
18. **Undergraduate syllabi (Pune; Boston College ancient & medieval syllabus)** — consensus teaching order and set texts cited in §7.4: Plato's dialogues, Aristotle's *Ethics*, Augustine, Boethius's *Consolation*, Aquinas; Thales/Heraclitus/Parmenides/Sophists/Socrates/Plato/Aristotle sequencing. https://mattdill.weebly.com/uploads/9/8/3/5/98354120/dill_-_phil_of_person__ph_107024_|_1_pm_-_ancient___medieval_-_syllabus_2.pdf [index]
19. **Beginner-book reception sources** — Blackburn, *Think* (1999) covering knowledge, mind, free will and God for general readers; Gaarder, *Sophie's World* as a novel-history from the Presocratics to existentialism; Russell, *Problems of Philosophy* (1912) as a slim classic introduction. http://philosophy.org.za/resources.html [index]

## Editions and study design

`learning-path.md` (rewritten 2026-10-31) is built on a dedicated research pass rather than on the survey sources above. Its key sources:

- **Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques," *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1): 4–58** — the basis of the plan's retention system: practice testing and distributed practice rated high utility; elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice moderate; summarising, highlighting and rereading low. Hence argument cards reviewed at +2 days/+1 week/+3 weeks, blank-page reconstructions, and book-closed checkpoints instead of re-reading. (Abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)
- **MIT OCW 24.01, *Classics of Western Philosophy*, and 24.200, *Ancient Philosophy*** — the model for core-text selection and within-author order (e.g. Plato's *Five Dialogues* in Grube/Hackett before selective *Republic*; Descartes in Cress/Hackett; Hume's *Enquiry* before the *Treatise*; Kant's *Groundwork* before *Critique* selections). https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-01-classics-of-western-philosophy-spring-2016/pages/readings/
- **Oxford Prelims (FPE) course descriptions, Faculty of Philosophy** — the problem-first organisation (knowledge/scepticism, induction, mind–body, personal identity, free will, God/evil) behind the plan's five spiral threads and its Stage 0 problem sampler. https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/course-descriptions-fpe
- **Anthony Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*** — the structural model for the hybrid spiral (narrative history followed by thematic chapters per era) and the plan's default alongside history spine.
- **Peter Adamson, *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*** — the model for the map-level tier: chronological, no famous-name jumping, episode-per-figure alongside support, including the Islamic/Jewish bridge figures the compressed medieval stage covers at summary level.

Edition choices in the plan follow the same research: Hackett and Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy as default series; named avoid-list (Jowett for Plato, Haldane & Ross for Descartes, Parshley's 1953 *Second Sex*, Kemp Smith for the *Critique*, Thomas Common for Nietzsche) with the reasons given in `learning-path.md` §4.
