# Western Philosophy — A Beginner's Self-Study Map

A structured way to pick up Western philosophy on your own: **79 key figures** across 2,500 years. Every entry is a full detailed treatment: biography, major theories and concepts explained with their reasoning, key works with dates, contributions, impact and legacy, criticisms and debates, and the first thing of theirs you should actually read.

Researched against the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP), the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (IEP), and Encyclopaedia Britannica — all free. Where scholars genuinely disagree (e.g. Heidegger and Nazism, young vs mature Marx, early vs late Wittgenstein), the disagreement is flagged rather than flattened.

## Start here

1. **Skim `timeline.md`** — 2,500 years on one page; get the shape of the story.
2. **Follow `learning-path.md`** — a staged route (Stages 0–5, roughly a year at 4–5 hrs/week) that starts with short, readable primary texts (Plato's *Apology*, Descartes' *Meditations*, Sartre's lecture) instead of the famous-but-brutal books.
3. **Read era overviews in order** (`eras/`), opening a philosopher's entry file when the overview reaches them.
4. **Tick off `tracker.md`** as you finish each figure.

## How the entries are written

Following the research's reading policy: mostly plain-language summary, with short primary-text excerpts only where a passage is load-bearing. Every entry gives, in this order: the problem the philosopher inherited, their signature idea, key works with dates, lineage (responded to / influenced), why it matters, the recommended first read, and cautions where beginners usually go wrong.

Three habits that make the material stick:

- For each figure learn **their one problem, their one signature move, and the strongest objection from the next figure** — philosophy is an argument carried on across centuries, and the entries are cross-linked that way.
- Prefer the **"read first"** text over the famous one (Russell's *Problems of Philosophy*, not *Principia Mathematica*; Beauvoir's *Ethics of Ambiguity* before *The Second Sex* — in the complete 2010 translation).
- When a summary and a claim seem to conflict, check the SEP entry linked in `sources.md` — it is the arbiter used throughout.

## Honesty notes

- Dates marked **c.** are scholarly conventions, especially for ancient and medieval births (e.g. Maimonides 1135 vs 1138).
- A few publication dates genuinely differ between sources and are flagged where they occur: Hegel's *Philosophy of Right* (1820 vs 1821), Schopenhauer's *The World as Will and Representation* (1818 vs 1819).
- Labels like "rationalist vs empiricist" and "analytic vs continental" are later textbook maps — useful, but the SEP warns both must be adopted with great caution; the entries say where they mislead.
- Grouped entries exist where the school is the unit (Stoics, Skeptics, Vienna Circle, Frankfurt School, Anscombe & Foot).

*Summaries are original study notes based on the sources in `sources.md`; quotations from primary texts are kept short and attributed.*
