# Common Misconceptions and Oversimplifications

Read this before the popular summaries — these are the errors the research flagged most often, with the correction for each.

> Repository rule: every entry below should become a **callout box** in the relevant philosopher file. Beginners remember slogans; the repo's job is to attach the correction to the slogan at the moment of first encounter.

1. **Stoicism ≠ suppressing emotion / "stiff upper lip."** Massimo Pigliucci: the common misconception is that "living like a Stoic is like living like Mr. Spock… stiff upper lip and suppressing emotions, both of which have nothing to do with actual Stoicism." Stoics distinguished destructive passions (*pathē*, based on false judgments) from healthy emotions (*eupatheiai*: joy, rational wish, caution) and cultivated the latter; *apatheia* means freedom from destructive passions, not apathy. Modern "pop-Stoicism" that sells emotional unavailability or mere productivity/grit — stripped of Stoic ethics, physics and concern for others — is a dilution to flag, not to reproduce.
2. **Epicureanism ≠ hedonistic indulgence.** An "epicure" in restaurant reviews means the opposite of Epicurus's teaching: the safest pleasures are modest, and overindulgence is *irrational* on Epicurus's own calculus because of its painful consequences. Friendship, not luxury, is his chief security.
3. **Nietzsche: three linked errors.** (a) "God is dead" is a *diagnosis of cultural unbelief and impending nihilism*, spoken by a madman who says he has come too early — not a cheerful atheist slogan. (b) The Übermensch is a *value-creating ideal*, not a racial/biological type; Nietzsche broke with Wagner in part over antisemitism, despised German nationalism, and called himself a "good European." His Nazi image was manufactured posthumously, substantially through his sister's editing and selective publication. (c) "Will to power" and "what doesn't kill me makes me stronger" are not self-help endorsements of cruelty or of all suffering — Nietzsche explicitly valued self-overcoming and creation, not domination for its own sake, and his texts are aphoristic and deliberately provocative, so single-quote summaries systematically distort him. Present the scholarly contest over his meaning rather than a settled caricature in either direction.
4. **Machiavelli ≠ "the ends justify the means."** He never wrote that sentence. *The Prince* argues a ruler must be prepared to act against conventional virtue *from necessity* to preserve the state, and even there praises founders and blames pointless cruelty (Agathocles' crimes, he says, cannot be called virtue). The *Discourses* — the longer, republican work — argue free republics with citizen virtue are the best regime. Reducing him to *The Prince* alone, chapters 15–18 alone, is the error.
5. **Plato's Forms are not "ideas in Plato's head," and the Cave is not "the Matrix with togas."** Forms are mind-independent realities on Plato's account — that is precisely what Aristotle attacks. The pop-culture Plato (a dreamy idealist who hated the real world) misses the mathematician, political theorist and relentless arguer of the dialogues — and many "Platonic doctrines" are spoken by characters in dialogues whose conclusions Plato may be testing.
6. **Socrates wrote nothing, and "Socratic" ≠ "Plato's character Socrates."** Any quote attributed to Socrates needs its source (Plato? Xenophon? Aristophanes' caricature?) named in the repo file.
7. **Aristotle's "mean" is not mediocrity or splitting the difference.** The mean is "relative to us," determined by practical wisdom in the situation — for a soldier, courage may lie far toward boldness. And *eudaimonia* is a lifetime's activity, not a mood.
8. **Medieval philosophy ≠ theology with no arguments, and it was not only Christian.** See §2: Avicenna, Averroes and Maimonides are not "transmitters" who merely couriered Aristotle — they are original philosophers *and* the Latin West's Aristotle arrived *through* their interpretations. Also avoid "Aquinas proved God, end of story": the Five Ways presuppose Aristotelian metaphysics (act/potency, causation) that must be presented with the arguments, or the proofs look like word-magic.
9. **Descartes' cogito is not "I think, therefore I am special / consciousness creates reality."** It is a minimal certainty claim found *while doubting*, and whether it is an inference or an intuition is a live scholarly question. Note (§3) the exact slogan does not appear in the *Meditations*.
10. **The rationalist/empiricist divide is a teaching map, not history itself.** SEP warns against it explicitly (§3 headnote): Locke accepts intuition/deduction for God's existence; Descartes does extensive empirical science; Leibniz wrote a book-length reply to Locke. In the repo, tag philosophers "rationalist-leaning / empiricist-leaning *on the sources-of-knowledge question*" rather than as team members.
11. **Kant's thing-in-itself is not a hidden second world we could peek at with better instruments.** It is a limit-concept: things considered apart from the conditions under which we can experience them. And the categorical imperative is not "the Golden Rule" — Kant explicitly distinguishes his universal-law test from it.
12. **Hegel's dialectic is not a mechanical "thesis–antithesis–synthesis" machine** (see §4, Hegel entry), and "Hegel = apologist for the Prussian state" is as flattened as "Marx = Hegel + economics." Marx's own texts, later Marxisms, and regimes invoking Marx are three different subjects — keep the file structure separate.
13. **Utilitarianism is not "whatever maximises my happiness" or crude act-counting.** Mill's qualitative pleasures, rule-utilitarian variants, and Singer's impartial, demanding version each answer the standard objections differently; present at least one serious objection (rights/integrity — e.g. Williams, Rawls) in the same file.
14. **Existentialism is not a single doctrine and Sartre is not its pope for all members.** Kierkegaard (theist, pre-label), Heidegger (rejected the label for *Being and Time*), Camus (rejected it), Beauvoir (systematically corrected Sartre) differ on God, freedom and others. "Existence precedes essence" is Sartre's formula, not a shared creed.
15. **Analytic vs continental is not science vs nonsense (or clarity vs depth).** It is a historically produced divergence of canons, styles and institutions (§5 headnote), with shared roots and growing overlap. A repo that sneers in either direction will mis-educate.
16. **Foucault does not say "everything is power, so nothing is true," and Derrida does not say "nothing exists outside language."** Both are caricatures addressed in their entries above (§5). Genealogy and deconstruction are *reading and research methods* with specific steps — teach the steps.
17. **"God is dead," "the unexamined life," "I think therefore I am," "hell is other people," "the banality of evil"** — the repo should maintain a `quotes-in-context.md`: every famous line gets its work, chapter/section, speaker (often a character, not the author) and immediate context. Half of §8 is generated by decontextualised quotation.
