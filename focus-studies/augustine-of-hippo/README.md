# Focus Study: Augustine of Hippo (354 – 430)

## The figure in brief

Augustine was born in 354 at Thagaste in Roman North Africa, now Souk Ahras in Algeria, to a pagan father, Patricius, and a Christian mother, Monica. He was educated at Madauros and Carthage and became a teacher of rhetoric. At eighteen Cicero's *Hortensius* turned him toward philosophy, and for some nine years he was a hearer among the Manichees, a dualist sect that taught two warring principles of good and evil. In 384 he won the chair of rhetoric at Milan. There he heard Bishop Ambrose preach, read "the books of the Platonists" in Latin translation, and in 386 underwent the conversion he describes in the garden scene of *Confessions* VIII. He was baptised at Easter 387, lost his mother at Ostia that autumn, and went home to Africa to live in a small community. In 391 the congregation at Hippo Regius seized him and made him a priest, and in 395 or 396 he became its bishop. He held the see for some 34 years, preaching, judging and writing against the Manichees, the Donatists and the Pelagians. After the sack of Rome in 410 he wrote *City of God* (413–426/7). He died on 28 August 430, with the Vandals besieging the city.

The ideas this library keeps meeting begin with him or take their Western form in his hands: the restless heart that finds rest only in God, evil as the privation of good, the will divided against itself, the inward turn to memory and self-knowledge, time as a stretching of the mind, *si fallor, sum* as an answer to the sceptics, free will and its dependence on grace, the two cities founded on two loves, and the conditions of a just war.

## Why he rewards weeks of study

First, *the book*: the *Confessions* is a philosophical argument in the form of a life, and its arguments about evil, the will, memory and time are often read as set pieces. Reading the whole book shows how each one answers a question the life has raised. Second, *the change of mind*: Augustine wrote for forty years and revised his views in public, above all on free will and grace, where the confident *On Free Choice* of his thirties meets the anti-Pelagian treatises of his old age. Tracing that change is one of the best lessons in reading a philosopher across a career. Third, *the reach*: Boethius, Anselm and Aquinas built on him; Luther and Calvin, the Jansenists and Pascal claimed him; Descartes' cogito was compared with his *si fallor, sum* within months of publication; and Wittgenstein's *Philosophical Investigations* opens by quoting him. Reading him closely is reading the bridge from ancient philosophy to the Middle Ages, which is what Stage 2 is for.

## The study plan at a glance

The library's [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Augustine first in **Stage 2** (Medieval and Renaissance Bridge): *Confessions* I–VIII and XI in Boulding or Sheed, *On Free Choice* in selection if the stage is going well, and *City of God* XIX later, never first. This dossier keeps that order and those editions, reads the *Confessions* through Book XI, and gives the treatises one week and *City of God* two. [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md) lays out a **7-week plan** at the path's weekly rhythm:

| Week | Reading | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Confessions* I–IV | The restless heart, the pear theft, the Manichees |
| 2 | *Confessions* V–VII | Milan, the Platonists, evil as privation |
| 3 | *Confessions* VIII–IX | The divided will, the garden, Ostia |
| 4 | *Confessions* X–XI | Memory and time |
| 5 | *On Free Choice* selections; *City of God* XI.26; *On the Trinity* X; the anti-Pelagian works | Free will, certainty and grace |
| 6 | *City of God* I, IV, V, XI–XII, XIV selections | Providence, the evil will, the two loves |
| 7 | *City of God* XIX; *Against Faustus* XXII.74–75; capstone essay | Peace, justice, just war, synthesis |

Weeks 1–4 also use the library's [study guide to the *Confessions*](../../study/augustine-confessions.md), book by book.

The honest total is about **42 hours: 30 at the desk, about 5.6 of assigned media, and about 6.5 of daily card review**, with weekly loads of about 4.8 to 6.5 hours. These hours sit on top of Stage 2's own budget, and Stage 2 is the path's most compressed stage (eight weeks, about 62 hours all-in), so this dossier adds a large share to it; the plan file states the net addition and what to trim.

## On the quotations

The dossier quotes only public-domain translations, so that every quotation could be checked word for word: E. B. Pusey's *Confessions* (1838), Marcus Dods's *City of God* (1871), and the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers translations of *On the Trinity*, the *Enchiridion*, *On Christian Doctrine*, *Against Faustus* and the anti-Pelagian works. Pusey's English is archaic ("Thou madest us for Thyself"), so read the *Confessions* in Boulding or Sheed, as the learning path rules, and use the dossier's quotations to find the passage. *On Free Choice of the Will* has no public-domain English translation, so the dossier paraphrases it. References follow the standard divisions that every modern translation prints: book, chapter and section for the *Confessions* (VIII.12.29), book and chapter for *City of God* (XIX.13).
