# Bibliography — Descartes
**English** | [简体中文](../../zh/focus-studies/rene-descartes/08-bibliography.md)

Annotated and tiered: read downward only as far as your questions require. Prices are publisher list prices where checked (Hackett's own pages, November 2026); Cambridge and Chicago prices were not visible to this check and are left out. The primary texts are *owned*, per the learning path's rule, because library lending windows are shorter than this plan's seven weeks. One fact governs the whole list: every serious edition prints **Adam–Tannery (AT)** volume and page numbers in its margins. AT is the critical edition of Descartes' works, edited by Charles Adam and Paul Tannery in twelve volumes (Paris, 1897–1913) and later revised by the publisher Vrin. A reference such as AT VII 25 means volume VII (the Latin *Meditations*), page 25. Every citation in this dossier uses it, so any of the editions below will find it.

## 1. Primary texts — the editions the plan uses

**John Cottingham, Robert Stoothoff & Dugald Murdoch (trans.), *The Philosophical Writings of Descartes*, vols. I–II (Cambridge, 1985 and 1984), and vol. III, *The Correspondence*, with Anthony Kenny (Cambridge, 1991).** Known everywhere as **CSM** (I–II) and **CSMK** (III). This is the standard English Descartes and the set to own if you buy one thing. Vol. I has the *Rules*, the *Discourse*, the *Principles* in selection and the *Passions of the Soul*; vol. II has the *Meditations* with the complete Objections and Replies; vol. III has the letters, including the Elisabeth correspondence of 1643 that Week 5 reads. Week 6's *Principles* selections and the *Passions* articles are in vol. I. Its terminology is consistent from volume to volume, which makes cross-referencing between the works easy.

**John Cottingham (ed. and trans.), *Meditations on First Philosophy, with Selections from the Objections and Replies* (Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy, 1996; revised edition 2017).** The learning path's ruling and [the study guide's](../../study/descartes-meditations.md) Cambridge option. It reprints the CSM translation with an introductory essay by Bernard Williams and, in the revised edition, a new introduction and bibliography by Cottingham. Its selections cover the passages Weeks 2–4 assign: Caterus on the ontological argument, the Second Replies on clear and distinct perception, Arnauld on the circle and the real distinction, and Gassendi. Use it as the portable copy and CSM II as the desk copy. Cambridge also publishes a Latin–English facing-page version edited by Cottingham (2013), useful if you want to see *cogitatio* on the page.

**Donald A. Cress (trans.), *Discourse on Method and Meditations on First Philosophy* (Hackett, 4th ed., 1998; 128 pp., $13 paper).** The learning path's other ruling. Weeks 1–2 in one slim volume, with AT page references, in a translation revised from the corrected Latin edition. Cress's *Meditations* alone (3rd ed., 1993; 72 pp., $10) and *Discourse* alone (3rd ed., 1998; 58 pp., $10) are also in print; the combined volume is the better buy. Pick Cress or Cottingham for the *Meditations* and do not alternate.

**Roger Ariew & Donald Cress (ed. and trans.), *Meditations, Objections, and Replies* (Hackett, 2006; 208 pp., $14 paper).** The Hackett counterpart to Cottingham's selection. It prints the objections of Caterus, Arnauld and Hobbes with Descartes' replies in full, and gives Gassendi's case through the letter in which Descartes' associates summarized it. Week 7's Hobbes (Third Objections) is here whole. Buy it if you read Cress and do not own CSM II.

**Roger Ariew (ed.), *Philosophical Essays and Correspondence* (Hackett, 2000; 358 pp., $25 paper).** The one-volume Hackett reader: the *Rules*, *Discourse*, *Meditations*, and selections from the *Principles*, the *Passions* and the letters, with a biographical introduction and chronology. It is the cheap way to own Weeks 1, 5 and 6 in a single book if CSM is out of reach.

**Lisa Shapiro (ed. and trans.), *The Correspondence between Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia and René Descartes* (Chicago, The Other Voice in Early Modern Europe, 2007; 280 pp.).** Week 5's letters. Between 1643 and 1649 the two exchanged fifty-eight letters, twenty-six of them Elisabeth's, and this is the only English edition that prints all of them in order with notes. CSMK is built around Descartes' own letters, so it cannot stand in for this volume. Elisabeth's objection of 6/16 May 1643 is what [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) reconstructs as Argument 13; read it here, in full, before you read anyone's summary of it. Shapiro's introduction is also the best short account of Elisabeth as a philosopher in her own right (see [the library entry on early modern women philosophers](../../eras/03-early-modern/early-modern-women-philosophers.md)).

**Desmond M. Clarke (trans.), *Meditations and Other Metaphysical Writings* (Penguin Classics, 1998) and *Discourse on Method and Related Writings* (Penguin Classics, 1999).** A sanctioned alternative pair, and cheap. The first has the *Meditations* with a selection of Objections and Replies and some letters from 1641–49; the second has the *Discourse* with the opening chapters of *The World*. Clarke translates afresh and reads more freely than Cottingham. Use the pair if it is what you own, and check that your printing gives AT references before relying on it for the dossier's citations.

**Avoid as a reading text: Haldane & Ross.** Elizabeth Haldane and G. R. T. Ross's *Philosophical Works of Descartes* (2 vols., 1911–12) is what most free web texts reproduce. The learning path rejects it: it renders *cogitatio* (Latin) and *pensée* (French), "thought", inconsistently, and that blurs the argument of *Meditation* 2. The dossier still *quotes* it, because it is public domain and every word can be checked against the free [Wikisource text of the *Meditations*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Meditations_on_First_Philosophy). Where its English misleads, the dossier gives the Latin or French term in brackets. That is a checking tool, not a reading recommendation. Jonathan Bennett's free versions at [Early Modern Texts](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/authors/descartes) are paraphrases, not translations: a crib for a sentence you cannot parse, never the text you study or cite.

**The originals, free.** French Wikisource hosts page images and text of [the Adam–Tannery edition](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery), volume by volume. You need no French or Latin for this plan, but a glance at AT VII when a Latin term is in dispute settles more arguments than any commentary.

## 2. First secondary books, in order

1. **Tom Sorell, *Descartes: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford, 2000; first published 1987 in the Past Masters series).** One or two evenings; read it before Week 1. Sorell covers the life, the method, the *Meditations* and the physics in short chapters, and he presents Descartes as a scientist as much as a metaphysician, which prepares you for Week 6.
2. **John Cottingham, *Descartes* (Blackwell, 1986).** Read across Weeks 1–5. The translator of CSM writes a compact topical study: method, doubt, the cogito, God, mind and body, matter, ethics. It is the right second book because it uses the same terms as the edition you are reading, and it argues rather than summarizes.
3. **Bernard Williams, *Descartes: The Project of Pure Enquiry* (Pelican, 1978; Routledge Classics, 2005).** Read during Weeks 2–5. Williams asks what it would take to build knowledge from a standpoint free of any particular perspective, and reads the *Meditations* as the attempt. It is demanding and the most philosophically alive book on this list; its chapters on the cogito and the real distinction are the standard modern treatments. Read it slowly, alongside [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md).
4. **Gary Hatfield, *The Routledge Guidebook to Descartes' Meditations* (Routledge, 2014).** The revised and expanded version of his *Descartes and the Meditations* (Routledge Philosophy Guidebooks, 2003), with new material on the method of doubt, the theory of ideas and vision. A meditation-by-meditation commentary by the author of the SEP's main Descartes article. Use it as the tie-breaker when Cottingham and Williams disagree, and for the capstone.

A. C. Grayling's *Descartes: The Life of René Descartes and Its Place in His Times* (Free Press, 2005; US title *Descartes: The Life and Times of a Genius*) is a readable popular biography, not a first book on the philosophy. Its suggestion that Descartes worked as a Catholic intelligence agent during his travels is Grayling's own conjecture; the scholarly biographies in tier 3 do not adopt it.

## 3. Advanced and specialist, by area

Open these when a week's checkpoint question has narrowed to a debate; most are scholarly books at academic prices, best borrowed or bought used.

- **Life (all weeks).** Stephen Gaukroger, *Descartes: An Intellectual Biography* (Clarendon, 1995): the life read through the development of the science and mathematics, with the metaphysics placed late, where it belongs chronologically. Desmond M. Clarke, *Descartes: A Biography* (Cambridge, 2006): more personal, closer to the letters, and sceptical of the legends that go back to Adrien Baillet's *Vie de Monsieur Des-Cartes* (1691). Geneviève Rodis-Lewis, *Descartes: His Life and Thought*, trans. Jane Marie Todd (Cornell, 1998; French original 1995): the French tradition's biography, strong on his schooling at La Flèche. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) draws on all three; where they disagree it says so.
- **Background (Weeks 1, 3, 7).** Jorge Secada, *Cartesian Metaphysics: The Late Scholastic Origins of Modern Philosophy* (Cambridge, 2000). Secada reads Descartes against Suárez and the Jesuit scholastics he was trained on, which explains the vocabulary of Week 3 (objective reality, formal and eminent causes). Pair it with [the library entry on Suárez and Grotius](../../eras/03-early-modern/suarez-and-grotius.md).
- **Doubt and the cogito (Weeks 1–2).** Janet Broughton, *Descartes's Method of Doubt* (Princeton, 2002): the clearest account of what the method of doubt is for and why the cogito survives it. Harry Frankfurt, *Demons, Dreamers, and Madmen* (1970; Princeton reprint 2008): a close commentary on *Meditation* 1 and the opening of the theory of knowledge.
- **The whole system, read in order (Weeks 2–5).** Martial Gueroult, *Descartes selon l'ordre des raisons* (Aubier, 1953), trans. Roger Ariew as *Descartes' Philosophy Interpreted According to the Order of Reasons* (2 vols., Minnesota, 1984–85): the great French reading, which takes the *Meditations*' claim to follow a strict order of reasons at its word. Long and hard; use the chapter on the meditation you are stuck on. Anthony Kenny, *Descartes: A Study of His Philosophy* (Random House, 1968): a lucid analytic reading of the whole system, still the best short guide to the theory of ideas. Margaret Dauler Wilson, *Descartes* (Routledge, Arguments of the Philosophers, 1978): the most influential argument-by-argument study in English, especially on the real distinction.
- **Knowledge and the circle (Week 3).** Edwin Curley, *Descartes Against the Skeptics* (Harvard, 1978): reads the *Meditations* as a reply to sceptics and gives one of the standard solutions to the Cartesian circle. Broughton (above) gives another. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 7, names the positions.
- **Mind and body (Week 5).** Marleen Rozemond, *Descartes's Dualism* (Harvard, 1998), on the real distinction and its scholastic background. Lisa Shapiro, "Princess Elizabeth and Descartes: The Union of Soul and Body and the Practice of Philosophy", *British Journal for the History of Philosophy* 7 (1999): the paper that changed how Elisabeth is read, as a philosopher pressing a real dilemma rather than a confused pupil. Her edition (tier 1) and SEP entry (below) carry the same reading.
- **Physics (Week 6).** Daniel Garber, *Descartes' Metaphysical Physics* (Chicago, 1992): how the laws of motion and the identification of matter with extension rest on the metaphysics, and where they fail. The standard book on Week 6; read the chapters on extension and the laws.
- **Companions.** *The Cambridge Companion to Descartes*, ed. John Cottingham (1992), one chapter per area; *The Cambridge Companion to Descartes' Meditations*, ed. David Cunning (2014), one or more chapters per meditation; *A Companion to Descartes*, ed. Janet Broughton & John Carriero (Blackwell, 2008), the fuller survey. John Carriero, *Between Two Worlds: A Reading of Descartes's Meditations* (Princeton, 2009), reads the book against Aquinas and is the right next step from [the library entry on Aquinas](../../eras/02-medieval/thomas-aquinas.md). These are library or ebook purchases.

## 4. Reference

- **[SEP, "René Descartes"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes/)** — by Gary Hatfield (first published 2008, revised 2023): the map of the life, works and system. Per the learning path's SEP rule, read the opening sections before Week 1 and the rest after. The satellite entries are the deepening move for each week: ["Descartes' Life and Works"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-works/) (Kurt Smith) before Week 1; ["Descartes' Epistemology"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/) (Lex Newman) for Weeks 2–3; ["Descartes' Theory of Ideas"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-ideas/) (Kurt Smith) for Week 3; ["Descartes' Ontological Argument"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-ontological/) (Lawrence Nolan) for Week 4; ["Descartes' Modal Metaphysics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-modal/) (David Cunning) for Weeks 4–5, on eternal truths and the real distinction; ["Elisabeth, Princess of Bohemia"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/elisabeth-bohemia/) (Lisa Shapiro) and ["Dualism"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dualism/) (Howard Robinson & Ralph Weir) for Week 5; ["Descartes' Physics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-physics/) (Edward Slowik) and ["Descartes' Mathematics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-mathematics/) (Mary Domski) for Week 6; ["Descartes' Ethics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-ethics/) (Denis Kambouchner & Louis Rouquayrol, new in 2026) for the provisional morality of Week 1 and the *Passions*. Free; budget an hour or two per entry (an estimate).
- **[IEP, "Descartes, René"](https://iep.utm.edu/rene-descartes/)** — by Justin Skirry: the plainer first pass. Its topic entries are good the night before a week begins: ["Mind-Body Distinction"](https://iep.utm.edu/descartes-mind-body-distinction-dualism/) (Skirry) for Week 5, ["Scientific Method"](https://iep.utm.edu/descartes-scientific-method/) (Fred Wilson) for Weeks 1 and 6, and ["Descartes: Ethics"](https://iep.utm.edu/rene-descartes-ethics/) (Saja Parvizian). Free.
- **[PhilPapers, "René Descartes"](https://philpapers.org/browse/descartes)** — the working bibliography of the live literature; use it after the SEP article, not before.
- **Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, Vol. 3** — the learning path's history spine for Stage 3, alongside throughout.

## 5. Media (all verified November 2026; assignments in [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md))

- **Peter Adamson, [*History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/all-episodes)** — the Descartes run, **eps. 466–475** (posted 30 March–7 September 2025), free, with transcripts for the interviews. This is the learning path's Stage 3 audio spine (eps. 462–502), arriving on schedule. Ep. 466, ["Well Hidden: Descartes' Life and Works"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-life-works) (before Week 1); ep. 468, ["Perchance to Dream: Descartes' Skeptical Method"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-skeptical-method), and ep. 470, ["Gary Hatfield on Descartes' Meditations"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-meditations-hatfield) (Week 2); ep. 471, ["Unclear and Indistinct Ideas: Debating the Meditations"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-debating-meditations), on the circle and the objections (Week 3); ep. 472, ["Less Cheer, More Knowledge: Descartes' Ethics"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-ethics), on the provisional morality, free will and virtue (Week 4); ep. 469, ["Ghost in the Machine: Cartesian Dualism"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-dualism), ep. 473, ["As Rational As You: Elisabeth of Bohemia"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/elisabeth-bohemia), ep. 474, ["States of the Union: Descartes on the Passions"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-passions), and ep. 475, ["Ariane Schneck on Elisabeth and Descartes"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-elisabeth-schneck) (Week 5); ep. 467, ["Written in Mathematics: Descartes' Physics"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/descartes-physics) (Week 6). Optional for Week 7 and after: ep. 476, ["What He Should Have Said: the Early Cartesians"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/early-cartesians); ep. 477, ["The Mind Has No Sex: Cartesianism and Gender"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/cartesianism-gender); ep. 478, ["This Gland Is Your Gland: Cartesian Science"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/cartesian-science); ep. 481, ["True Fool's Gold: Pierre Gassendi"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/gassendi), the author of the Fifth Objections; and ep. 489, ["All Power to Him: Malebranche and Occasionalism"](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/early-modern/france-and-netherlands/malebranche-occasionalism), one heir's answer to Elisabeth (see [Malebranche](../../eras/03-early-modern/nicolas-malebranche.md)).
- **BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*** — three programmes, free on BBC Sounds, chaired by Melvyn Bragg; listen after your own reconstruction, per the path's media rules and its one-per-figure cap. The assigned one is ["Cogito Ergo Sum"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b010mvcp) (28 April 2011; 45 minutes; with Susan James, John Cottingham and Stephen Mulhall), after Week 2. Two are optional: ["The Mind/Body Problem"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p003k9b8) (13 January 2005; 45 minutes; with A. C. Grayling, Julian Baggini and Susan James), after Week 5; and ["The Ontological Argument"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b01mwx64) (27 September 2012; 42 minutes; with John Haldane, Peter Millican and Clare Carlisle), after Week 4, which links Descartes back to [Anselm](../../eras/02-medieval/anselm-of-canterbury.md). No *In Our Time* programme on Elisabeth of Bohemia was found.
- **Peter Millican, [*General Philosophy*](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/series/general-philosophy)** (Oxford Podcasts, added 2010; free under a Creative Commons licence) — the Oxford first-year lectures the learning path names. The Descartes parts total about an hour: [1.4 "From Galileo to Descartes"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/14-galileo-descartes) (11 minutes; before Week 1); [4.1 "Scepticism about the External World"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/41-scepticism-about-external-world) and [4.2 "Possible Answers to External World Scepticism"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/42-possible-answers-external-world-scepticism) (9 minutes each; after Week 2); [4.3 "Cartesian Dualism"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/43-cartesian-dualism) and [4.4 "The Mind-Body Problem"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/44-mind-body-problem) (15 and 18 minutes; after Week 5, with Ryle's and Strawson's responses). Listen-after: Millican assumes you have read the text. Part 2.2, on [Hobbes](../../eras/03-early-modern/thomas-hobbes.md), suits Week 7.
- **[MIT OCW 24.01, *Classics of Western Philosophy*](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-01-classics-of-western-philosophy-spring-2016/)** — lecture notes for the Descartes weeks, keyed to the Hackett editions; the path's Session B companion. Notes, not video. **[Wi-Phi](https://www.wi-phi.com/)**'s short epistemology videos are the path's watch-before for Week 2.
