# Bibliography — Aristotle
**English** | [简体中文](../../zh/focus-studies/aristotle/08-bibliography.md)

Annotated and tiered: read downward only as far as your questions require. Prices are publisher list prices where checked (Hackett's own pages, November 2026); the primary texts are *owned*, per the learning path's rule, because library lending windows are shorter than this plan's nine weeks. One fact governs the whole list: what survives of Aristotle is not what he published. The polished dialogues that ancient readers admired are lost except for fragments; what we read are the school treatises, edited lecture materials whose repetitions and abrupt joins reflect that origin (see [the library entry](../../eras/01-ancient/aristotle.md), "Misreadings to avoid"). Every edition below prints Bekker numbers (page, column and line of the 1831 Berlin Academy edition, e.g. 1106b36) in the margin, so any citation in this dossier can be found in any of them.

## 1. Primary texts — the editions the plan uses

***Nicomachean Ethics*, trans. Terence Irwin (Hackett, 3rd ed., 2019; 480 pp., $18 paper)** or ***Nicomachean Ethics*, trans. C. D. C. Reeve (Hackett, 2014; lvi + 447 pp.; now in a second edition, in paper and since April 2026 a $39.95 hardcover).** The learning path's ruling and [the study guide's](../../study/aristotle-nicomachean-ethics.md) for Weeks 6–7. Irwin has the fuller apparatus: notes on almost every chapter, a long glossary of Greek terms, and in the third edition an appendix of related passages from other works. Reeve reads more smoothly and has numbered endnotes that work like a running commentary. Pick one; do not alternate.

**The New Hackett Aristotle (trans. C. D. C. Reeve) for everything else the plan reads whole.** One translator, one set of terms across the corpus, endnotes keyed to the hard places, and an index of terms that finds every discussion of *energeia* or *ousia* for you:

- ***Physics*** (2018; 420 pp., $25) — Week 2.
- ***Generation of Animals & History of Animals I, Parts of Animals I*** (2019; 488 pp., $29) — Week 3: exactly the plan's biology, *Parts of Animals* I.5 included, in one volume.
- ***De Anima*** (2017; 272 pp., $24) — Week 4.
- ***Metaphysics*** (2016; 688 pp., $29) — Week 5.
- ***Politics: A New Translation*** (2017; 532 pp., $25) — Week 8. (Hackett still sells Reeve's 1998 *Politics*; buy the 2017 one, which matches the series.)
- ***Rhetoric*** (2018; 504 pp., $25 paper) — Week 9.

**For the short logic passages (Week 1): *Readings in Ancient Greek Philosophy*, ed. S. Marc Cohen, Patricia Curd & C. D. C. Reeve (Hackett, 5th ed., 2016; 736 pp.).** The Hackett *Readings* the learning path names, and MIT's course text; you may already own it from the Presocratics row. Where a passage the plan names is not in it, use the reference set below. *Aristotle: Selections*, trans. Irwin & Fine (Hackett, 1995; 650 pp., $34) is the larger alternative anthology, with the glossary Irwin's *Ethics* uses.

***Poetics*, trans. Richard Janko (Hackett, 1987; 261 pp., $17).** The learning path is silent on the *Poetics*; this dossier rules for Janko: an exact translation with a line-by-line commentary and the fragments of the lost dialogue *On Poets*, which matter for the catharsis debate. Joe Sachs's version (Hackett, 2006; 80 pp., $12.95) is the cheap, slim alternative without the commentary.

**The reference set: *The Complete Works of Aristotle: The Revised Oxford Translation*, ed. Jonathan Barnes (Princeton, 1984; 2 vols, Bollingen Series).** The learning path's reference set, and the only place to find everything: the *Categories*, *De Interpretatione*, *Posterior Analytics*, the whole *History of Animals*. It revises the older Oxford translations by many hands, so terms shift from work to work. Use it to look things up, not as your reading copy. Hackett's new two-volume *Aristotle: Complete Works*, ed. C. D. C. Reeve & Pavlos Kontos (Oct. 2025; $130–145), is offered as its successor, mostly in Reeve's translations. It is too new for this dossier to rank; it is the set to buy if you want one translator throughout and the budget allows.

**Avoid as a first read: the old Oxford translations alone.** W. D. Ross's *Ethics* and *Metaphysics* and the rest of the 1908–1952 Oxford series are accurate but terse and unhelped, as the learning path says. The dossier still *quotes* them (Ross, Jowett, J. A. Smith, Hardie & Gaye, Edghill and the others), because they are public domain and every word can be checked online at the [MIT Internet Classics Archive](http://classics.mit.edu/Browse/browse-Aristotle.html). That is a checking tool, not a reading recommendation.

## 2. First secondary books, in order

1. **Jonathan Barnes, *Aristotle: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford, 2000; first published 1982 in the Past Masters series).** About 160 pages, one evening or two; also an audiobook. Read it before Week 1. It covers the whole man (the Lyceum, the research program, logic, change, soul, ethics, art) in short chapters by the editor of the reference set, and it is frank about what is lost and what is wrong.
2. **J. L. Ackrill, *Aristotle the Philosopher* (Oxford, 1981; 160 pp.).** Read during Weeks 1–5. Ackrill's aim is to show Aristotle arguing rather than to summarize him: change, nature, soul, logic, method, metaphysics and ethics, each treated as a live problem with modern parallels. It is the best short training in reading the treatises the way this dossier's [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) does. Inexpensive used; long in print in Oxford's OPUS series.
3. **Jonathan Lear, *Aristotle: The Desire to Understand* (Cambridge, 1988).** Read across Weeks 3–7. Lear starts from the first line of the *Metaphysics*, that all human beings by nature desire to know, and builds the whole system around that desire: nature, change, soul, mind, first philosophy, ethics. It is the most unified picture on this list, and its chapters on nature, change and soul fit Weeks 2–4 closely.
4. **Christopher Shields, *Aristotle* (Routledge Philosophers, 2nd ed., 2014; about 530 pp.).** The comprehensive one, for the capstone and after. Shields is the author of the SEP's main Aristotle article and of the Clarendon *De Anima* below; the second edition adds sections on method, perception, thought, *akrasia* (weakness of will) and *mimēsis* (imitation, the *Poetics*' key term), and closes on today's neo-Aristotelian movements. Use it as the tie-breaker when Ackrill and Lear disagree.

## 3. Advanced and specialist, by area

Open these when a week's checkpoint question has narrowed to a debate; each is a scholarly book, priced at academic rates ($30–100) unless a paperback is noted.

- **Logic (Week 1).** J. L. Ackrill, *Categories and De Interpretatione* (Clarendon Aristotle Series, 1963), translation with philosophical notes: the standard way into *Categories* 1–5 and the sea battle of *De Int.* 9. Robin Smith, *Prior Analytics* (Hackett, 1989; $20), for the syllogism itself. Jonathan Barnes, *Posterior Analytics* (Clarendon, 2nd ed., 1993), for II.19.
- **Nature (Week 2).** William Charlton, *Physics Books I and II* (Clarendon, 1970), translation with notes on the four causes and the teleology argument of II.8. Sarah Waterlow (later Broadie), *Nature, Change, and Agency in Aristotle's Physics* (Oxford, 1982), a hard but searching study of change as actualization.
- **Biology (Week 3).** Armand Marie Leroi, *The Lagoon: How Aristotle Invented Science* (2014), by an evolutionary biologist on Lesbos: the friendly entry, and paperback-priced. James G. Lennox, *Aristotle's Philosophy of Biology* (Cambridge, 2001), the scholarly essays on explanation, kinds and teleology. *The Cambridge Companion to Aristotle's Biology*, ed. Sophia M. Connell (2021), for the state of the field.
- **Soul (Week 4).** Christopher Shields, *De Anima* (Clarendon Aristotle Series, 2016), translation and commentary. *Essays on Aristotle's De Anima*, ed. Martha Nussbaum & Amélie Oksenberg Rorty (Oxford, 1992), where the soul-as-form debate is staged: Burnyeat's essay asking whether an Aristotelian philosophy of mind is still credible, and Nussbaum & Putnam's reply. The active intellect of III.5 is disputed in all of them, as [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) flags.
- **First philosophy (Week 5).** Myles Burnyeat, *A Map of Metaphysics Zeta* (Mathesis, 2001; 176 pp.), a guide to the structure of the hardest book in the corpus before you argue about its conclusion. For Λ and the unmoved mover, the SEP articles below are the right next step before any monograph.
- **Ethics (Weeks 6–7).** *Essays on Aristotle's Ethics*, ed. Amélie Oksenberg Rorty (University of California Press, 1980), classic papers in book order that work as a running commentary. Sarah Broadie, *Ethics with Aristotle* (Oxford, 1991), the close study of happiness, virtue, voluntary action, practical reason, *akrasia* and the place of contemplation. The study guide's debates (the circularity worry, Book X) are argued at full length here.
- **Politics (Week 8).** Richard Kraut, *Aristotle: Political Philosophy* (Oxford, 2002), on the ethical basis of the *Politics* and its major themes, slavery and the common good among them.
- **Rhetoric and Poetics (Week 9).** Stephen Halliwell, *Aristotle's Poetics* (Duckworth, 1986; Chicago reprint 1998), the fullest single-book interpretation in English, catharsis included. Janko's commentary (tier 1) covers the same ground line by line.

## 4. Reference

- **[SEP, "Aristotle"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)** — by Christopher Shields (first published 2008, revised 2020): the map of the life, works and system. Per the learning path's SEP rule, read the introduction and opening sections before Week 1 and the rest after. The satellite entries are the deepening move for each week: ["Aristotle's Logic"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-logic/) (Robin Smith) and ["Aristotle's *Categories*"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-categories/) (Paul Studtmann) for Week 1; ["Aristotle's Natural Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-natphil/) (István Bodnár) and ["Aristotle on Causality"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-causality/) (Andrea Falcon) for Week 2; ["Aristotle's Biology"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-biology/) (James Lennox & Mariska Leunissen) for Week 3; ["Aristotle's Psychology"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-psychology/) (Shields) for Week 4; ["Aristotle's Metaphysics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/) (S. Marc Cohen & C. D. C. Reeve) and ["Aristotle on Non-contradiction"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-noncontradiction/) (Paula Gottlieb) for Week 5; ["Aristotle's Ethics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-ethics/) (Richard Kraut) for Weeks 6–7; ["Aristotle's Political Theory"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-politics/) (Fred Miller) for Week 8; ["Aristotle's *Rhetoric*"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-rhetoric/) (Christof Rapp) and ["Aristotle's Aesthetics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-aesthetics/) (Pierre Destrée) for Week 9. Free; budget roughly an hour or two per entry (an estimate).
- **[IEP, "Aristotle"](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle/)** — by Justin Humphreys: the plainer first pass, per the path's IEP-before rule. Its topic entries are shorter than the SEP's and good the night before a week begins: [Logic](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-logic/) (Louis Groarke), [Motion and its Place in Nature](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-motion/), [Metaphysics](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-metaphysics/), [Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-ethics/) and [Poetics](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-poetics/) (all four by Joe Sachs), [Biology](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-biology/) (Michael Boylan), [Politics](https://iep.utm.edu/aristotle-politics/) (Edward Clayton). Free.
- **Companions.** *The Cambridge Companion to Aristotle*, ed. Jonathan Barnes (Cambridge, 1995): one chapter per area, written for readers new to the field; the first companion to open. *A Companion to Aristotle*, ed. Georgios Anagnostopoulos (Wiley-Blackwell, 2009): forty commissioned essays "from art to zoology", the fuller survey. *The Oxford Handbook of Aristotle*, ed. Christopher Shields (Oxford, 2012; 744 pp.), the research-level version. These are library or ebook purchases (the hardbacks run $100–150), not shelf buys.
- **[PhilPapers, "Aristotle"](https://philpapers.org/browse/aristotle)** — the category and its subcategories are the working bibliography of the live literature; use it after the SEP article, not before.
- **[Perseus Digital Library](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/)** — free Greek texts with Bekker numbers, for seeing the word behind a key term (*ousia*, *energeia*, *eudaimonia*) when you have no Greek edition. The site asks browsers to accept cookies before it loads.
- **Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, Vol. 1** — the learning path's history spine for Stage 1, alongside throughout.

## 5. Media (all verified November 2026; assignments in [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md))

- **Peter Adamson, [*History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/classical-greek/aristotle)** — the Aristotle run, **eps. 34–51** (posted May–October 2011), about 20 minutes each, free; roughly six hours in all. The episodes line up with the plan's weeks: ep. 34, "Mr. Know It All: Aristotle's Life and Works"; ep. 35, "The Philosopher's Toolkit: Aristotle's Logical Works", ep. 36, "A Principled Stand: Aristotle's Epistemology", and ep. 37, "Hugh Benson on Aristotelian Method" (Week 1); ep. 39, "Form and Function: Aristotle's Four Causes", ep. 40, "Let's Get Physical: Aristotle's Natural Philosophy", and ep. 41, "Richard Sorabji on Time and Eternity in Aristotle" (Week 2); ep. 43, "Classified Information: Aristotle's Biology" (Week 3); ep. 42, "Soul Power: Aristotle's *De Anima*" (Week 4); ep. 38, "Down to Earth: Aristotle on Substance", ep. 47, "God Only Knows: Aristotle on Mind and God", and ep. 50, "MM McCabe and Raphael Woolf on Aristotle on Plato" (Week 5, with the critique of the Forms); ep. 44, "The Goldilocks Theory: Aristotle's Ethics" (Week 6); ep. 45, "The Second Self: Aristotle on Pleasure and Friendship", and ep. 46, "Dominic Scott on Aristotle's Ethics" (Week 7); ep. 48, "Constitutional Conventions: Aristotle's Political Philosophy" (Week 8); ep. 49, "Stage Directions: Aristotle's *Rhetoric* and *Poetics*" (Week 9). Ep. 51, "The Next Generation: the Followers of Plato and Aristotle", and ep. 82, "Lost and Found: Aristotelianism after Aristotle", are optional after the capstone. This is the learning path's Stage 1 audio spine (eps. 1–51 complete), arriving on schedule.
- **[*Philosophize This!*](https://www.philosophizethis.org/) (Stephen West)** — [#005, "Aristotle Part 1"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/aristotle-part-1-8z586) (14 July 2013) and [#006, "Aristotle Part 2"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/aristotle-part-2-axker) (25 July 2013). Free, with transcripts. A popularizer's quick tour, useful as a Week 9 review of the whole figure; never as evidence.
- **BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*** — four Aristotle programmes, free on BBC Sounds, each about 45–52 minutes: ["Aristotle's Politics"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b00f8530) (6 Nov. 2008; Week 8); ["Aristotle's Poetics"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b00xw210) (27 Jan. 2011; Week 9); ["Aristotle's Biology"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/m0002cfd) (7 Feb. 2019, with Armand Leroi, Myrto Hatzimichali and Sophia Connell; Week 3); ["Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/m001rylh) (2 Nov. 2023, with Angie Hobbs, Roger Crisp and Sophia Connell; Week 6). Specialist panels chaired by Melvyn Bragg; listen after your own reconstruction, per the path's media rules.
- **Steven B. Smith, *Introduction to Political Philosophy* (Yale PLSC 114, [Open Yale Courses](https://oyc.yale.edu/political-science/plsc-114); session list at [openmedia.yale.edu](https://openmedia.yale.edu/projects/iphone/departments/plsc/plsc114.html))** — lectures **7–9**, "The Mixed Regime and the Rule of Law: Aristotle", on *Politics* I and III, IV, and VII: the plan's Week 8 reading, plus Book IV as a bonus. Free, with video, audio and transcripts. Optional, watch-after, per the path's ruling; Smith reads Aristotle as a political scientist, which is its value as a second voice.
