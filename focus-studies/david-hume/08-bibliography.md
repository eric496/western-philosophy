# Bibliography — Hume
**English** | [简体中文](../../zh/focus-studies/david-hume/08-bibliography.md)

Annotated and tiered: read downward only as far as your questions require. Prices are the usual paperback ranges; the primary texts are *owned*, per the learning path's rule, because library lending windows are shorter than this plan's pace. One fact governs the whole list: Hume is the best-edited early modern philosopher in English. The Clarendon critical editions have been replacing the nineteenth-century texts volume by volume, and the learning path's edition rulings — which this dossier follows throughout — are mostly a matter of not reaching for the superseded books that still crowd secondhand shelves.

## 1. Primary texts — the editions the plan uses

***A Treatise of Human Nature*, ed. David Fate Norton & Mary J. Norton (Oxford University Press, Oxford Philosophical Texts).** The working *Treatise*: clean text, the editors' introduction and annotations, and an analytical index that is itself a map of the system. The companion Clarendon critical edition (2007), by the same editors, is the scholarly text with full apparatus — a library consultation, not a purchase, for this plan's purposes.

***An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*: two ruled editions.** The scholarly text is the Clarendon edition, ed. Tom L. Beauchamp (Oxford, 2000). The learning path's classroom alternative — and the edition its section references assume — is the Hackett edition, ed. Eric Steinberg (2nd ed., 1993): a fraction of the Clarendon price, with the text this dossier's study guide ([*Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*](../../study/hume-enquiry.md)) is keyed to. Buy the Hackett for the plan; consult the Beauchamp for its introduction and notes. **Avoid Selby-Bigge/Nidditch** — the older Oxford text, superseded by Beauchamp's and explicitly ruled out by the learning path.

***An Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals*, ed. Tom L. Beauchamp (Clarendon Press, 1998).** The critical edition of the work Hume called "incomparably the best of my writings"; Beauchamp's introduction is the shortest reliable account of how the second *Enquiry* reorganizes *Treatise* Book III.

***Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*, ed. Dorothy Coleman (Cambridge University Press, Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy, 2007).** The learning path's ruling: a careful text, an introduction that states the who-speaks-for-Hume problem (see [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md)) without rigging it, and the *Dialogues*' publishing history laid out. Kemp Smith's older edition (1935) remains worth consulting for its famous introduction — as an exhibit in the debate, not as the working text.

**The rest of the shelf.** The *Essays Moral, Political, and Literary*, ed. Eugene F. Miller (Liberty Fund, 1985) — the standard one-volume Essays, including "Of the Original Contract," "Of National Characters" (footnote and all: see [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md)), and the economics essays. The *History of England* (Liberty Fund, 6 vols., ed. William B. Todd, 1983) is for readers the plan's Hume converts into Humeans; nothing in the plan requires it. Behind all of these stands the **Clarendon Hume Edition** (Oxford), the ongoing critical edition of the complete works: cite it, consult it in libraries, and let it settle any textual dispute the paperbacks leave open.

## 2. First secondary books, in order

1. **A. J. Ayer, *Hume* (Oxford University Press, Very Short Introductions predecessor series, Past Masters, 1980).** One evening's orientation by the positivist who conscripted Hume in 1936 and came, forty years on, to write about him with sympathy and economy. Ayer's Hume is the auditor of metaphysics; knowing that bias in advance is precisely what makes the little book a good first map — its emphases are labeled by its author's career.
2. **Don Garrett, *Hume* (Routledge, Routledge Philosophers, 2015).** The current standard single-author survey: the whole system — cognitive psychology, causation, morals, religion, politics — expounded as one naturalistic project, with the live interpretive disputes (the New Hume included) flagged at the points where a beginner can use them. Read second, as the frame into which the primary reading is fitted.
3. **Barry Stroud, *Hume* (Routledge, The Arguments of the Philosophers, 1977).** The classic argument-by-argument study, and still the book this dossier argues with most: lucid, exact, and committed to the explanatory reading of Hume — the science of human nature as description, not justification — whose consequences [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) tracks. Read third, slowly, with the *Treatise* open.
4. **Annette Baier, *A Progress of Sentiments: Reflections on Hume's Treatise* (Harvard University Press, 1991).** The *Treatise* read as a developing story: trust, reflection, and the sentiments maturing across its three books into a social philosophy. Baier's Hume is warmer and more communitarian than Stroud's, and the contrast between books 3 and 4 on this list is itself an education in how much of a commentary is a temperament. Her *The Cautious Jealous Virtue: Hume on Justice* (2010) is the sequel for the politics.
5. **Peter Millican (ed.), *Reading Hume on Human Understanding: Essays on the First Enquiry* (Oxford University Press, 2002).** Commissioned essays on the first *Enquiry* section by section — the companion to have open during the plan's *Enquiry* weeks, especially on induction, causation, and miracles, where Millican's own contributions state the current technical questions. Read alongside, not after.

## 3. Advanced and specialist

**Norman Kemp Smith, *The Philosophy of David Hume* (Macmillan, 1941).** The monument that re-founded the field: the naturalist manifesto argued at book length, from the 1905 *Mind* articles onward. Read as a classic *in* the reception history ([06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md)), with the caution its own success created — for two generations, its Hume was simply Hume.

**The New Hume file.** John P. Wright, *The Sceptical Realism of David Hume* (Manchester University Press, 1983) — the founding statement, on the *Treatise*'s natural philosophy. Galen Strawson, *The Secret Connexion: Hume on Causation* (Oxford University Press, 1989) — the case at its most forceful and quotable. Against them, Simon Blackburn, *Ruling Passions* (Oxford University Press, 1998) — quasi-realist projectivism built for morals and meant for export to causation; the debate's two poles in three books.

**Don Garrett, *Cognition and Commitment in Hume's Philosophy* (Oxford University Press, 1997)** — the *Treatise*'s theory of belief and its skeptical crisis, by the author of tier 2's survey writing at full scholarly depth. **David Owen, *Hume's Reason* (Oxford University Press, 1999)** — Book I reread as a theory of reasoning itself: what Hume thinks an inference *is*, and why the skeptical arguments follow from the answer. **Peter Millican's papers** on induction and on the miracles argument — the technical frontier of both debates, best found through the reference tools below. **Angela M. Coventry, *Hume's Theory of Mind* (Continuum, 2007)** — the bundle, the passions, and personal identity treated as one philosophy of mind.

## 4. Reference

- **[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/) (SEP), "David Hume"** — by William Edward Morris and Charlotte R. Brown; the current scholarly map of the life, the system, and the disputes, and this dossier's first resort per the learning path's SEP rule (§1 and introduction before the primary text, the rest after). Its satellite articles deepen the plan's side-quests: "Hume's Moral Philosophy" (Rachel Cohon), "Hume on Religion," "Hume's Aesthetics," and "Hume on Free Will." (House link rule: SEP article links are given only for the registry's verified slugs; reach the Hume articles through the Encyclopedia's search.)
- **[Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/) (IEP), "David Hume"** — the plainer first pass, per the path's IEP-before rule: biography, the system in outline, and the problem of induction walked through slowly. The better first read if the SEP's density daunts.
- ***The Cambridge Companion to Hume*, ed. David Fate Norton (Cambridge University Press, 1993; 2nd ed., with Jacqueline Taylor, 2009)** — the one-volume state of the question by topic: knowledge, causation, morals, religion, politics, aesthetics, by the editor of the working *Treatise*.
- ***Hume Studies*** — the journal of the Hume Society (from 1975); where the live debates of [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) are conducted in public. Library access; the Society's meetings and bibliographies are the field's switchboard.
- **[PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/)** — the Hume categories (under History of Western Philosophy: David Hume, with subcategories for causation, induction, morals, and the *Dialogues*) are the working bibliography of the current literature; use after the SEP article, not before.

## 5. Media (the assignments are in [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md))

- **[*Philosophize This!*](https://www.philosophizethis.org/) (Stephen West)** — the learning path's Stage 3 audio spine, and this dossier's: the series takes over the chronological relay from Locke, Berkeley, and Hume onward, in ~30–35-minute episodes, free, with transcripts. Preview or review only, under the path's rigor caveat — West is a skilled popularizer, and the dossier's reconstructions are the standard, not the show's compressions.
- **Peter Adamson, *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*** — an honest absence, recorded per house rule. The series is the library's breadth engine for Stages 1–2, but as verified for this dossier it stands at [ep. 502 (Bayle)](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/all-episodes), in the mid-seventeenth century: **it has not yet reached Hume**, and at its pace will not for years. Do not wait for it; the relay passes to *Philosophize This!* for exactly this stretch.
- **Peter Millican, *General Philosophy* (Oxford Podcasts, [series page](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/series/general-philosophy))** — Oxford's first-year lecture course in audio, whose early-modern sequence tracks this stage's Descartes → Locke/Berkeley → Hume → Kant order almost exactly. Millican is also tier 2's editor and the miracles literature's central figure; listen *after* the reading, per the path's ruling, roughly an hour a week across the Hume weeks. Free.
- **[MIT OCW 24.01, *Classics of Western Philosophy*](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-01-classics-of-western-philosophy-spring-2016/)** — lecture **notes** (there is no video) for the course's Hume sessions, keyed to the same Hackett editions the path names; the path assigns them in place of other secondary reading for the Hume week. Free.
- **Gregory Sadler, Hume's *Enquiry* lecture set ([YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@GregoryBSadler/))** — 25 short lectures working through the *Enquiry* section by section, per the learning path's inventory. Walk-time re-consolidation, and the path's cap applies: at most one lecture set per figure.
