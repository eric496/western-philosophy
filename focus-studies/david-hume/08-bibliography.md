# Bibliography — Hume
**English** | [简体中文](../../zh/focus-studies/david-hume/08-bibliography.md)

Annotated and tiered: read downward only as far as your questions require. Prices are given only where checked on the publisher's own page (Hackett, November 2026); Oxford, Cambridge and Liberty Fund prices were not visible to this check and are left out. The primary texts are *owned*, per the learning path's rule, because library lending windows are shorter than this plan's eight weeks. One fact governs the whole list: the dossier cites Hume by the **Clarendon paragraph numbers** (T 1.3.6.11, EHU 4.18, EPM 9.22, DNR 12.1), which the Clarendon Edition of the Works of David Hume introduced and which the editions below print. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md) explains the system and the older SBN page numbers. Buy editions that print the paragraph numbers, and every reference in this dossier will find its place.

## 1. Primary texts — the editions the plan uses

**Eric Steinberg (ed.), *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, 2nd ed. (Hackett, 1993; 151 pp., $12 paper).** The learning path's first ruling for the book the plan is built around, and the cheapest good copy. It also prints the *Abstract* of the *Treatise* (Week 2) and *A Letter from a Gentleman to His Friend in Edinburgh* (1745), Hume's reply to the charges of atheism and extreme scepticism that cost him the Edinburgh chair. Steinberg's introduction is short and sets the *Enquiry* against Book I of the *Treatise*. Weeks 1–5 and the start of Week 8 live in this book.

**Tom L. Beauchamp (ed.), *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (Oxford Philosophical Texts, 1999).** The learning path's other ruling, and the student version of Beauchamp's critical text (Clarendon, 2000). It has a long editor's introduction and annotations keyed to the paragraphs. Pick Steinberg or Beauchamp and do not alternate; the paragraph numbers are the same.

**Tom L. Beauchamp (ed.), *An Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals* (Oxford Philosophical Texts, 1998).** Week 8's text, from the same editor and on the same plan as his first *Enquiry*. It includes the four appendices; Appendix 1, on moral sentiment, is assigned. The critical text is the Clarendon volume of the same year.

**David Fate Norton & Mary J. Norton (eds), *A Treatise of Human Nature* (Oxford Philosophical Texts, 2000).** The *Treatise* for Weeks 1–4 and 7, the student edition of their Clarendon text (2007, two volumes). It prints the Appendix, whose paragraphs 10–21 hold Hume's second thoughts on personal identity (Week 4), and the *Abstract*. Norton's introduction is a good map of the three Books. Per the learning path, open it only after the matching *Enquiry* sections: "Starting with the *Treatise* is the classic error."

**Dorothy Coleman (ed.), *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion and Other Writings* (Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy, 2007).** The learning path's ruling for Weeks 5–6. Besides the twelve Parts of the *Dialogues*, its "Other Writings" give Hume's memoranda, the fragment on evil, extracts from his letters to Hutcheson, Mure and Gilbert Elliot about the *Dialogues*, selections from *The Natural History of Religion*, and selections from Bayle (see [Bayle & Voltaire](../../eras/03-early-modern/bayle-and-voltaire.md)). That covers Week 6's NHR reading. Coleman's introduction explains what "natural religion" meant in 1750s Edinburgh.

**Eugene F. Miller (ed.), *Essays, Moral, Political, and Literary*, revised ed. (Liberty Fund, 1987).** Week 8's "Of the Standard of Taste" and "Of the Original Contract", plus *My Own Life* (Week 1), the essays Hume withdrew, and "Of Suicide" and "Of the Immortality of the Soul". It is the standard text of the essays and the one most scholarship cites by page. Liberty Fund keeps a free online text on its Online Library of Liberty; that site refused this check (see the report), so no link is given here.

**Hume Texts Online, free.** Peter Millican and Amyas Merivale's [davidhume.org](https://davidhume.org/) gives every work in Hume's own spelling, with the Clarendon number and SBN page at the head of each paragraph: the [first *Enquiry*](https://davidhume.org/texts/e/), the [*Treatise*](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/), the [second *Enquiry*](https://davidhume.org/texts/m/), the [*Dialogues*](https://davidhume.org/texts/d/), the [*Natural History of Religion*](https://davidhume.org/texts/n/), the [*Abstract*](https://davidhume.org/texts/a/), [*My Own Life*](https://davidhume.org/texts/mol/), and the essays (for example ["Of the Standard of Taste"](https://davidhume.org/texts/empl1/st) and ["Of the Original Contract"](https://davidhume.org/texts/empl2/oc)). Every Hume quotation in this dossier is checked against it. Use it to search a phrase, to check a reference, or to read on a phone. It is a text, not an edition with notes, so own one of the books above as well.

**Avoid as a first read: Selby-Bigge/Nidditch.** L. A. Selby-Bigge's Oxford *Treatise* (1888) and *Enquiries* (1894), revised by P. H. Nidditch, are the source of the SBN page numbers that older scholarship cites. The learning path rejects them as reading texts: they are superseded by the Clarendon editions, they have no paragraph numbers, and they give a beginner no help. Jonathan Bennett's versions at [Early Modern Texts](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/authors/hume) are modernized paraphrases. They are a crib for a sentence you cannot parse, never the text you study or cite.

## 2. First secondary books, in order

1. **Simon Blackburn, *How to Read Hume* (Granta, 2008).** One evening, before Week 1. Short chapters, each built on a passage from the *Treatise* or the *Enquiries*, read closely. Blackburn is a quasi-realist about causation and morals and an opponent of the New Hume reading, and he says so; keep that in mind when you reach [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).
2. **A. J. Ayer, *Hume* (Oxford, Past Masters, 1980; reissued as *Hume: A Very Short Introduction*, 2000).** One or two evenings, Weeks 1–2. Ayer reads Hume as a forerunner of logical positivism, which makes him a sharp guide to the fork and the copy principle and a partial one on the passions and morals. Read it as a witness to the twentieth-century Hume (see [the Vienna Circle](../../eras/05-20th-century/vienna-circle-logical-positivism.md)), not as the last word.
3. **Peter Millican, introduction to his edition of *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (Oxford World's Classics, 2007).** Read across Weeks 1–5. You do not need a second copy of the text for this: borrow it for the introduction, which is a section-by-section guide to the *Enquiry*'s arguments by the editor of Hume Texts Online. It is the clearest single account of how the *Enquiry* differs from Book I of the *Treatise*.
4. **James A. Harris, *Hume: An Intellectual Biography* (Cambridge, 2015).** The life, Weeks 1 and 8. Harris follows Hume's writing career work by work, the essays and the *History of England* included, and reads him as a man of letters as much as a philosopher. It is the newest full biography and the right one for [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md).
5. **Ernest Campbell Mossner, *The Life of David Hume* (Nelson, 1954; 2nd ed., Oxford, 1980).** The standard documentary life, built from the letters, and the source of most of the dates in [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md). Use it as a reference: look up the year you need. Harris is the better read; Mossner has more facts.

## 3. Advanced and specialist, by area

Open these when a week's checkpoint question has narrowed to a debate. Most are scholarly books at academic prices, best borrowed or bought used.

- **The whole philosophy (all weeks).** Barry Stroud, *Hume* (Routledge, Arguments of the Philosophers, 1977): Hume as a naturalist who explains belief rather than justifies it; the best book-length treatment of the sceptical solution (Week 2) and the external world (Week 4). Don Garrett, *Cognition and Commitment in Hume's Philosophy* (Oxford, 1997): close reconstructions of the copy principle, the missing shade, induction, causation, personal identity and the sceptical crisis of T 1.4.7, often defending Hume against standard objections. Garrett's *Hume* (Routledge Philosophers, 2015) is the shorter, later survey of the whole system. Norman Kemp Smith, *The Philosophy of David Hume* (Macmillan, 1941; Palgrave reissue 2005): the book that turned the reading of Hume from sceptic to naturalist, arguing that feeling governs reason across the *Treatise*. Long and dated in places, it is still the origin of the debate.
- **The *Treatise* read as a whole (Weeks 4, 7).** Annette C. Baier, *A Progress of Sentiments: Reflections on Hume's Treatise* (Harvard, 1991): reads Book I's sceptical end as a turn toward Books II and III, where reason is corrected by the passions and by society. The right companion to Week 7. Robert J. Fogelin, *Hume's Skepticism in the Treatise of Human Nature* (Routledge, 1985): the case that Hume's scepticism is real and is not dissolved by his naturalism. Paul Russell, *The Riddle of Hume's Treatise: Skepticism, Naturalism, and Irreligion* (Oxford, 2008): reads the *Treatise* as an irreligious book from the start, aimed at Samuel Clarke and the Christian rationalists, and so links Weeks 1–4 to Weeks 5–6.
- **Reason, induction and causation (Weeks 2–3).** David Owen, *Hume's Reason* (Oxford, 1999): what Hume meant by "reason", and why the induction argument is about the inferential faculty and not about deductive validity. Helen Beebee, *Hume on Causation* (Routledge, 2006): a clear guide to the two definitions of cause and to the New Hume debate, from a projectivist. Rupert Read & Kenneth A. Richman (eds), *The New Hume Debate* (Routledge, 2000; revised 2007): the papers on both sides of the claim that Hume believed in real causal powers. The New Hume side is John P. Wright (*The Sceptical Realism of David Hume*, Manchester, 1983), Galen Strawson (*The Secret Connexion*, Oxford, 1989; revised 2014) and Edward Craig; the traditional side is Kenneth Winkler, Simon Blackburn and Peter Millican. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 5, names the positions. For the reply to Hume that the path's Stage 3 gate essay asks for, read [Kant](../../eras/03-early-modern/immanuel-kant.md) and [Popper](../../eras/05-20th-century/karl-popper.md) next to the SEP's "Kant and Hume on Causality" (below).
- **The first *Enquiry* section by section (Weeks 1–5).** Peter Millican (ed.), *Reading Hume on Human Understanding: Essays on the First Enquiry* (Oxford, 2002): essays by leading scholars on each part of the book, with Millican's long critical survey of section 4. The deepening move after his World's Classics introduction.
- **Miracles (Week 5).** John Earman, *Hume's Abject Failure: The Argument Against Miracles* (Oxford, 2000): a probabilist's attack on EHU 10, arguing that Hume's maxim is either trivial or wrong. Robert J. Fogelin, *A Defense of Hume on Miracles* (Princeton, 2003): the reply, arguing that Earman misreads the structure of the section. Read the two together after you have reconstructed Argument 10 yourself.
- **Religion (Weeks 5–6).** J. C. A. Gaskin, *Hume's Philosophy of Religion* (2nd ed., Macmillan, 1988): the standard survey of the *Dialogues*, NHR, miracles and the essays on suicide and immortality, and the source of the "attenuated deism" reading of Philo's reversal in DNR 12. Russell's *Riddle* (above) argues the other way.
- **Morals and the passions (Weeks 7–8).** Rachel Cohon, *Hume's Morality: Feeling and Fabrication* (Oxford, 2008): the motivation argument of T 2.3.3, the moral sense of T 3.1, and justice as an artificial virtue, by the author of the SEP's entry on Hume's moral philosophy. Pair it with [the moral sense school](../../eras/03-early-modern/moral-sense-school.md), Hume's background, and with [Adam Smith](../../eras/03-early-modern/adam-smith.md), his friend and heir.
- **The friendship with Smith (Week 8).** Dennis C. Rasmussen, *The Infidel and the Professor: David Hume, Adam Smith, and the Friendship That Shaped Modern Thought* (Princeton, 2017): a readable joint biography that ends with Hume's death and Smith's letter to Strahan, which [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) discusses.
- **Companions.** *The Cambridge Companion to Hume*, ed. David Fate Norton (1993; 2nd ed. with Jacqueline Taylor, 2009), one chapter per area, opening with Norton's own introduction to Hume's thought. These are library or ebook purchases.

## 4. Reference

- **[SEP, "David Hume"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)** — by Hsueh Qu and Elizabeth S. Radcliffe (first published June 2026, replacing the former entry by William Edward Morris and Charlotte R. Brown): the map of the life, works and system. Per the learning path's SEP rule, read the opening sections before Week 1 and the rest after. There is no separate SEP entry on Hume on causation. The satellite entries, by week: ["The Problem of Induction"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/induction-problem/) (Leah Henderson) for Week 2; ["Kant and Hume on Causality"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-hume-causality/) (Graciela De Pierris & Michael Friedman) for Weeks 2–3 and the gate essay; ["Hume on Free Will"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-freewill/) (Paul Russell) for Week 3; ["Hume's Newtonianism and Anti-Newtonianism"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-newton/) (Eric Schliesser & Tamás Demeter) for Weeks 1–3; ["Hume on Religion"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-religion/) (Paul Russell & Anders Kraal), ["Miracles"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/miracles/) (Timothy McGrew & Robert Larmer) and ["Teleological Arguments for God's Existence"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/teleological-arguments/) (Del Ratzsch & Jeffrey Koperski) for Weeks 5–6; ["Hume's Moral Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-moral/) (Rachel Cohon) and ["Kant and Hume on Morality"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-hume-morality/) (Eric Entrican Wilson & Lara Denis) for Week 7; ["Hume's Aesthetics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-aesthetics/) (Theodore Gracyk) for Week 8; ["Scottish Philosophy in the 18th Century"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scottish-18th/) (Alexander Broadie & Craig Smith) for the context, including [Reid](../../eras/03-early-modern/thomas-reid.md). Free; budget an hour or two per entry (an estimate).
- **[IEP, "Hume, David"](https://iep.utm.edu/hume/)** — by James Fieser: the plainer first pass. Its topic entries suit the night before a week begins: ["Hume, David: Causation"](https://iep.utm.edu/hume-cau/) (C. M. Lorkowski) for Weeks 2–3, ["Hume, Imagination"](https://iep.utm.edu/hume-ima/) (Jonathan Cottrell) for Weeks 1 and 4, and ["Hume, David: Religion"](https://iep.utm.edu/hume-rel/) (C. M. Lorkowski) for Weeks 5–6. Free.
- **[The Hume Society](https://www.humesociety.org/)** publishes the journal [*Hume Studies*](https://www.humesociety.org/hume-studies/), where most of the specialist debate happens. Use it after the books, not before.
- **Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, Vol. 3** — the learning path's history spine for Stage 3, alongside throughout.

## 5. Media (all verified November 2026; assignments in [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md))

Peter Adamson's *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps* has not reached Hume. The learning path's Stage 3 "Listen & watch" switches spines here: *Philosophize This!* takes over from Locke, Berkeley and Hume, with Millican's lectures as the lecture audio. Per the path's media rules, listen after your own reconstruction, never instead of it.

- ***[Philosophize This!](https://www.philosophizethis.org/)*** (Stephen West; free) — the Hume run, **eps. 51–55** (posted 24 February–31 March 2015). The path's rigor caveat applies: West is a skilled popularizer, not an academic. Ep. 51, ["David Hume Pt. 1"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/david-hume-7waml), on is and ought, the limits of science and causation (Weeks 2–3); ep. 52, ["David Hume Pt. 2"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/david-hume-pt2-g685x), on the design argument and miracles (Weeks 5–6); ep. 53, ["David Hume Pt. 3"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/david-hume-pt3-mbc8z), on the soul, the self and custom (Week 4); ep. 54, ["David Hume Pt. 4 - Art"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/david-hume-art-prcgk), on "Of the Standard of Taste" (Week 8); ep. 55, ["Interview on Hume with Massimo Pigliucci"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/interview-massimo-pigliucci-6lm4e), optional review. Ep. 56 begins Kant.
- **Peter Millican, [*General Philosophy*](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/series/general-philosophy)** (Oxford Podcasts; free, audio) — the Oxford first-year lectures the path names, by the editor of Hume Texts Online. The Hume parts total about 55 minutes: [2.6 "David Hume"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/26-david-hume) (13 minutes; after Week 1); [3.1 "Hume's Argument Concerning Induction"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/31-humes-argument-concerning-induction) and [3.2 "Responses to Hume's Famous Argument"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/32-responses-humes-famous-argument) (13 and 10 minutes; after Week 2); [7.3 "Hume on Liberty and Necessity"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/73-hume-liberty-and-necessity) and [7.4 "Making Sense of Free Will and Moral Responsibility"](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/74-making-sense-free-will-and-moral-responsibility) (10 minutes each; after Week 3). Millican assumes you have read the text.
- **BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*** — chaired by Melvyn Bragg, free on BBC Sounds; the path's one-per-figure cap applies. The assigned one is ["David Hume"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b015cpfp) (6 October 2011; 42 minutes; with Peter Millican, Helen Beebee and James Harris), after Week 4. Optional: ["Free Will"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b00z5y9z) (10 March 2011, the 500th edition; 45 minutes; with Simon Blackburn, Helen Beebee and Galen Strawson), after Week 3; ["The Enlightenment in Scotland"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00548ln) (5 December 2002; 45 minutes; with Tom Devine, Karen O'Brien and Alexander Broadie), for the context of Week 1; and ["Common Sense Philosophy"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b007qhbn) (21 June 2007; 45 minutes; with A. C. Grayling, Melissa Lane and Alexander Broadie), on Reid's answer to Hume, after Week 4. ["Miracles"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b00dkh78) (25 September 2008) is a history of miracles in religion and touches EHU 10 only in passing. No *In Our Time* programme on the problem of induction was found.
- **[MIT OCW 24.01, *Classics of Western Philosophy*](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-01-classics-of-western-philosophy-spring-2016/)** — lecture notes for the Hume weeks, keyed to the Hackett *Enquiry*; the path's Session B companion. Notes, not video.
- **Bonus audio.** Gregory Sadler's [*Sadler's Lectures* podcast](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/the-sadlers-lectures-podcast) has a 25-episode set on the first *Enquiry*. The path allows at most one bonus set per figure, as walk-time review. The Kenny Vol. 3 audiobook may replace *Philosophize This!* for this stretch, per the path; not both.
