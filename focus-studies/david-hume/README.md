## The figure in brief

David Hume was born in Edinburgh in 1711, raised at Ninewells in the Scottish Borders, and educated — briefly, and without a degree — at Edinburgh University before family expectations of a legal career collapsed against what he called his "insurmountable aversion to everything but the pursuits of Philosophy and general Learning." In three cheap years in France he wrote, in his twenties, *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739–40), an attempt to run a complete science of man on Newton's experimental model: impressions and ideas, association, causation dissolved into custom, the self dissolved into a bundle of perceptions, morality rebuilt on sentiment. It failed in public — his own later verdict was that it "fell dead-born from the press" — and Hume spent the rest of his career recasting it into the clearer forms that made him famous: the two *Enquiries* (1748, 1751), the essays, and the *History of England* (1754–62) that made him financially independent. Blocked twice from university chairs by the religious opposition his skepticism provoked, he made his living and his reputation outside the academy — as tutor, military secretary, librarian, embassy secretary in Paris, where the salons adored him as "le bon David," and under-secretary of state in London. He died in Edinburgh on 25 August 1776, composed enough about his own death to unsettle his visitors, and the *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*, withheld throughout his life, appeared under his nephew's care in 1779. The *Treatise* he had recast and partly disowned is now widely regarded as the greatest work of philosophy in English; Kant testified that Hume had woken him from his "dogmatic slumber," and the problems Hume posed — induction, causation, personal identity, the is–ought gap, the evidential standing of miracles — still set the agenda they were posed in.

## Why he rewards weeks of study

First, *method*: Hume is the most instructive arguer in the early-modern canon to reconstruct line by line, because his arguments are short, exact, and honest about their own limits — the problem of induction, the two definitions of cause, the bundle theory and its Appendix confession of failure teach more about how philosophical arguments actually work, and how to concede what they do not do, than any survey of conclusions. Second, *position*: Stage 3 turns on him. Locke and Berkeley are his materials, Reid his first systematic respondent, Adam Smith his closest reader, and Kant's critical philosophy is unintelligible except as an answer to him; the induction thread that runs forward through Popper and Quine starts in *Enquiry* §IV. Third, *the recasting problem*: uniquely among the library's core figures, Hume published his system twice — once as a young man's difficult masterpiece, once as a mature man's polished *Enquiries* — and the scholarly argument about which Hume is the real one (and what the recasting dropped, softened, or sharpened, personal identity and the Appendix among it) is an argument the reader can conduct from the two texts themselves.

## The study plan at a glance

The library's [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Hume in **Stage 3** (the long early-modern stage), week 10, as a *core* figure — reached after Locke and Berkeley, whose materials he inherits, and ahead of the Kant block that answers him two rows later (Rousseau at row 11 and the Smith group at row 12 intervene); the stage's gate essay ("What, if anything, justifies our expectation that the future will resemble the past?") is his problem, with Kant's reply required. This dossier keeps the path's fixed order and deepens it rather than replacing it: the *Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* **whole** first, then the *Dialogues*, then selections from the *Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals*, and only last the *Treatise* I.iv selections — starting with the *Treatise* is the classic error, and Hume himself superseded it with the *Enquiries* for exactly the reader this plan serves. [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md) lays out a **7-week plan, ~45 hours all-in** at the learning path's weekly rhythm, with the plan file itself carrying the audited week-by-week total, including the daily card queue and assigned media. It runs inside Stage 3 at the Hume block; Kant, whose Stage 3 block follows two rows later, reads throughout as Hume's greatest respondent, and the dossier is built to hand you over to him.
