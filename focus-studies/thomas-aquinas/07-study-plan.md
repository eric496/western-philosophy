## What this plan is, and where it plugs in

The [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Aquinas in **Stage 2 (Medieval & Renaissance Bridge)** as a core figure (●) at row 5, after Augustine (row 1), Boethius (row 2), Anselm (row 3) and the Islamic and Jewish bridge figures (row 4). Its block is ST Ia Q2 (the Five Ways), Q3–11 in selection (simplicity, perfection), Ia-IIae Q90–97 in selection (natural law) and *On Being and Essence* in selection. Its editions are Davies & Leftow, *Questions on God* (Cambridge Texts), with McDermott's *Concise Translation* for orientation, and its rule is plain: **not** the full *Summa* in the old Dominican translation. It sets Feser's *Aquinas: A Beginner's Guide* alongside the block, names Aquinas's form (objections, *sed contra*, *respondeo*, replies) as the stage's known stall point, and warns that reading the *Summa* cover to cover means you have left the plan. This dossier keeps those editions, that companion and that warning, and widens the block to eight weeks. The path's texts stay at the centre: Q2 in Week 2, *De ente* and Q3 in Week 3, Q4–11 in Week 4, Q90–97 in Week 7. Five additions surround them: the method of Q1 (Week 1), creation, the eternity of the world and evil (Week 4), the soul and knowledge (Week 5), happiness and virtue (Week 6), and faith and reason with the Paris condemnations (Week 8). [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), "The works in the dossier's reading order," gives the reasons week by week.

Run it as one block in place of Stage 2's Aquinas row, after row 4. Week 2's checkpoints assume you have met [Avicenna](../../eras/02-medieval/avicenna.md), whose argument from the possible and the necessary lies behind the Third Way, and Week 5 assumes [Averroes](../../eras/02-medieval/averroes.md). Row 6 follows: [Duns Scotus](../../eras/02-medieval/john-duns-scotus.md)'s univocity of being answers Week 3's analogy and his account of the will answers Week 6's happiness, and [Ockham](../../eras/02-medieval/william-of-ockham.md) at row 7 takes apart much of Week 3's metaphysics.

**Total cost, stated honestly: ≈45 hours all-in across eight weeks: 34.5 hours at the desk, ≈3.5 hours of assigned media, and ≈7.5 hours of the daily [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) [FSRS](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) queue (about 8 minutes a day for 56 days, 448 minutes).** The table rounds each week's 56 minutes of cards to 0.9 hours, so its columns sum to 34.5 + 3.5 + 7.2 = 45.2. With the cards unrounded (448 minutes, 7.47 hours) and the media in exact minutes (208 minutes, 3.47 hours), the total is 34.5 + 3.47 + 7.47 = 45.4. Weekly totals run from 4.8 to 6.4 hours. Optional items are not counted. Every running time comes from [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md), which took each from the show's own feed or page: Adamson eps. 243–248 at 22 + 20 + 21 + 24 + 22 + 28 = 137 minutes, *Philosophize This!* ep. 21 at 26, and the *In Our Time* programme at 45, so 137 + 26 + 45 = 208. The Wi-Phi videos have no stated lengths; the path prices them at about 40 minutes and puts them inside desk hours, and this plan does the same.

**Card time is an estimate tied to deck size.** The path's daily queue is about 10 minutes, about six days a week, for the whole cumulative deck (§2). This plan counts 8 minutes a day, 56 minutes a week, as the share that this dossier's cards add; that is the house rate used for the 34-card [Augustine deck](../augustine-of-hippo/07-study-plan.md), kept here for a deck of **41 cards** (16 argument cards, one per argument in [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), and 25 concept cards keyed to [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md)). At a different rate, card hours are rate × 56 days ÷ 60.

**The accounting against the path, stated plainly: these hours sit on top of Stage 2's budget (8 weeks, 36 desk hours, ≈62 all-in), they are not hidden inside it, and Stage 2 is the path's compressed stage.** The path says of it: do not skip it, and do not expand it. It fixes no week count for the Aquinas row. Its 12-month trim (§10) keeps Augustine, Anselm and Aquinas in primary text inside 24 desk hours, about 8 each, so this dossier estimates the row at about 8 desk hours, roughly two weeks at the stage's rate of 4.5 a week; that is this dossier's estimate, not the path's figure. Most of the media is already in the path. Adamson's Aquinas block, eps. 243–248 (137 minutes, 2.3 hours), is named in the path's Stage 2 audio spine, inside its curated ≈18 hours, so it is not new cost. Wi-Phi is the path's own watch-before. *Philosophize This!* ep. 21 and the *In Our Time* programme (71 minutes, 1.2 hours) are new. Take away the row's desk time (≈8), the 2.3 media hours already in Stage 2 and the row's share of the card queue (about 2 hours, an estimate), and the net addition to Stage 2 is 45.4 − 8 − 2.3 − 2 ≈ 33 hours, again an estimate. Stage 2 would cost about 95 hours all-in instead of ≈62. If you also run the [Augustine dossier](../augustine-of-hippo/README.md) in the same stage, its own plan adds roughly 29 more, for about 124.

The weeks matter as much as the hours. The path's stage discipline (§1.4) lets you extend a stage by at most two weeks. Eight weeks in place of a two-week row extends Stage 2 by about six, which breaks that rule. Choose deliberately. (a) Run the whole plan in the comfortable version (Calendar B, §10), where extra weeks are expected. (b) Run Weeks 1–4, method and God (17.5 desk hours, 21.9 all-in by the table), as the row, which keeps the extension near two weeks and covers the path's exit checkpoint on the First and Third Ways and most of the material of its gate essay; take Weeks 5–8 (17 desk hours, 23.3 all-in) after Stage 2's gate. Count those four weeks as new time on Calendar B, not as buffer weeks: §10 reserves buffer weeks for illness, holidays, two named re-reading passes and media spill. The plan splits cleanly there: Weeks 1–4 are God, Weeks 5–8 the human being. Option (b) postpones Q90–97, which the path sets in the row. If you want the row to keep it, run Weeks 2, 3, 4 and 7 as the row (18 desk hours, 23.5 all-in), read Q1 a1, a2 and a8 with the [study guide](../../study/aquinas-summa.md)'s edition note in Week 2's Session B (about an hour more), and take Weeks 1, 5, 6 and 8 later. Week 7 then comes before Week 6's account of the last end, which Q90 a2 and Q94 a2 assume; read Ia-IIae Q1 a1 and Q3 a8 in McDermott first (half an hour).

**Reading texts.** Follow the path and [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md), section 1. ST Ia Q2–26 (Weeks 2–4) in Davies & Leftow; check its contents for which articles of Q19 and Q25 it prints. The rest of the ST (Weeks 1 and 5–8) in McDermott, which abridges into continuous prose: use it to see the shape of an argument, and check every premise you reconstruct against a full text. For that check, use Freddoso's free [online translation](https://freddoso.com/summa-translation/TOC.htm) of the whole ST, or the 1920 English Dominican text at [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/) for a single article. *On Being and Essence* in Peter King's translation (*The Hackett Aquinas*, 2014) or Maurer's. The *Summa contra Gentiles* in Pegis and others (Notre Dame). *On the Unity of the Intellect* in McInerny (Purdue, 1993). *On the Eternity of the World* in Vollert, Kendzierski & Byrne (Marquette, 1964), which prints Bonaventure's and Siger of Brabant's texts beside Aquinas's. The dossier quotes the ST in the 1920 translation of the Fathers of the English Dominican Province because it is public domain and can be checked word for word, not because you should read it at the desk; the path's rule stands. *De ente*, *De unitate* and *De aeternitate* are quoted only in Latin from [Corpus Thomisticum](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/), with the dossier's own gloss.

**Cite by part, question and article, and budget by article.** ST Ia Q2 a3 is the First Part, question 2, article 3; "ad 2" is the reply to the second objection; Ia-IIae is the first half of the Second Part, IIa-IIae the second half. The SCG goes by book and chapter (SCG I.13), the short treatises by chapter (*De ente* ch. 4, in the numbering of the modern translations; Corpus Thomisticum calls it "cap. 3"). Every edition above prints these divisions, but editions paginate differently and McDermott abridges, so each week's target is given as questions and articles plus a session length, not as a page count. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), "A note on citation," explains the system.

**The guide is your companion, by design.** The library's [study guide to the *Summa* selections](../../study/aquinas-summa.md), with its edition note on the article form, its question-by-question sections on Ia Q2 and Ia-IIae Q90–97, four reconstructions and fifteen checkpoints with answers, is not duplicated here. Weeks 1, 2 and 7 assign its sections, reconstructions (a)–(d), checkpoints and misreadings by number; the checkpoints printed in those weeks are the dossier's own additions. The guide covers only Q2 and the law treatise, so this plan supplies Weeks 3–6 and 8 itself. Its own five-week reading plan is folded into Weeks 2 and 7 here.

## Week 1 — Method and setting: ST Ia Q1 (~4.8 hrs: 3.5 desk + 0.4 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** The ST Prologue and Ia Q1, about an hour and a half; the plan's lightest week of text, because the form is the thing to learn. The Prologue is half a page: a book for beginners, ordered by the subject matter and not by a commented text. Then Q1's ten articles. Read four closely, objections and replies included: a1, why a teaching revealed by God is needed besides philosophy; a2, whether sacred doctrine is a science, with the comparison to optics taking its principles from geometry; a8, whether it argues, with ad 2, the sentence that grace does not destroy nature but perfects it; and a10, the senses of Scripture. Skim a3–7 and a9 for their questions only. Read a8 whole twice: once body first (the *respondeo*), as the SEP's author advises for a first reading, then in printed order. In McDermott, check a1 and a8 against a full text (Freddoso or New Advent), since the abridgement drops the objections.

**The guide.** Its "Edition note," on the four parts of an article, and "How to use this guide," before Session A. Nothing else from the guide this week.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), from "Roccasecca and Monte Cassino, c. 1225–1239" through "Master of theology and the mendicant controversy, 1256–1259," with "Weighing the sources"; [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), "The forms Aquinas wrote in," the *Summa Theologiae* entry and "A note on citation"; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "The order of the system" and "Sacred doctrine and philosophy"; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Aristotle," "Albert the Great," "The secular masters: William of Saint-Amour" and "Bonaventure." The entry on [Aristotle](../../eras/01-ancient/aristotle.md) as a refresher on act and potency and the four causes, which Week 2 assumes; the [Aristotle dossier](../aristotle/README.md) if you ran it in Stage 1. In Session B, Feser's opening chapter, and the SEP's ["Albert the Great"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/albert-great/) if you want Aquinas's teacher in more than a paragraph.

**Media.** *Preview, before the reading:* [*Philosophize This!*](https://philosophizethis.libsyn.com/episode-021-one-god-st-thomas-aquinas), ep. 21, "One God - St. Thomas Aquinas" (26 minutes), an orientation from a popularizer, not an academic; its weight falls on Aristotle's unmoved mover and on creation and an eternal world, so it previews Weeks 2 and 4 as well. *Optional, not counted:* Adamson, ep. 222, ["Rediscovery Channel - Translations into Latin"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-222-rediscovery-channel-translations-into-latin/) (24 minutes), and ep. 223, ["Straw Men - The Rise of the Universities"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-223-straw-men-the-rise-of-the-universities/) (22 minutes); eps. 240–241 on Albert the Great (20 and 19 minutes). *In Our Time*'s ["Greyfriars and Blackfriars"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p003k9dz) (45 minutes) is optional only in place of the Week 7 programme, since the path caps *In Our Time* at one per figure.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *Why does Aquinas think a teaching beyond philosophy is needed, and what are his two reasons?* Human beings are directed to an end, the vision of God, that exceeds reason, and one cannot steer toward an end one does not know. And the truths about God that reason can reach would, left to reason, be known by few, after a long time, and with errors mixed in (Q1 a1). So truths about God come in two kinds, and the second kind are later called the preambles of faith. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Sacred doctrine and philosophy."
2. *How can sacred doctrine be a science if its principles are not self-evident?* In the Aristotelian sense a science reasons from principles to conclusions, and some sciences take their principles from a higher one, as optics from geometry and music from arithmetic. Sacred doctrine takes its principles from the knowledge of God and the blessed (Q1 a2). Then say what this claim does not mean: that theology is an empirical science, or that its principles can be demonstrated. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Sacred doctrine and philosophy."
3. *Name the four parts of an article, say which part holds Aquinas's own argument, and say why the objections are not straw men.* Objections, *sed contra*, *respondeo*, replies; the argument lives in the *respondeo*, and the replies show which objections fail and why. The objections are the strongest reasons against his view, often from Augustine or Aristotle, and some (Q2 a3 obj. 2) are the modern objection stated in advance. The form comes from the university disputation, but the written *Summa* is a composed text laid out as a debate, not a transcript. The guide's "Edition note"; [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), "The forms Aquinas wrote in."
4. *What was the new Aristotle, and why was it dangerous in 1250?* Works lost to Latin readers for centuries, arriving in the previous hundred years from Arabic and Greek with the commentaries of Avicenna and Averroes. Three teachings troubled Christian readers: no providence over human affairs, an eternal world, a mortal soul. Say which later week answers each (Weeks 4, 4 and 5). Then say what the mendicant controversy was about and why it mattered to a Dominican master. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), "Naples: Aristotle and the Dominicans, 1239–1244" and "Master of theology and the mendicant controversy, 1256–1259."
5. *Which stories about the young Thomas rest on the early lives, and what does the record support?* The family's seizure and confinement are well attested, though its length is reported as a year (SEP, IEP) or up to two (the early lives). The woman sent to his room, the angels' cord and the "dumb ox" saying come from the early biographies (Tocco, Gui, Calo) and the canonization testimony, written for or drawn from the cause, and cannot be checked. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), "The family's resistance, 1244–1245," "Paris and Cologne with Albert, 1245–1252" and "Weighing the sources."

**Reconstructions.** No numbered argument this week. Practise the form instead: write out Q1 a8 book-closed in four labelled parts, objection, *sed contra*, *respondeo*, reply, then check it. And put Q1 a1's two reasons in numbered premises. Both return as warm-ups in Week 8.

**Cards.** The first 3 cards of the dossier's deck: concept cards on sacred doctrine (*sacra doctrina*), the preambles of faith (*praeambula fidei*) and the disputed-question form of an article. Import after the week's reading, never before. If you use the library's shipped decks in [`study/cards/`](../../study/cards/README.md), suspend any Aquinas card these duplicate. Deck at 3.

## Week 2 — The Five Ways: ST Ia Q2; SCG I.13 (~5.8 hrs: 4.5 desk + 0.4 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** ST Ia Q2 a1–3 and SCG I.13, about three hours across Sessions A and B. Q2 is a few pages and the most compressed text in the plan. a1: whether God's existence is self-evident, with the distinction between self-evident in itself and self-evident to us. a2: whether it can be demonstrated, and only from effects, with ad 2 on taking the meaning of the word "God" as the middle term. a3: the Five Ways, with obj. 2 (nature explains everything without God) and its reply. Read a3 three times: once straight through; once Way by Way, writing each premise as you go; once with obj. 1 and 2 and the replies. In Davies & Leftow. Then SCG I.13, long and technical: Aristotle's two proofs from motion out of *Physics* VIII and *Metaphysics* XII, the assumptions Aquinas admits (eternal motion, self-moved heavens), and the shorter arguments at its end, from *Metaphysics* II (efficient causes) and from John of Damascus (governance). Read it in Pegis for the argument's shape, and do not try to master the *Physics* proofs; an hour is enough. Then Ia Q46 a2 ad 7 alone, a paragraph: the series *per se* and *per accidens*, which decides how premise 6 of the First Way is read.

**The guide.** Its sections on Ia q.2 a.1, a.2 and a.3; its reconstructions **(a)** the First Way, **(b)** the Third Way and **(c)** the Fifth Way, each written book-closed before you open it; its **checkpoints 1–7**; its misreadings 1–3. The library entry's "Signature arguments" reconstructs the First and Third Ways as a second check.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Act and potency" and "God as subsistent being: the Five Ways and simplicity" (its first two paragraphs); [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), the opening note on what the Ways reach, then Arguments 1–5; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Avicenna, al-Ghazali, Averroes, Maimonides," "Anselm," "Descartes, Leibniz and the cosmological argument" and "Hume and Kant." The entries on [Avicenna](../../eras/02-medieval/avicenna.md), for the Third Way's source, [Anselm](../../eras/02-medieval/anselm-of-canterbury.md), for the ontological argument a1 refuses, [David Hume](../../eras/03-early-modern/david-hume.md) and [Immanuel Kant](../../eras/03-early-modern/immanuel-kant.md); the library entry's study questions 1–4. Before the week, Kenny's *Aquinas* (Past Masters), one or two evenings. In Session B, Feser's chapter on natural theology, and the SEP's ["Cosmological Argument"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmological-argument/) for the current state of the per se regress dispute. Kenny's *The Five Ways* (1969) is the advanced check on your reconstructions, Way by Way, if you have it.

**Media.** *Watch-before, inside desk hours:* Wi-Phi, [*Cosmological Argument*](https://www.wi-phi.com/series/cosmological-argument/) (Timothy Yenter), Parts 1–2, the path's video patch; the series teaches the position, not Aquinas by name. *Review, after your reconstructions of Arguments 1 and 3:* Adamson, ep. 243, ["The Ox Heard Round the World - Thomas Aquinas"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-243-the-ox-heard-round-the-world-thomas-aquinas/) (22 minutes), on faith and reason and the Five Ways.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *Reconstruct the First and Third Ways, and state the objection Hume and Kant later press against cosmological arguments.* This is the path's Stage 2 exit checkpoint; answer it aloud, then check it against guide (a) and (b). First Way: things change; change is potency brought to act by something in act; nothing is in act and potency in the same respect, so whatever is moved is moved by another; a *per se* series of movers cannot be infinite; so there is a first mover moved by no other. Third Way: perishable things exist; what can fail to be at some time is not; if everything could fail to be, at some time nothing would have been, and nothing would be now; so something is necessary, and finally something necessary of itself. Hume: causation as a productive power and the principle that every contingent thing has a cause are not given in experience, and a collection explained member by member needs no further cause. Kant: the cosmological argument leans on the ontological argument when it identifies the necessary being with the most real being, and causal reasoning holds only within possible experience. Then build the strongest Thomist reply (the library entry's study question 3). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Arguments 1 and 3; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Hume and Kant."
2. *Does Newton's first law refute the First Way?* Inertia says a body in uniform motion needs no mover, which seems to falsify premise 4 for local motion. Reply: "motion" in the Way is any passage from potency to act, so what needs a cause is a potency being actualized now, not uniform velocity. Then give the stronger objection to premise 6, that an infinite series of movers leaves nothing unexplained (Oberle), and the per se reply that its members have causal power only on loan (Cameron's vicious regress). Say why "what moved God?" misfires. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 1.
3. *State the Hume-Edwards principle and say why it does or does not touch the Second Way.* If each member of a collection is explained by another, the whole is explained, and asking for a cause of the whole is a confusion (Hume, *Dialogues* part 9; Edwards; named by Rowe). Reply: it fits the series *per accidens*, which Aquinas grants may be infinite (the generation of a son, Q46 a2 ad 7), not the series *per se*; and Rowe himself rejected the principle. Name the critics who doubt there are per se series (Sobel, Oppy). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 2.
4. *What is the quantifier shift in the Third Way, and what are the three defences?* From *each perishable thing at some time is not* it does not follow that *at some time no perishable thing is* (Plantinga, Kenny, Rowe). Defences: (a) the temporal reading with its principle that an unrealized possibility over infinite time is no real possibility; (b) Haldane's atemporal reading; (c) concede steps 1–5 and run step 6 alone. Then give Kenny's point about what "necessary" means here and Rundle's matter-energy objection, with the reply from step 6. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 3.
5. *Why is the Fifth Way not Paley's design argument, and how does Aquinas answer the Darwinian objection in advance?* Paley argues from complex contrivances treated as artifacts; Aquinas from any natural thing's steady tendency to a definite result, Aristotle's final cause. Q2 a3 obj. 2 already says nature explains everything; ad 2 replies that nature's own directedness is what needs explaining. Then state the critics' answer, that "acts for an end" is a metaphor for regularity, and say on what the dispute turns. Add one sentence on the Fourth Way: why Dawkins's parody, a supreme smelly being inferred from degrees of smelliness, misses an argument about transcendentals. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Arguments 4 and 5.

**Reconstructions.** The First and Third Ways (Arguments 1 and 3; guide (a) and (b) are the check, not the source), before Adamson ep. 243. Then the Second, Fourth and Fifth (Arguments 2, 4, 5) in shorter form; guide (c) is the check for the Fifth. Five arguments is the heaviest first-reconstruction load in the plan, so give Arguments 1 and 3 full premises and the other three their premise skeletons. Arguments 1 and 3 get their +2-day touches in Session C; Week 1's Q1 a8 gets its +1-week touch.

## Week 3 — Being and essence: *De ente* ch. 4; ST Ia Q3, Q13 (~5.9 hrs: 5 desk + 0 media + 0.9 cards)

Then the dispute: Patt, Kenny and Long take stage one to aim at a real distinction; Wippel says stage two settles it; Owens says only stage three does, and charges Wippel with an ontological-argument move. 2. *Give the two objections to the real distinction, and Aquinas's resources against the second.* (a) The phoenix shows only that two concepts differ, not two things (the morning and evening star). (b) The Fregean objection: existence is not an ingredient of anything; "phoenixes do not exist" says nothing is a phoenix. Name a later scholastic who also denied the real distinction ([Suárez](../../eras/03-early-modern/suarez-and-grotius.md)). 3. *How does Q3 reach simplicity, and what is Plantinga's objection?* By removal: not a body (a1), not matter and form (a2), not nature and individual (a3), essence is existence (a4), in no genus (a5), no accidents (a6); and every composite is posterior to its parts and has a cause, but God is first and uncaused (a7). Plantinga: if God is identical with each property and the properties with each other, God is a property, abstract and inert. Replies: the objection assumes properties as abstract objects; the truthmaker reading (Bergmann and Brower); and Q13 a4 on why the names are not synonyms. 4. *What is the modal-collapse objection, and what does Q19 a3 reply?* If God's act of creating is his necessary essence, he could not have failed to create, and everything exists necessarily (Mullins, Leftow). 5. *Why must the divine names be analogical, and what is Scotus's objection?* Not univocal, since a simple God has no perfection distinct from his essence and a creaturely concept circumscribes what it signifies; not purely equivocal, or nothing could be demonstrated of God from creatures, and the Five Ways would commit the fallacy of equivocation; so analogical, by proportion to one source, as "healthy" is said of animal, medicine and urine (Q13 a5). ## Week 7 — Law: Ia-IIae Q90–97; IIa-IIae Q40, Q64 a7 (~6.4 hrs: 4 desk + 1.5 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** The treatise on law in the study guide's order, about two and a half hours in McDermott, with Freddoso's *Treatise on Law* or the 1920 text as the full-text check for Q90 a4, Q91 a2 and Q94 a2. Q90, the essence of law (a1–4, ending in the definition). Q91, the kinds of law: eternal, natural, human, divine (a1–4). Q92 on the effects of law and Q93 on eternal law, which you may read at a steady pace but must not skip, since Q94 assumes them. Q94, natural law: a2 slowly, twice, on the first precept and the order of inclinations, then a4–6 on how far natural law is the same for all and can change. Q95 (human law; a2 on derivation by conclusion and by determination), Q96 (the power of human law; a2–4 on which vices it represses and when it binds in conscience, a6 on equity), Q97 (change in laws; a2). Then IIa-IIae Q40 a1, about fifteen minutes: whether it is always sinful to wage war, with the three conditions of the reply. Then Q64 a7, about fifteen minutes: whether it is lawful to kill a man in self-defence. Read Q64 a7 twice, the second time marking where "intention" and "proportion" do their work.

**The guide.** Its sections on Ia–IIae q.90–97; its reconstruction **(d)**, the derivation of natural law, written book-closed before you open it; its **checkpoints 8–15**; its misreadings 4–7. The guide's own reading plan gives these questions four weeks; this plan gives them one because the guide has already done the question-by-question work, so lean on it.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Law," and "Will, action and happiness" for double effect, with the key terms "Natural law" and "Synderesis"; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Arguments 15 and 16; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Cicero, the Stoics and the lawyers," "Hume and Kant" (for the is–ought objection), "Luther, the humanists and Hobbes," "Anscombe, Geach, Kenny and analytic Thomism" and "Grisez, Finnis and MacIntyre." The entries on [David Hume](../../eras/03-early-modern/david-hume.md), [Thomas Hobbes](../../eras/03-early-modern/thomas-hobbes.md), [Anscombe and Foot](../../eras/05-20th-century/anscombe-and-foot.md) and [Alasdair MacIntyre](../../eras/05-20th-century/alasdair-macintyre.md). If you ran the [Augustine dossier](../augustine-of-hippo/README.md), its Argument 14 on just war is what Q40 a1 builds on. In Session B, the SEP's ["The Natural Law Tradition in Ethics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-law-ethics/) for Argument 15 and ["Doctrine of Double Effect"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/double-effect/) for Argument 16; or the IEP's ["Aquinas: Political Philosophy"](https://iep.utm.edu/thomas-aquinas-political-philosophy/) and ["Natural Law"](https://iep.utm.edu/natlaw/). Finnis's *Aquinas* if you want the new natural law reading at length.

**Media.** *Review, after your reconstructions of Arguments 15 and 16:* Adamson, ep. 246, ["What Pleases the Prince - The Rule of Law"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-246-what-pleases-the-prince-the-rule-of-law/) (24 minutes), and ep. 247, ["Onward, Christian Soldiers - Just War Theory"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-247-onward-christian-soldiers-just-war-theory/) (22 minutes). Then, at the end of the week, as a review of Weeks 1–7: BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*, ["St Thomas Aquinas"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b00mkd63) (17 September 2009, 45 minutes, with Martin Palmer, John Haldane and Annabel Brett), whose page singles out natural law, human rights and just war. This is the path's one *In Our Time* for the figure. *Optional, not counted:* ["Just War"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p00545jx) (30 minutes), only in place of it.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *How does Q94 a2 reach the first precept, and what does it share with Q2 a1?* Natural law's precepts are to practical reason what the first principles of demonstration are to theoretical reason, self-evident. The distinction of Ia Q2 a1 returns: self-evident in itself, to all, or only to the wise. As being is the first thing theoretical reason grasps, and non-contradiction rests on it, good is the first thing practical reason grasps, since every agent acts for an end under the aspect of good; so the first precept is that good is to be done and pursued and evil avoided. Then the three levels of inclination and what reason does with them (guide checkpoint 11 is the check). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 15.
2. *State Hume's Law against Q94 a2, and the two Thomist answers.* From facts about inclination to life, offspring, knowledge and society, the article seems to infer goods and precepts, an "is" to an "ought" (*Treatise* III.1.1); and an order of precepts by order of inclinations seems to rank bare survival first. New natural law (Grisez 1965, Finnis 1980): the first precept is a principle of intelligible action, and the basic goods are self-evident to practical reason, not inferred, and not ranked. The older line (McInerny, Hittinger): this cuts natural law off from the metaphysics of nature and eternal law that Q91 builds. Say which reading the text of Q94 a2 better fits. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 15.
3. *What does Q64 a7 permit, and on what conditions?* A private person may use force that kills an attacker if the killing is beside the intention, the intention is to save one's own life, and the force is proportionate; one act can have two effects, and moral acts take their species from what is intended. A private person may not intend to kill, since that belongs to public authority (Q64 a3). Then say how this connects to the first inclination of Q94 a2. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 16.
4. *Does Q64 a7 state the principle of double effect, and what is the problem of closeness?* One reading (Mangan, the manual tradition, Grisez 1970) finds the full principle: the attacker's death lies outside the intention even when foreseen as certain. Another takes Aquinas's "intention" to bear chiefly on the end, so the article is silent on lethal means. Closeness: it is hard to tell a foreseen side effect from a harm so close to the means that it must be intended (the hysterectomy case against abortion). Anscombe defends the line as real; consequentialists deny it matters. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 16.
5. *State the three conditions of a just war in Q40 a1, and say what each excludes.* The authority of the sovereign, since a private person can seek redress from a superior and has no right to summon the people to war; a just cause, that those attacked deserve it for some fault; and a right intention, to advance good or avoid evil, so that a war with lawful authority and just cause can still be made unlawful by a wicked intention. Say which of the three Aquinas takes from Augustine. No dossier file reconstructs Q40; check your answer against the article itself and Adamson ep. 247.

**Reconstructions.** The first precept and its order (Argument 15; guide (d) is the check) and double effect in self-defence (Argument 16), before Adamson eps. 246 and 247. Arguments 9 and 10 get their +3-week touches; Argument 14 its +2-day and +1-week touches.

**Cards.** +4: the argument cards for Arguments 15 and 16, and concept cards on natural law (*lex naturalis*) and synderesis. Suspend the shipped core card on natural law if you imported it; the shipped checkpoint cards duplicate the guide's checkpoints, so keep one set or the other. Deck at 40.

## Week 8 — Faith and reason; the capstone: SCG I.3–8; IIa-IIae Q1–2; the condemnations (~5.9 hrs: 5 desk incl. the essay + 0 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** About an hour and a half of reading, then the capstone essay, about two and a half hours. SCG I.3–8 first, about forty minutes in Pegis: the two modes of truth about God, one that reason can reach and one that surpasses it (I.3); why even the first is fittingly proposed for belief, since by reason alone it would be known by few, slowly and with error (I.4, which repeats ST Ia Q1 a1); why the second is fittingly proposed too (I.5); why assenting to it is not light-minded (I.6); why the truth of reason is not contrary to the truth of faith (I.7); and how reason stands to the truth of faith, by likenesses that are not demonstrations (I.8). Then IIa-IIae Q1 and Q2 in selection, about forty minutes in McDermott with a full-text check: Q1 a1 (the object of faith is the first truth), a4 (faith is of things not seen) and a5 (whether what is of faith can also be known by demonstration, and by whom); Q2 a1–2 (what believing is), a4 (why truths reason can prove must still be believed), a9 (the definition of the act of faith) and a10 (whether reasons for what is believed lessen the merit of faith). Then the condemnations, about ten minutes in the dossier: no primary text is set, since the 1270 and 1277 lists are read here through [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) and [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), "Paris again: Averroists and Franciscans, 1268–1272," "The silence of December 1273," "After the death: 1277, 1323, 1325" and "Weighing the sources," and the "Chronology" as a self-test; [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), the *Summa contra Gentiles* entry; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Sacred doctrine and philosophy" again, "Grace perfects nature" and "How the pieces interlock," the last as the week's self-test, with all five tensions; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Siger of Brabant and the Latin Averroists," "The Franciscan theologians: Pecham, Kilwardby, William de la Mare," "William of Ockham" and "The themes trace"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) in full, as capstone raw material, above all "1277 and the correctorium dispute," "Live debates" and "Misconceptions, in full form"; [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md) for what to read next. In Session B, the SEP's ["Condemnation of 1277"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/condemnation/) and the faith-and-reason sections of ["Medieval Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/medieval-philosophy/).

**Media.** None assigned; the week's time goes to the essay. *Optional, after the capstone, not counted:* Adamson, ep. 249, ["Paris When it Sizzles - the Condemnations"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-249-paris-when-it-sizzles-the-condemnations/) (21 minutes), and ep. 251, ["Masters of the University - 'Latin Averroism'"](https://hopwag.podbean.com/e/hop-251-masters-of-the-university-%e2%80%9clatin-averroism/) (23 minutes), on Siger of Brabant and Boethius of Dacia. If you take them, they add 44 minutes (0.7 hours) to the total.

**Checkpoints (book closed).**
1. *What are the two modes of truth about God, and why is it fitting that even the provable truths be believed?* Some truths surpass reason, as that God is three and one; others reason can reach, as that God exists and is one, and the philosophers demonstrated them (SCG I.3). Left to reason, the second kind would be known by few, after long study, and with errors mixed in, so they are fittingly proposed for belief as well (SCG I.4; ST Ia Q1 a1). Then say which kind the Five Ways belong to, and what that makes of them. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Sacred doctrine and philosophy."
2. *Can faith and reason conflict, and what follows for an argument against the faith?* No: both come from the same God, so the truth of reason cannot be contrary to the truth of faith (SCG I.7). So an argument against the faith is not a demonstration but a difficulty that can be answered (ST Ia Q1 a8), and reason's arguments for the articles are likenesses, not proofs (SCG I.8). Then say how the Week 4 verdict on the eternity of the world applies this rule from both sides. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Sacred doctrine and philosophy."
3. *What is the act of faith, and can one person know and believe the same thing at once?* An act of the intellect assenting to divine truth at the command of the will moved by grace (IIa-IIae Q2 a9): neither feeling nor demonstration. The same thing cannot be both seen and believed by the same person in the same respect, but what one person knows by demonstration, such as that God exists, another may believe (Q1 a5, Q2 a4). Reasons that come before the will to believe lessen merit; reasons sought afterward, out of love for what is believed, do not (Q2 a10). [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Grace perfects nature."
4. *What was condemned in 1270 and 1277, and how far did it touch Aquinas?* 1270: Tempier condemned thirteen propositions, among them the unity of the intellect and the eternity of the world by the usual account. 1277: 219 propositions at Paris, mostly aimed at the arts masters; Wippel holds that some come from Aquinas's writings, Hissette found fewer; Wielockx argued for a separate process against Aquinas, stopped by the curia, which Wippel and Thijssen have questioned. At Oxford Kilwardby condemned the unity of substantial form and prime matter as pure potency. In 1325 the bishop of Paris declared the condemnation not to apply to Aquinas's teaching, without endorsing it. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), "After the death: 1277, 1323, 1325"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md), "1277 and the correctorium dispute."
5. *What, on Aquinas's view, can reason prove about God unaided?* That God exists, from effects (Week 2); what God is not, by removal, and that God is simple, perfect, infinite, unchanging, eternal and one (Weeks 3–4); and what the names mean, by analogy (Week 3). Not the Trinity, not the Incarnation, and not that the world began (Week 4). Then say where Anselm would disagree (an argument from the concept of God, which Q2 a1 refuses) and where Maimonides would (the names say only what God is not, which Q13 a2 refuses). This is the path's Stage 2 gate question; answer it aloud. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "How the pieces interlock"; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Anselm" and "Avicenna, al-Ghazali, Averroes, Maimonides."

**Reconstructions.** No new numbered argument; the capstone is this week's long reconstruction, and Week 1's Q1 a8 returns as its warm-up, set beside SCG I.7. Arguments 11–13 get their +3-week touches; Arguments 15 and 16 their +2-day and +1-week touches. Do the touches before the essay.

**Cards.** +1: the concept card on grace (*gratia*), with the rule that grace perfects nature. ## Capstone essay (800–1,200 words)

Write in Week 8, after the checkpoints, book closed for the first draft. **The gate relationship, stated exactly:** Stage 2's gate essay asks what reason can prove about God unaided, everything, something or nothing, with Anselm against Aquinas against one of the bridge figures. Anselm (row 3) and the bridge figures (row 4) come before Aquinas on the path, so by Week 8 you have all three. **Prompt 1 is the gate essay**: write it to the gate's terms, then cut a 600–800-word version, the path's gate length, and it serves as the stage's gate essay, not as an extra. **Prompt 2 is built to pass the path's Stage 2 exit checkpoint on the First and Third Ways.** Each prompt draws on at least three weeks.

1. **What can reason prove about God unaided? (the gate essay).** Reconstruct what Aquinas thinks reason can reach: the Five Ways (Arguments 1–5), simplicity (Argument 7) and analogy (Argument 8), and the line SCG I.3 draws between the two modes of truth. Set Anselm's *Proslogion* argument, with Gaunilo, against Q2 a1's reply that "God exists" is self-evident in itself but not to us. Take one bridge figure: Maimonides on negative theology against Q13 a2, or Avicenna on the necessary being behind the Third Way. Then answer the gate's question for yourself: everything, something or nothing.
2. **Do the First and Third Ways survive Hume and Kant? (the exit-checkpoint prompt).** Reconstruct both Ways with the per se series of Q46 a2 ad 7. State the quantifier-shift objection to the Third Way, the Hume-Edwards principle, and Kant's charge that the cosmological argument rests on the ontological. Judge whether the per se reply (Cameron) or Haldane's atemporal reading answers them, or only moves the question to whether there are per se series in nature (Sobel, Oppy).
3. **Does the whole system rest on *esse*?** Reconstruct the real distinction (Argument 6) and say how simplicity (Argument 7), the Fourth Way's participation (Argument 4) and analogy (Argument 8) depend on it. Weigh the Fregean objection and Kenny's doubts against Wippel's defence. If existence is not a real act distinct from essence, what of the system is left?
4. **One human being: soul, intellect and the 1277 condemnations.** Reconstruct the soul as form and the unity of form (Argument 11), the case against one intellect (Argument 12) and knowledge through phantasms (Argument 13). Say what the pluralists and Averroes each saw that Aquinas denied, and whether the soul that is both form and subsistent (tension (4)) is a coherent middle between Averroes and Descartes. Use the Kilwardby condemnation and the correctorium dispute.
5. **From happiness to law: is natural law an "ought" from an "is"?** Reconstruct happiness as the vision of God (Argument 14) and the first precept of natural law (Argument 15), and say how the ordering to one last end shapes Q94 a2. Set the new natural law reading (Grisez, Finnis) against the older Thomist line (McInerny, Hittinger) as answers to Hume's Law, and test your answer on double effect (Argument 16).
