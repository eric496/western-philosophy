# Bibliography — Thomas Aquinas
**English** | [简体中文](../../zh/focus-studies/thomas-aquinas/08-bibliography.md)

Annotated and tiered: read downward only as far as your questions require. No prices are given. The publisher pages that would show them (Cambridge, Oxford, Oneworld, Hackett) did not answer this check (November 2026), so publishers and years below were taken from a library catalogue (Open Library) or from the bibliographies of the SEP and IEP entries, and are marked where the two disagree. Page counts are given only where the catalogue record states them. One fact governs the whole list: Aquinas is cited by **part, question and article** for the *Summa Theologiae* (ST Ia Q2 a3), by **book and chapter** for the *Summa contra Gentiles* (SCG I.13), and by title and chapter for the short treatises. Every modern translation prints these divisions, so any edition below will find every reference in this dossier. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md) explains the system and gives the edition rulings this list follows.

## 1. Primary texts — the editions the plan uses

**Brian Davies & Brian Leftow (eds.), *Aquinas: Summa Theologiae, Questions on God* (Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy; Cambridge University Press, 2006; 344 pp.).** The learning path's ruling for the questions on God, Weeks 2–4. Cambridge's own description, seen in a search result, says it gives most of the first 26 questions of the *Summa*, including all of those on God's existence and nature; the Cambridge page itself refused this check. The introduction explains the article form, which is the path's known stall point. Check its contents for which articles of Q19 and Q25 it prints.

**Timothy McDermott (ed.), *Summa Theologiae: A Concise Translation* (1989).** The path's ruling for orientation in the rest of the ST: Weeks 1 and 5–8. The catalogue record checked is a Christian Classics printing of 1991 (651 pp.). It abridges the whole *Summa* into continuous prose, so it shows the shape of an argument and hides its premises. Check every premise you reconstruct against a full text.

**Full texts for checking.** The dossier quotes the ST only in the 1920 translation of the Fathers of the English Dominican Province, free at [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/). It quotes that translation because it is public domain and can be checked word for word, not because you should read it at the desk: the path's rule is "**Not** the full *Summa* in the old Dominican translation". For a modern full text, Alfred J. Freddoso (Notre Dame, emeritus) has put [his own translation of the whole ST](https://freddoso.com/summa-translation/TOC.htm) online free. His page reports a complete draft of all 502 questions, updated 26 October 2026, and the SEP calls it the best full English translation. Earlier versions of his *Treatise on Law* (Ia-IIae Q90–108) and *Treatise on Human Nature* (Ia Q75–102) were published by St. Augustine's Press in 2009 and 2010. Use it to check a passage you are reading in McDermott, Weeks 5 and 7 especially.

**Edward Feser, *Aquinas: A Beginner's Guide* (Oneworld, 2009; 224 pp.).** The path's companion, read alongside the whole block; listed here because the path rules it with the texts. Its chapters on metaphysics and natural theology go with Weeks 2–4, the chapter on psychology with Week 5. Feser is a Thomist arguing for Aquinas, not a neutral surveyor; the IEP calls the book more sophisticated than its title suggests. Read Kenny (below) against it.

**The dossier's rulings where the path is silent** (reasons in [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md)):

- ***On Being and Essence*, Week 3.** Peter King's translation in Jeffrey Hause & Robert Pasnau (eds.), *The Hackett Aquinas: Basic Works* (Hackett, 2014). The same volume has *On the Principles of Nature*, translated by Eleonore Stump & Stephen Chanderbhan, the short primer the SEP recommends to novices. Armand Maurer's translation (Pontifical Institute of Mediaeval Studies; first published 1949, later revised) is the older standard.
- ***Summa contra Gentiles*, Weeks 2 and 8.** Anton C. Pegis and others (trans.), *Summa contra Gentiles* (University of Notre Dame Press; Book I is Pegis's). The catalogue check did not find this record; the date is as [02](02-works-and-texts.md) gives it. Free: the English Dominican translation of 1924, which the dossier quotes, on the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/summacontragenti01thomuoft) (Book I). The SEP names a parallel Latin–English text at Calibrenet as the best complete translation; it was not checked here.
- ***On the Unity of the Intellect*, Week 5.** Ralph McInerny, *Aquinas Against the Averroists: On There Being Only One Intellect* (Purdue University Press, 1993). Latin and English with an introduction and essays.
- ***On the Eternity of the World*, Week 4.** Cyril Vollert, Lottie H. Kendzierski & Paul M. Byrne (trans.), *On the Eternity of the World* (Marquette University Press, 1964). It prints Bonaventure's and Siger of Brabant's texts beside Aquinas's, which is the point: you hear all three sides of the dispute.

**Latin.** [Corpus Thomisticum](https://www.corpusthomisticum.org/) has the whole Latin corpus with the Index Thomisticus search engine. Each page names the edition it follows. The dossier quotes the Latin of the three short treatises from it.

**Avoid** reading the 1920 ST straight through; anthologies that print a Way without the objections and replies of its article; and Joseph Rickaby's *Of God and His Creatures* (1905), an abridged SCG, now that the full 1924 text is free. The SEP warns that the online Latin–English reader of the Aquinas Institute (Wyoming), though well built, often uses old and sometimes unreliable translations.

## 2. First secondary books, in order

1. **Feser** (above), alongside from Week 1.
2. **Anthony Kenny, *Aquinas* (Past Masters, Oxford University Press, 1980).** One or two evenings before Week 2. A short book by an analytic philosopher who is not a Thomist, and whose *Five Ways* and *Aquinas on Being* (below) are the best-known critiques of the arguments Feser defends. It is the counterweight to Feser that a beginner needs.
3. **Brian Davies, *The Thought of Thomas Aquinas* (Clarendon Press, 1992).** The companion the library entry names, read across Weeks 2–8. The IEP notes that it follows the order of the ST, so it can be read one week at a time. Davies is also an editor of the Cambridge volume above.
4. **Jean-Pierre Torrell, *Saint Thomas Aquinas*, Vol. 1: *The Person and His Work* (Catholic University of America Press).** The life, for [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md). The IEP cites Robert Royal's translation, revised edition 2005, as the most up-to-date scholarly life; the SEP cites a third edition, translated by Matthew K. Minerd & Royal (2022). Use the later one if you can. Its "Brief Catalogue" of the works is the reference for dates. J. A. Weisheipl, *Friar Thomas D'Aquino* (CUA Press, 1983), is the older life it replaced.
5. **Eleonore Stump, *Aquinas* (Routledge, Arguments of the Philosophers, 2003).** Weeks 3–6, when you want one analytic book on the whole system. The IEP describes it as setting out and defending Aquinas's views in the terms of current analytic debates in metaphysics, epistemology, philosophy of religion, philosophy of mind and ethics.

G. K. Chesterton's book on Aquinas, cited by the IEP as *The Dumb Ox* (first published 1933; Image Books, 1956), is a journalist's essay, not scholarship. The IEP reports that Étienne Gilson praised it highly. Read it, if at all, for pleasure. Its picture of the life repeats the legends that [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) flags.

## 3. Advanced and specialist, by area

Open these when a week's checkpoint question has narrowed to a debate. Most are academic books at academic prices, best borrowed.

- **The whole philosophy.** Norman Kretzmann & Eleonore Stump (eds.), *The Cambridge Companion to Aquinas* (Cambridge, 1993), and its successor, Eleonore Stump & Thomas Joseph White (eds.), *The New Cambridge Companion to Aquinas* (Cambridge, 2022), whose first chapter, by Dominic Legge, is a short life. Brian Davies & Eleonore Stump (eds.), *The Oxford Handbook of Aquinas* (Oxford, 2012), has a chapter on each topic, among them Timothy Pawl's on the Five Ways. Christopher Shields & Robert Pasnau, *The Philosophy of Aquinas* (2nd ed., Oxford, 2016), is a two-author survey by philosophers. Pasquale Porro, *Thomas Aquinas: A Historical and Philosophical Profile* (trans. Joseph G. Trabbic & Roger W. Nutt; CUA Press, 2016), which the SEP lists with Torrell for the biography.
- **The *Summa* as a book (Week 1).** Torrell, *Aquinas's Summa: Background, Structure, and Reception* (trans. Benedict M. Guevin; CUA Press, 2005). Jason Eberl, *The Routledge Guidebook to Aquinas' Summa Theologiae* (Routledge, 2015), is a close reading for the philosophy.
- **The Five Ways and natural theology (Weeks 2, 8).** Anthony Kenny, *The Five Ways* (Routledge, 1969): a short, hostile, exact analytic critique, Way by Way. Hold it against your reconstructions of Arguments 1–5, and against Feser's replies to it. Norman Kretzmann, *The Metaphysics of Theism: Aquinas's Natural Theology in Summa Contra Gentiles I* (Clarendon, 1997), a sympathetic reconstruction of the book behind Weeks 2 and 8.
- **Being, essence, simplicity, analogy (Weeks 3–4).** John F. Wippel, *The Metaphysical Thought of Thomas Aquinas: From Finite Being to Uncreated Being* (CUA Press, 2000): the standard large study, and the main defence of the real distinction (Argument 6). Anthony Kenny, *Aquinas on Being* (Oxford, 2002): a critical analytic study of Aquinas's doctrine of *esse*, from the side that doubts the real distinction is coherent. Read Wippel and Kenny as the two sides. Étienne Gilson, *The Christian Philosophy of St. Thomas Aquinas* (trans. L. K. Shook, 1956; University of Notre Dame Press reprint, 1994), is the classic case that *esse*, the act of being, is the key to the system. Ralph McInerny, *Aquinas and Analogy* (CUA Press, 1996), for Argument 8.
- **Soul and knowledge (Week 5).** Robert Pasnau, *Thomas Aquinas on Human Nature: A Philosophical Study of Summa theologiae 1a 75–89* (Cambridge, 2002; one catalogue record gives 2001): question by question through exactly the Week 5 texts, by the author of the current SEP entry. Anthony Kenny, *Aquinas on Mind* (Routledge, 1993), shorter and analytic. McInerny's *Against the Averroists* (above) for Argument 12.
- **Happiness, virtue, law (Weeks 6–7).** John Finnis, *Aquinas: Moral, Political, and Legal Theory* (Oxford, 1998), reads the natural law in the light of the theory of basic goods that Finnis developed with Germain Grisez, a reading other natural law theorists dispute. Read it with the SEP's natural law entry, which sets the Grisez and Finnis lists of basic goods beside other readings, for Argument 15. Scott MacDonald & Eleonore Stump (eds.), *Aquinas's Moral Theory* (Cornell, 1998), and Stephen J. Pope (ed.), *The Ethics of Aquinas* (Georgetown, 2002), are essay collections with a chapter per topic. Freddoso's *Treatise on Law* (above) gives the full text of Week 7.
- **Reception.** Fergus Kerr, *After Aquinas: Versions of Thomism* (Blackwell, 2002), on the rival twentieth-century Thomisms, for [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

## 4. Reference

- **[SEP, "Thomas Aquinas"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aquinas/)** — by Robert Pasnau (first published 7 December 2022). It replaced Ralph McInerny & John O'Callaghan's entry "Saint Thomas Aquinas", which survives in the [Fall 2022 archive](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2022/entries/aquinas/). Its list of works gives dates for each, and its bibliography sticks deliberately to English-language sources. Read its opening sections before Week 1 and the rest after, per the path's SEP rule. Satellite entries, by week:
  - ["Cosmological Argument"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmological-argument/) (Bruce Reichenbach; revised July 2026), Week 2.
  - ["Divine Simplicity"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-simplicity/) (William F. Vallicella) and ["Medieval Theories of Analogy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/analogy-medieval/) (E. Jennifer Ashworth & Domenic D'Ettore), Week 3.
  - ["Albert the Great"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/albert-great/) (Bruno Tremblay; first published November 2025), on Aquinas's teacher, for Weeks 1 and 5.
  - ["Aquinas' Moral, Political, and Legal Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aquinas-moral-political/) (John Finnis), ["The Natural Law Tradition in Ethics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/natural-law-ethics/) (Mark Murphy) and ["Doctrine of Double Effect"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/double-effect/) (Alison McIntyre), Weeks 6–7 and Arguments 15–16.
  - ["Condemnation of 1277"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/condemnation/) (Hans Thijssen) and ["Medieval Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/medieval-philosophy/) (John Marenbon), Week 8.

  Free; budget an hour or two per entry (an estimate). There is no SEP entry on Siger of Brabant or on Thomism at the obvious addresses.
- **[IEP, "Thomas Aquinas"](https://iep.utm.edu/thomas-aquinas/)** — by Christopher M. Brown: the plainer first pass, with an annotated list of introductions. Its companions: ["Aquinas: Metaphysics"](https://iep.utm.edu/thomas-aquinas-metaphysics/) (Gaven Kerr), Week 3; ["Aquinas' Philosophical Theology"](https://iep.utm.edu/thomas-aquinas-political-theology/) (Shawn Floyd; the web address says "political-theology", but the entry is the philosophical theology), Weeks 2–4; ["Aquinas: Political Philosophy"](https://iep.utm.edu/thomas-aquinas-political-philosophy/) (Peter Koritansky) and ["Natural Law"](https://iep.utm.edu/natlaw/) (Kenneth Einar Himma), Week 7. Free.
- **[PhilPapers, "Thomas Aquinas"](https://philpapers.org/browse/thomas-aquinas)** — the category for finding articles on one argument once a week's question has narrowed.
- **Translations.** The SEP's links point to Thérèse Bonin's bibliography *Thomas Aquinas in English*, a guide to every English translation. Look a work up there before buying a translation not named above. [Isidore.co](https://isidore.co/aquinas/) collects older English translations of many works. Treat both as finding aids, not reading texts.
- **Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*, Vol. 2, *Medieval Philosophy* (Oxford University Press; first published 2005; the catalogue record checked is the 2007 paperback, 352 pp.)** — the path's history spine for Stage 2, alongside throughout. Peter Adamson's *Medieval Philosophy* (Oxford University Press, 2019, per the catalogue) appears to be the book volume of the podcast's Latin-medieval run; its contents were not checked.

- **Lecture course.** No free academic lecture course on Aquinas was verified, which matches the path's finding for medieval philosophy as a whole. [Aquinas 101](https://aquinas101.thomisticinstitute.org/) is free (with an account) and offers short videos and 15+ courses. It is made by the Thomistic Institute of the Dominican House of Studies in Washington, D.C., which presents Aquinas as a guide to Catholic thought. Treat it as advocacy from inside the tradition, like Feser, and not as a neutral course. Lengths not stated.
