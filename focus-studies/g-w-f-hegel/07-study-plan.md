# Study Plan — Hegel in Nine Weeks

## What this plan is, and where it plugs in

The [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Hegel in **Stage 4 (Nineteenth Century)** at row 2, marked "● (excerpts only)": the *Phenomenology* Preface in selection and Lordship and Bondage (¶¶178–196) only, with the *Lectures on the Philosophy of History*, Introduction, in selection; Miller or Pinkard; Sadler's *Half Hour Hegel* for those two blocks only, about 3–4 hours inside desk hours; and the warning in bold, "Not the full *Phenomenology*, and never the *Science of Logic*, solo." The path calls this row the most common Stage 4 abandonment point and says the guide matters more than the translation. It sets the row after [Fichte](../../eras/04-19th-century/johann-gottlieb-fichte.md) (row 1, map level but "not skippable"), and before [Schopenhauer](../../eras/04-19th-century/arthur-schopenhauer.md) (row 3) and [Kierkegaard](../../eras/04-19th-century/soren-kierkegaard.md) (row 4), whose texts presuppose "the Hegel you just excerpted".

**How far beyond the path this plan goes, stated plainly.** The path's excerpts are about forty paragraphs of the *Phenomenology* and one lecture introduction. This plan keeps them and adds, in guided sections and never cover to cover: the rest of chapters I–IV, the Introduction (¶¶73–89), two sections of chapter VI, three short stretches of the *Encyclopaedia Logic*, about a third of the *Philosophy of Right*, and the absolute-spirit sections of the *Encyclopaedia*. That is several times the path's reading, at about six times its desk hours (43.5 against the row's estimated 7). It keeps the path's two prohibitions: no week reads the *Phenomenology* whole, and the *Science of Logic* is never set. There is no study guide to Hegel in this library, so the dossier does the guide's work: [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) (Arguments 1–8) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) (Arguments 9–16) supply the reconstructions, the checkpoints below the questions, and [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), "The works in the dossier's reading order," the reasons for each block. Write your own reconstruction, book closed, before you read 04's.

**A short route, for a reader who wants less.** Take the path's own excerpts plus Weeks 3 and 7: the Preface sessions of Week 1 (¶¶1–37 in selection, Arguments 1–2), all of Week 3 (¶¶166–196, Arguments 6–8, which contains the path's ¶¶178–196), all of Week 7 (the *Philosophy of Right*, Arguments 12–15), and the *Philosophy of History* Introduction from Week 8 (Argument 16). That is about 3.5 weeks and ≈21 hours all-in, priced under "The accounting" below. It covers every Hegel item in Stage 4's exit checkpoints and the whole political thread that Marx (row 8) answers, and it leaves out the consciousness chapters, the logic and absolute spirit. If the full plan stalls, fall back to the short route; that is the plan working, not failing, in the same spirit as the path's stated fallback for Heidegger.

Run the full plan as one block in place of row 2. It assumes [Kant](../../eras/03-early-modern/immanuel-kant.md) at Stage 3 depth (the [Kant dossier](../immanuel-kant/README.md), if you took it, supplies the universal-law test that Argument 12 attacks) and Fichte's entry read before Week 1, as the path's non-negotiable §1.3 requires: "Hegel after Kant (and Fichte at map level)." Kierkegaard (row 4) then takes up Weeks 1, 4 and 9, Marx (row 8) Weeks 3, 7 and 9, and Mill (row 6) is the liberal answer to Week 7.

**Total cost, stated honestly: ≈55 hours all-in across nine weeks: 43.5 hours at the desk, ≈3.2 hours of assigned media, and ≈8.4 hours of the daily [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) [FSRS](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) queue (about 8 minutes a day for 63 days, 504 minutes).** The table rounds each week's 56 minutes of cards to 0.9 hours and each week's media to one decimal, so its columns sum to 43.5 + 3.3 + 8.1 = 54.9; unrounded, 43.5 + 3.2 + 8.4 = 55.1. Weekly totals run from 5.4 to 6.9 hours; optional items are not counted. Running times are those of [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md), §5, which states one: *In Our Time*, "Hegel's Philosophy of History," 52 minutes. The four *Philosophize This!* pages (eps. 66, 67, 74, 76) state none; the path puts the show's episodes at about 30–35 minutes (§3), so each is priced at 35, an upper estimate: 4 × 35 + 52 = 192 minutes. Sadler's lectures state no lengths either; as in the path, the assigned ones sit **inside** desk hours, capped at about an hour a week.

**Card time is an estimate tied to deck size.** The path's daily queue is about 10 minutes, about six days a week, for the whole cumulative deck (§2). This plan counts 8 minutes a day as the share this dossier's cards add, the house rate the [Hobbes plan](../thomas-hobbes/07-study-plan.md) uses for a deck of similar size, here **41 cards** (tag `focus::hegel`): 16 argument cards, one per argument in 04, and 25 concept cards keyed to the key terms of [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md). **The accounting against the path, stated plainly: these hours sit on top of Stage 4's budget (11 weeks, 48 desk hours, ≈80 all-in).** The path fixes no week count for row 2. At the stage's rate of about 4.4 desk hours a week, this dossier estimates the row at about 1.5 weeks and 7 desk hours, Sadler's 3–4 hours included; that is its own estimate, not the path's. The four *Philosophize This!* episodes presumably fall inside the path's 19th-century arc of *Philosophize This!* (≈30 episodes, ≈+15 hours, already in Stage 4's media), so they are not new time (an inference from the path's description; it names no episodes); *In Our Time* is new. The path (§1.4) allows a stage at most two extra weeks; nine weeks for a 1.5-week row is about seven and a half. Choose deliberately. (a) Run the whole plan in the comfortable version (Calendar B, path §10), which already names "a second, slower Hegel ... pass" among its additions: this dossier is that pass, taken in place of the row rather than after it. (b) Run the short route as the row (16 desk hours, ≈21 all-in, about 3.5 weeks): about two weeks' extension, everything the path sets, and Schopenhauer straight after. Take the rest (Week 1's life and Kant sessions, Weeks 2, 4, 5, 6, the art and religion half of Week 8, and Week 9) after Stage 4's gate as new Calendar B time, not buffer weeks, which path §10 keeps for illness, holidays, two named re-reading passes and media spill. Under (b), read Argument 10 in [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) before Week 7's Session A (about twenty minutes), since Argument 15 leans on it, and read 03's "The road of the *Phenomenology*" through "Self-consciousness" before Week 3, since Week 3 starts from Argument 5's result.

**Reading texts.** Follow [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md), §1: the *Phenomenology* in Pinkard (Cambridge, 2018) or Miller (Oxford, 1977; the 1979 paperback adds Findlay's paragraph-by-paragraph analysis), both with the paragraph numbers this plan uses; the *Encyclopaedia Logic* in Geraets, Suchting and Harris (Hackett, 1991); the *Philosophy of Right* in Nisbet, ed. **Cite by paragraph or section, and budget by paragraph.** (PhG ¶178) is a paragraph in the Miller and Pinkard numbering; (EL §88 Z) an Addition; (PhR §258 R) and (PhR §258 A) Hegel's Remark and Gans's Addition; (PH Introduction) Sibree's Introduction, not identical to Nisbet's ([02](02-works-and-texts.md), "A note on citation and editions"). The hours assume roughly fifteen to twenty *Phenomenology* paragraphs an hour on a first close reading, slower in the Preface and chapter III (this plan's estimate).

## Week 1 — Life; Kant and Fichte; the Preface: PhG ¶¶1–37 sel. and ¶¶73–89 (~6.6 hrs: 4.5 desk + 1.2 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** About two and a half hours, the slowest pages per hour in the plan. The Preface in four passes: ¶¶1–5 closely (why a preface misleads; bud, blossom and fruit, ¶2); ¶¶14–17 closely (the formalism of the Absolute in which all is one, and the thesis of ¶17); ¶¶18–27 twice, from substance as subject through "The truth is the whole" (PhG ¶20; Baillie p. 17), the false first principle (¶24) and spirit (¶25) to the "long and laborious journey" (¶27); ¶¶28–37 at a steady pace, stopping on the "power of the negative" (¶32). Skip ¶¶38–72 (Argument 2 points to ¶¶60–66 if the speculative proposition holds you). Then the Introduction, ¶¶73–89, about forty minutes: ¶¶73–76 closely (knowledge as instrument or medium, and the fear of error), ¶79 on determinate negation, ¶¶84–87 twice (each shape's own standard, and experience). It is the one block this plan adds to Week 1's reading in [02](02-works-and-texts.md), because Argument 1's fifth premise needs it.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) through "Jena, 1801–1807" at a steady pace, then "The *Phenomenology* and the battle, 1806" and "Weighing the sources" closely; [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), "The forms Hegel wrote in," the Jena essays and the *Phenomenology* entry; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "The shape of the problem" through "Determinate negation, and what dialectic is and is not," with tension (1); [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), the opening note and Arguments 1–2; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Spinoza," "Kant," "Fichte," "Jacobi," "Schelling" and "Hölderlin"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md), "Myths about the life." The library entries on [Hegel](../../eras/04-19th-century/g-w-f-hegel.md) (the map), [Fichte](../../eras/04-19th-century/johann-gottlieb-fichte.md) and [Schelling](../../eras/04-19th-century/friedrich-schelling.md). In Session B, Pinkard's biography on Jena and the SEP's "Hegel," §§1–2. Sadler: from Lectures 1–16, those on ¶¶17–20.

**Media.** *Preview:* *Philosophize This!*, [ep. 66, "Hegel Pt. 1 – Introduction"](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast/episode-066-hegel-pt-1-introduction) (priced at 35 minutes). *Review, at the week's end:* ep. 67, "What Hegel Was Saying!" (priced at 35 minutes; 08 found it only in a search result, so search the show's site by title). Note how West states the dialectic; Week 6 tests it.

**Checkpoints (book closed).**
1. *Why can philosophy not begin from a first principle?* A starting point stated immediately is merely universal, an empty word ("all animals" is not zoology, ¶20), so a first principle, even if true, is false insofar as it is only a first principle (¶24). The truth is the whole, and the whole is a result (¶20). Name the targets (Reinhold and Fichte), and say why the beginning is not arbitrary: it is the standpoint every reader already holds. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 1.
2. *Whose Absolute is "the night in which, as we say, all cows are black" (PhG ¶16; Baillie p. 15), and what came of the remark?* An Absolute of undifferentiated identity reached by intellectual intuition, the philosophy of identity Schelling taught; Hegel told Schelling it was aimed at his imitators, but Schelling took it as aimed at him and the friendship ended (SEP). Say why the text fits either reading. [01](01-life-and-times.md), "The *Phenomenology* and the battle, 1806"; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 1, "Where scholars disagree" (c).
3. *State "substance as subject" and the two ways to read it.* Spinoza's one substance submerged self-consciousness; the subjective view is as empty; living substance is subject, a negativity that splits itself and returns, and its name is spirit (¶¶17–25). Metaphysical: Taylor's cosmic subject, Beiser's organic monism, Houlgate's and Stern's structures of thought as structures of being. Post-Kantian: Pippin, Pinkard, Brandom. Give Feuerbach's inversion of subject and predicate. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 2; [06](06-reception-and-debates.md), live debate (1).
4. *Why does the Introduction refuse to test knowledge before using it, and what replaces the test?* If knowledge is an instrument it alters its object, if a medium it refracts it; either way the critique assumes the Absolute stands on one side and knowledge on the other, so "the fear of error" may be the first error (¶74). Instead each shape of consciousness is measured by its own standard; its failure is a determinate negation (¶79), and the new object is experience (¶¶84–87). Say what Kant would answer. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 1, premise 5; [03](03-system-and-concepts.md), "A ladder and a highway of despair"; [05](05-conversations.md), "Kant."
5. *What did Hegel need from Fichte, and what did he refuse?* The demand for a genuine deduction of the categories (EL §42) and the theme of recognition; not the I that needs a shock from outside, which never got beyond Kant's finite knowledge (EL §60 Z), and not the words "thesis, antithesis, synthesis," which are Fichte's, attached to Hegel by Chalybäus. [05](05-conversations.md), "Fichte"; [03](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Determinate negation."

**Reconstructions.** Argument 1 in full, Argument 2 in premise skeleton; both get their +2-day touches in Session C. Write the Introduction's method in five numbered steps, book closed: it returns as the frame of every argument from 3 to 10.

**Cards.** +6: Arguments 1–2; the plan of the system; Geist (spirit); the *Phenomenology* as a ladder and a highway of despair; determinate negation. Import after the week's reading; if you use the shipped decks in [`study/cards/`](../../study/cards/README.md), suspend any Hegel card these duplicate. Deck at 6.

## Week 2 — Consciousness: PhG ¶¶90–165 (~5.9 hrs: 5 desk + 0 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** About three hours, one chapter per sitting; this is the first of the plan's extensions past the path. Sense-certainty (¶¶90–110), about forty-five minutes: the claim (¶¶90–91), the three rounds, truth in the object (¶¶95–99), in the I (¶¶100–102) and in pointing (¶¶103–108), then ¶¶109–110 twice. Perception (¶¶111–131), about forty-five minutes: ¶¶111–113 closely, for the salt and for *aufheben*, "at once to negate and to preserve" (¶113); the three strategies (¶¶117–128) steadily; ¶131 on "sound common sense". Force and the understanding (¶¶132–165), about ninety minutes, the hardest pages before Week 6: the play of forces (¶¶135–141), the inner world as "appearance qua appearance" (¶147), the kingdom of laws (¶149), explanation that "explains nothing" (¶¶154–155), the inverted world (¶¶157–160) twice, infinity (¶¶161–162) and the curtain (¶165). If chapter III stalls, read Argument 5's premises first; it is the one place where the order may flip.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "The road of the *Phenomenology*," its first paragraph and "Consciousness," with the key terms "Aufhebung," "Vermittlung," "Erfahrung" and "Shape of consciousness"; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Arguments 3–5; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Heraclitus," "Plato," "Aristotle," "Russell and Moore's revolt" and "The analytic return: Sellars, McDowell, Brandom, Pippin"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md), "The analytic eclipse, 1900–1950." In Session B, the SEP's "Hegel" on the *Phenomenology*'s opening chapters, and Stern's *Guidebook* on chapters I–III. Sadler: from Lectures 39–65, the lectures on ¶¶95–99 and on the inverted world. *Optional, not counted:* Brandom's 2021 seminar, the meetings on chapters 1 and 2 (08, §5), for the inferentialist reading.

**Media.** None assigned beyond Sadler. *Philosophize This!* has no episode on these chapters.

**Checkpoints (book closed).**
1. *Reconstruct sense-certainty's failure.* It claims the richest knowledge of the bare this, but to know it must say it: "the Now is night-time" (¶95), written down, is stale at noon, so the Now that survives is a universal, indifferent to night and day; so are the Here and the I; and pointing is itself a process of before and after. So "this" is a universal and the bare particular is only "meant." Give Feuerbach's objection (Hegel refutes the word "this", not the thing) and the reply that it gives up the claim to know. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 3.
2. *Why does the thing of perception dissolve?* It is a "one" and an "also" (the salt is white, also pungent, also cubical); taking the unity as the thing's and the diversity as ours fails, the reverse fails, and the "in so far as" distinctions of the third strategy are a distinction "that only exists in words" (¶127). Give Russell's charge (the "is" of predication confused with the "is" of identity) and Stern's reply (Hegel asks what the unity of the many properties consists in). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 4.
3. *What is wrong with explaining lightning by electrical force, and what is the inverted world?* The force is given exactly the content of the law, so the explanation repeats itself; taken as a second world, the inner world turns into its opposite, sweet into sour; it is the first world's own inner difference, so behind the curtain there is nothing unless we go there ourselves (¶165). Give the objection from Newton's unifying laws and the reply that unification says nothing for a hidden realm of powers. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 5.
4. *Why does chapter III end in self-consciousness?* The understanding finds in the inner world only the structure of its own explaining; a distinguishing of what is undistinguished is self-consciousness (¶164). The lesson of all three chapters is that the object consciousness took as independent is shaped by the concepts it brings. [03](03-system-and-concepts.md), "The road of the *Phenomenology*," "Consciousness."
5. *How close is Hegel to Sellars's attack on the "myth of the given"?* The SEP makes the comparison for sense-certainty; McDowell develops it; Westphal and others say Hegel's point is a realist one that Sellarsian readings underplay; Taylor reads the chapter as a transcendental argument, Brandom as a theory of determinate content, Houlgate as ontology. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 3, "Where scholars disagree"; [05](05-conversations.md), "The analytic return."

**Reconstructions.** Argument 3 in full, Argument 4 in skeleton, Argument 5 in full; all three get their +2-day touches in Session C, and Arguments 1–2 their +1-week touches.

**Cards.** +4: Arguments 3–5; Aufhebung (sublation). Deck at 10.

## Week 3 — Self-consciousness: PhG ¶¶166–196 (~5.9 hrs: 5 desk + 0 media + 0.9 cards)

**Primary.** About three hours, the core of the path's row. ¶¶166–177 first, about an hour: the turn from consciousness (¶¶166–167), desire and life (¶¶168–173 at a steady pace), and ¶¶174–177 twice, ending at the "turning-point" of ¶177 and the first statement of spirit, "Ego that is 'we'". Then ¶¶178–196 three times, which is the path's own excerpt and the passage its exit checkpoint asks you to reconstruct: once straight through; once slowly, the struggle (¶¶178–188) in one sitting and lordship and bondage (¶¶189–196) in another; and once book half-closed, checking your reconstruction against the text. Stop each time on ¶178 (recognition), ¶182 (action from one side only would be useless), ¶187 (the life-and-death struggle), ¶188 (death cancels the truth the struggle sought), ¶¶191–193 (the reversal) and ¶¶194–196 (fear, service and work).

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Self-consciousness," with the key terms "Begierde," "Anerkennung" and "Herrschaft und Knechtschaft"; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Arguments 6–8; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Fichte" (recognition), "Smith, Steuart and the political economists," "Marx and the Young Hegelians" and "Kojève and the French reception: Hyppolite, Sartre"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md), "Kojève's Paris, 1933–1939," "Recognition theory: Honneth," "Hegel, race and colonialism" (on Buck-Morss), and live debate (4). The library entry's "Lordship and Bondage" and its study questions 1–2. In Session B, the SEP's ["Recognition"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/recognition/) on Hegel, and Stern's *Guidebook* on chapter IV. Sadler: Lectures 66–79 cover ¶¶166–196; this is the block the path itself assigns him to, so spend the week's hour here, on the lectures for ¶¶178–196. *Optional, not counted:* Brandom's Leipzig lectures on self-consciousness and recognition.

**Media.** None assigned beyond Sadler.

**Checkpoints (book closed).**
1. *Why is self-consciousness desire, and why does desire need another self?* Self-consciousness assures itself by negating its object, so it is desire (¶174); but each satisfaction needs another object and reproduces the desire (¶175); only an object that negates itself while remaining independent can satisfy it, so "Self-consciousness attains its satisfaction only in another self-consciousness" (PhG ¶175; Baillie p. 173). Answer the objection that a tool or a tame animal would do. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 6.
2. *Why is recognition mutual in its concept, yet first a fight to the death?* A self exists only in being recognized (¶178), and recognition from one side only is useless (¶182); but the two first meet as living things, each certain only of itself, and each must prove it is not tied to life (¶¶186–187). Death defeats the aim, since a corpse recognizes no one (¶188). Give Honneth's objection, that the *Phenomenology* narrows the Jena account of love, right and solidarity, and the reply that the struggle is the first, failed attempt. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 7; [06](06-reception-and-debates.md), "Recognition theory."
3. *Why does the lord's victory defeat itself?* He is recognized by a consciousness he does not recognize, so recognition is "one-sided and unequal" (¶191) and what he has achieved is a dependent consciousness (¶192). "The truth of the independent consciousness is accordingly the consciousness of the bondsman" (PhG ¶193; Baillie p. 184). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 8.
4. *What do fear and work each give the bondsman, and why are both needed?* Absolute fear melts every fixed attachment (¶194); fear of the lord is the beginning of wisdom but not yet awareness of being for oneself (¶195); work is desire held in check, and its product lasts, so the worker finds his own activity outside him; work without the fear yields only stubbornness (¶196). Give Marx's 1844 objection (Hegel knows only abstract mental labor) and the reply that the bondsman is not yet free. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 8.
5. *Is this Hegel's philosophy of history in miniature?* Kojève said so: humanity begins with the risk of life for prestige, and history ends in universal recognition. Gadamer, Harris and most recent readers answer that it is a shape of self-consciousness in chapter IV, and that its outcome is stoicism, not revolt. Say where Buck-Morss's Haiti thesis stands. (2) Marx, the closing section of the third Paris manuscript of 1844, on Hegel's dialectic and the *Phenomenology*, about thirty minutes, for labor as self-creation and the charge of abstract mental labor (Arguments 6 and 8); it is in Tucker's *Marx-Engels Reader*, the path's Stage 4 edition for Marx (unchecked). (3) Marx, the [Afterword to the second German edition of *Capital*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/p3.htm) (1873), its last paragraphs only, about ten minutes: the dialectic "standing on its head", to be turned right side up "if you would discover the rational kernel within the mystical shell" (Marx, *Capital* I, Afterword, trans. Moore and Aveling). (4) Kierkegaard, *Concluding Unscientific Postscript*, the pages in which Johannes Climacus argues that a logical system is possible but a system of existence is not, except for God (Hong & Hong, vol. I, pp. 118–125, as the SEP's "Kierkegaard" cites them), about thirty minutes (Arguments 1 and 11). Strauss's *Life of Jesus* and the party labels are read in 06, not in the text.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), "Death, November 1831," "The *Werke* of the Friends, 1832–1845" and "After Hegel: the school divides," with the "Chronology" as a self-test; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "How the pieces interlock" as the week's self-test, and all five tensions; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Feuerbach," "Marx and the Young Hegelians," "Kierkegaard," "The British Idealists," "Adorno and the Frankfurt School" and "The themes trace"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) in full as capstone raw material, above all "The school divides, 1831–1841," "Marx's inversion," "Taylor, and the post-Kantian turn" and "The metaphysical revival"; [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md) for what next. The entries on [Feuerbach and Stirner](../../eras/04-19th-century/sidebars-feuerbach-and-stirner.md), [Marx](../../eras/04-19th-century/karl-marx.md) and [Kierkegaard](../../eras/04-19th-century/soren-kierkegaard.md). In Session B, finish the second of your two books on the metaphysical debate.

**Media.** None assigned; the time goes to the essay.

**Checkpoints (book closed).**
1. *How did the school split, and over what?* Over religion first: Strauss read the Gospels as myth, kept their truth for the human species, and sorted the Hegelians by their answer to the Gospel question into right, center and left; the left turned critique from the Gospels to religion as such and then to the state. [06](06-reception-and-debates.md), "The school divides, 1831–1841."
2. *What is Feuerbach's inversion, and which argument does it target?* Real subjects are finite beings; thought is their predicate; Hegel makes thought the subject and real beings its predicates, so the truth is Hegel turned right way up. It targets substance as subject, and Feuerbach's 1839 critique had already attacked the staged beginning and sense-certainty. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Arguments 1–3, "Objection"; [05](05-conversations.md), "Feuerbach."
3. *What does Marx take from Hegel, and what does he invert?* (Stage 4's exit checkpoint, completed.) He takes labor as human self-creation, alienation, and a dialectic of contradictions that drive change; he inverts the subject, so the material process is the demiurge and the Idea its reflection, and he turns the rabble of PhR §§241–245 into the proletariat. Write it in about 300 words, with the master–slave reconstruction of Week 3. [06](06-reception-and-debates.md), "Marx's inversion"; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), Argument 14, "Objection."
4. *What is Kierkegaard's objection, and how fair is it?* A logical system can be given, a system of existence cannot, since no existing person stands at its end; the unhappy consciousness is not a stage but the individual's condition before God; *Fear and Trembling* asks whether a duty to God can suspend ethical life. Thulstrup against Stewart on how much Kierkegaard owed Hegel, and how often his target was Danish Hegelians such as Martensen. [05](05-conversations.md), "Kierkegaard."
5. *Trace the claim that the modern state is rational back through the system.* The state unites subjective freedom with a universal end (Argument 15) because freedom is the will that wills itself in institutions (03, "The *Philosophy of Right*"); that needs recognition (Arguments 7–8) and the failure of abstract freedom (Arguments 9–10); and the method that links them is determinate negation, with the whole as the result (Arguments 1, 11). [03](03-system-and-concepts.md), "How the pieces interlock."

**Reconstructions.** No new argument. Argument 16 gets its +1-week touch and Argument 13 its +1-week touch; Argument 11 its +3-week touch; Arguments 12, 14 and 15 may count the capstone as their +3-week touch if the essay reconstructs them, otherwise they wait for Stage 4's Kierkegaard and Marx rows.

**Cards.** +1: the metaphysical and post-Kantian readings as a live debate. The deck is complete at 41, permanent in the cumulative FSRS queue.

## Capstone essay (800–1,200 words)

Write in Week 9, after the checkpoints, book closed for the first draft. **The gate relationship, stated exactly:** Stage 4's gate essay asks "Is morality a discovery, an invention, or a symptom?" and names Mill, Marx and Nietzsche, not Hegel; its first exit checkpoint is Hegel's ("Reconstruct the master–slave dialectic in ~300 words, and say what Marx takes from Hegel and what he inverts"). So this essay is the dossier's own consolidation, not the gate and not a substitute for it. Prompt 1 is built to discharge the exit checkpoint at length, and prompt 3 to give the gate essay a fourth position to argue against. Each prompt draws on at least three weeks.

1. **From desire to the state: does recognition carry the weight Hegel puts on it?** Reconstruct Arguments 6–8, 9 and 15 as one argument. Test it with Honneth's objection, Kojève's reading and Marx's 1844 charge, and say what Marx takes and what he inverts.
2. **Is Hegel's holism a method or a metaphysics?** Reconstruct Arguments 1, 2 and 11. Set Beiser or Houlgate against Pippin or Brandom, test the answer on sense-certainty (Argument 3), and say what Kierkegaard's existential objection costs each side.
3. **Ethical life against Kant: discovery, invention or symptom?** Reconstruct Arguments 12 and 15, with Argument 10. Set the Kantian answer (Korsgaard, O'Neill, Wood) against Hegel's charge, and say whether ethical life makes morality something found in institutions, and with what risk of blessing whatever exists (Tugendhat against Neuhouser).
4. **Does the rabble refute the *Philosophy of Right*?** Reconstruct Arguments 13–15. Set Marx's proletariat against Wood's and Neuhouser's reply and Avineri's remark that Hegel leaves the problem open, and say whether the corporation is an answer.
5. **Freedom in history: theodicy, standard, or Eurocentric myth?** Reconstruct Argument 16 with Argument 10. Set Popper and Berlin against the retrospective reading, and weigh the Africa pages with Bernasconi and Buck-Morss.

## Mastery checklist — you are done when you can…

- □ Reconstruct the master–slave dialectic in about 300 words, from desire to the bondsman's work, and say what Marx takes from Hegel and what he inverts. *(Stage 4's exit checkpoint; the path's row: ¶¶178–196.)*
- □ Explain why "The truth is the whole" (PhG ¶20) and why substance must be grasped as subject, and state both sides of the metaphysical debate with names. *(The path's row: the Preface in selection.)*
- □ Reconstruct the failure of sense-certainty, perception and the understanding, and say why consciousness becomes self-consciousness.
- □ Explain stoicism, scepticism and the unhappy consciousness as attempts to live the bondsman's freedom in thought.
- □ Reconstruct absolute freedom and the Terror, with the historian's and Berlin's objections.
- □ State the three sides of logical thought and reconstruct being, nothing and becoming, and explain why "thesis, antithesis, synthesis" is not Hegel's method.
- □ State the empty-formalism charge against Kant, the Kantian reply, and why Hegel puts ethical life above morality.
- □ Explain property, civil society and the rabble, and why civil society is "not rich enough."
- □ Explain the double saying with the distinction between existence and actuality, and weigh the Prussian charge from Haym to Popper against the liberal rehabilitation.
