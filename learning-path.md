# Learning Path — A Staged Beginner Route (about one year)

### 7.1 Principles behind the order
1. **Chronological is conceptual.** Later arguments quote earlier ones. Hume is unintelligible without Locke/Descartes; Kant without Hume; Nietzsche without Schopenhauer and Christian morality as a target; Foucault without Nietzsche.
2. **Alternate primary and secondary.** For every primary text below, read the SEP/IEP entry *first* (30–60 min), then the primary excerpt, then re-read the entry's objections section. Primary-first-with-no-map is the main way beginners drown in Kant and Hegel.
3. **Foundational vs advanced is about presupposition, not importance.** Scotus is not "lesser" than Plato; he is later in the dependency chain.
4. **Spiral, don't march.** After Stage 3, do the thematic second pass (§6.2) before tackling the hardest systems.

### 7.2 The staged path (approx. one self-paced year at ~4–5 hrs/week)

**Stage 0 — Orientation (2 weeks). Foundational.**
- Simon Blackburn, *Think: A Compelling Introduction to Philosophy* (1999) — knowledge, mind, free will, God, in a conversational tone; or Thomas Nagel, *What Does It All Mean?* (1987, very short); or Bertrand Russell, *The Problems of Philosophy* (1912, free online).
- Optional narrative route: Jostein Gaarder, *Sophie's World* (1991, novel) — Presocratics to existentialism inside a mystery story; excellent motivation, not a substitute for arguments.
- Set up tools: bookmark SEP and IEP; listen to one *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps* episode (Presocratics) to test fit.

**Stage 1 — Ancient foundations (8–10 weeks). Foundational.**
Order: Presocratics (fragments + SEP "Presocratic Philosophy") → Plato, *Apology*, *Meno*, *Republic* excerpts (Cave, Books II, IV, VII) → Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* Books I–II, X (excerpts) → Epicurus, *Letter to Menoeceus* + Epictetus, *Enchiridion* + Marcus Aurelius, selections → Sextus via SEP "Skepticism."
*Why here:* every later concept (form, substance, virtue, skepticism) is coined in this stage.

**Stage 2 — Medieval bridge (4–5 weeks). Foundational-to-intermediate.**
Order: Augustine, *Confessions* Books X–XI (memory, time) → Boethius, *Consolation* excerpts → Anselm, *Proslogion* chs 2–4 + Gaunilo's reply → Avicenna/Averroes/Maimonides via Adamson (podcast series on Islamic/Jewish philosophy) + SEP entries → Aquinas, Five Ways (*Summa Theologiae* I, Q.2) → Ockham via SEP "William of Ockham."
*Why here:* without it, Descartes looks like he invented the problems of God, universals and causation from nothing — he inherited them.

**Stage 3 — Early Modern core (8–10 weeks). Foundational — the centre of gravity.**
Order: Descartes, *Meditations* I–III (with SEP "Descartes") → Locke, *Essay* excerpts + *Second Treatise* excerpts → Berkeley, *Three Dialogues* → Hume, *Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (whole — short and superb) → Spinoza and Leibniz via secondary + *Monadology* → Rousseau, *Social Contract* Book I → **Kant last in this stage:** *Prolegomena* + *Groundwork* (whole); first *Critique* only in guided excerpts.
*Milestone check:* the learner should be able to state, in their own words, the rationalism/empiricism dispute *and* why SEP warns it is oversimple.

**Stage 4 — 19th century (6–8 weeks). Intermediate.**
Order: Mill, *On Liberty* + *Utilitarianism* (whole, both short) → Marx, *1844 Manuscripts* excerpts + *Manifesto* → Kierkegaard via SEP + *Fear and Trembling* guided → Schopenhauer excerpts → Nietzsche, *On the Genealogy of Morals* (whole) then *The Gay Science* selections → Hegel **by guided excerpts only** (Phenomenology Preface; Master–Slave, §§178–196) with a good secondary (see 7.3) → James, *Pragmatism* lectures I–II, VI.
*Why Hegel is excerpted, not assigned whole:* difficulty here is presupposition (Kant + German Idealism + Greek logic), not length.

**Stage 5 — 20th century, two tracks in parallel (8–10 weeks). Intermediate-to-advanced.**
- *Analytic track:* Russell, *Problems* (revisit) + "On Denoting" (guided) → Wittgenstein, *Investigations* §§1–65 + SEP "Wittgenstein" for the *Tractatus* → Popper, falsifiability chapters → Quine, "Two Dogmas" (guided).
- *Continental track:* Husserl via SEP + short excerpts → Heidegger via a reputable secondary first → Sartre, *Existentialism Is a Humanism* + Beauvoir, *Second Sex* Introduction and Vol. II opening chapter → Arendt, *Human Condition* excerpts.
- *Convergence:* Rawls, *A Theory of Justice* §§1–9 (or *Justice as Fairness: A Restatement*) → Foucault, *Discipline and Punish*, Part III → Habermas vs Foucault debate via SEP "Critical Theory" → Singer, "Famine, Affluence, and Morality" + *Animal Liberation* ch. 1.
*Capstone options:* re-read Plato's *Republic* whole; or write a 2,000-word dialogue between two figures from different eras on one theme from §6.2.

**Advanced / do-not-start-here list (label clearly in the repo):** Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason* (full); Hegel, *Science of Logic*; Spinoza, *Ethics* Part I unaided; Heidegger, *Being and Time* unaided; Scotus and Aquinas's *Summa* beyond selected questions; Derrida's 1967 books unaided; *Capital* Vol. I beyond Ch. 1 with a reading group/companion. "Advanced" = needs the stages above plus a companion text, not intelligence.

### 7.3 Survey books and secondary sources (verified)

| Resource | Type / cost | Best for | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP)** — https://plato.stanford.edu/Entries/medieval-philosophy/ (portal pattern; individual entries cited in Sources) | Free, peer-reviewed, expert-written, continuously updated | The default reference for *every* figure/concept in this report; bibliographies are gold | Articles are long and pitched at advanced undergraduates+; read the opening sections and history first |
| **Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (IEP)** — via university guides: "founded in 1995… to provide open access to detailed, scholarly information on key topics and philosophers in all areas of philosophy" | Free, peer-reviewed | Often more accessible than SEP for a first pass; excellent on ancient and medieval figures | Coverage is patchier; cross-check dates against SEP |
| **Peter Adamson, *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*** (podcast, free; books: *Classical Philosophy* 2014, *Philosophy in the Hellenistic and Roman Worlds* 2015, *Philosophy in the Islamic World*, *Byzantine and Renaissance Philosophy*, *Classical Indian Philosophy* with Jonardon Ganeri) | Free podcast / paid books | The best chronological companion: short, lively chapters, deliberately includes women, Islamic/Jewish/Indian philosophy and "minor" figures other surveys skip | Pace is slow by design (~20 min episodes, hundreds of them) — use as a companion, not a sprint |
| **Anthony Kenny, *A New History of Western Philosophy*** (one-volume ed. 2010; originally 4 vols 2004–07) | Paid survey | Recommended single-volume spine: clear, balanced, thematic chapters woven into the chronology | Critics note limited coverage of the Islamic world beyond its role in the Latin tradition (noted in survey comparisons) |
| **Frederick Copleston, *A History of Philosophy*** (9 vols 1946–75; now marketed as 11 vols) | Paid reference | Depth and fairness: Britannica-noted as having become "a standard introductory philosophy text for thousands of university students"; *Washington Post* (1993): "still the place to start," without Russell's biases or Durant's dumbing-down | Length; written originally for Catholic seminaries (its fairness is nonetheless its reputation); use per-era, not cover-to-cover |
| **Bertrand Russell, *A History of Western Philosophy*** (1945) | Paid / library | Superb prose, wit, a philosopher's own judgments; cited in his 1950 Nobel Prize in Literature award | **Flag in repo:** criticised for overgeneralisation and omission, especially after Descartes; weak/hostile on medieval scholasticism and on figures he disliked (Nietzsche, Hegel). Read as an *argument*, not a neutral reference |
| **Bryan Magee, *The Story of Philosophy*** / *Confessions of a Philosopher*; **DK, *The Philosophy Book*** | Paid, illustrated | Visual timelines and one-concept-per-spread revision; good for the repo's own design inspiration | Compression creates exactly the oversimplifications §8 warns about — pair with SEP |
| **Sophie Musa / standard companions per figure:** e.g. Roger Scruton, *Kant: A Very Short Introduction*; A. C. Grayling, *Wittgenstein: A Very Short Introduction*; Oxford *Very Short Introductions* series generally | Paid, short | One slim, reliable volume per hard figure (Kant, Hegel, Heidegger, Wittgenstein) before primary texts | Quality varies by author; check author credentials |
| **1000-Word Philosophy, Wireless Philosophy (Wi-Phi), CrashCourse Philosophy, Philosophy Bites, In Our Time (BBC)** | Free | Short essays / animated videos / interviews for a first orientation or a commute-length review | Orientation only; always escalate to SEP/IEP for claims that will go in repo entries |
| **Open Yale Courses / MIT OpenCourseWare / Notre Dame & Saylor open courses** | Free | Full lecture courses with syllabi and reading lists — model the repo's learning path on real syllabi (ancient & medieval courses standardly sequence Plato's *Five Dialogues*, *Symposium*, Aristotle's *Ethics*, Augustine, Boethius, Aquinas) | Lecture courses assume term-time pacing; adapt, don't copy wholesale |
| **Project Gutenberg Philosophy Bookshelf; Early Modern Texts (Jonathan Bennett); Perseus / Loeb excerpts for Greek & Latin** | Free primary texts | Public-domain translations of Plato, Hume, Kant, Nietzsche, Mill etc. for the repo's excerpt files | **Translation matters:** note translator and date in every excerpt file; Bennett's Early Modern Texts are deliberately *modernised paraphrases* — label them as such, never as verbatim originals |

### 7.4 How university syllabi corroborate this order
Introductory history-of-philosophy syllabi recovered in this research converge on the same skeleton used above: ancient units move Thales/Democritus → Heraclitus/Parmenides → Sophists/Socrates → Plato (Forms, knowledge vs opinion) → Aristotle; modern units move through Descartes-to-Kant and end the 19th century with Hegel (absolute idealism, dialectic); ancient-and-medieval personhood courses standardly assign Plato's *Five Dialogues* and *Symposium*, Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics*, Augustine's *City of God*, Boethius's *Consolation* and Aquinas's *Shorter Summa* in that order. The staged path in §7.2 is therefore not an idiosyncratic invention — it is the consensus teaching order, slowed down for self-study.
