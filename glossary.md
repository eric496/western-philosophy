# Glossary — Starter Set

Plain-language definitions for the terms you will meet first. Each philosopher entry also explains its key terms in context.

- **a priori / a posteriori:** knowable independently of experience (e.g. "all bachelors are unmarried") / only through experience ("it rained yesterday"). Kant adds the crucial third category: *synthetic a priori*.
- **Analytic / synthetic (judgments):** true by the meanings of the terms alone / true by how the world is. Quine famously attacks the distinction's sharpness.
- **Archē:** Greek for origin/principle — the Presocratics' sought first principle of everything.
- **Ataraxia:** untroubledness of mind; the shared *goal* of Epicureans and Pyrrhonian skeptics, reached by opposite routes.
- **Aufhebung (sublation):** Hegel's term for a stage's being at once cancelled, preserved and raised into a new stage.
- **Categorical imperative:** Kant's unconditional moral law (vs a *hypothetical* imperative: "if you want X, do Y").
- **Cogito:** Descartes' indubitable "I think / I am" found by methodical doubt.
- **Dasein:** Heidegger's term for the human being as the being for whom Being is an issue; being-there.
- **Dialectic:** (variously) Socratic question-and-answer; Hegel's development through internal contradiction; Marx's materialist version. **Always ask which sense an author means.**
- **Elenchus:** Socrates' refutative cross-examination of a definition.
- **Empiricism / Rationalism:** see §3 headnote; a spectrum about the sources of knowledge, not two party memberships.
- **Epochē:** suspension of judgment (Pyrrhonian skeptics; Husserl reuses the word for phenomenological bracketing — different purpose, note the difference).
- **Eudaimonia:** flourishing / living well over a whole life; mistranslated if rendered simply "happiness" as a feeling.
- **Forms (Platonic):** eternal, perfect patterns of which sensible things are copies and by which they are intelligible.
- **Genealogy:** Nietzsche/Foucault's method: explain a value or category by the contingent history and interests that produced it.
- **General will:** Rousseau's will of the citizen body aimed at the common good — distinct from the mere sum of private wills ("will of all").
- **Haecceity:** Scotus's "thisness," what makes an individual this individual.
- **Idealism:** (many senses — disambiguate!) Berkeley: only minds and ideas exist. Kant: we know things only as structured by our cognition (transcendental idealism ≠ Berkeley). Hegel: reality is the self-development of Spirit/Reason.
- **Logos:** Greek word spanning speech, reason, account, cosmic order — Heraclitus and the Stoics exploit all its senses.
- **Monad:** Leibniz's simple, perceiving, windowless substance.
- **Necessary Existent:** Avicenna/Aquinas's God as the being whose existence is not caused by another.
- **Nominalism / Realism (universals):** universals are only names/concepts / universals are real. *Not* the modern political senses of those words.
- **Noumenon / Phenomenon:** Kant: thing as it is in itself (unknowable) / thing as it appears under our cognitive forms.
- **Ontological argument:** Anselm's a priori argument from the concept of God to God's existence.
- **Phenomenology:** Husserl's descriptive method for conscious experience; later, a whole continental movement.
- **Tabula rasa:** Locke's mind as blank paper at birth, written on by experience.
- **Telos:** end/purpose; Aristotle explains things partly by their telos (final cause).
- **Transcendental:** Kantian: concerned with the *conditions of possibility* of experience/knowledge, not with a world "beyond" (avoid the colloquial "transcendent" confusion).
- **Utilitarianism:** right action maximises overall well-being/happiness (Bentham, Mill, Singer, in different versions).
- **Veil of ignorance:** Rawls's device for choosing principles of justice without knowing one's own social position.
- **Virtue (aretē):** excellence of character/function, not merely rule-obedience; central to Plato, Aristotle, Stoics, Aquinas.
